The 60-second version

  1. The thesis is largely right: agents, led by coding agents, are where the new money is arriving. Anthropic's run-rate went from $9bn to $65bn in seven months E1, OpenAI's re-accelerated to $40bn on Codex, business sales and ads E8, and Cursor quadrupled to $4bn ARR in seven months E19.
  2. But the money is not landing in an independent "app layer". The fastest-growing agent products belong to the model labs (Claude Code, Codex, Cowork), to incumbents (Microsoft, Salesforce, ServiceNow), or were bought by labs: SpaceX/xAI paid $60bn for Cursor R23. Apps and models are merging.
  3. The biggest and most profitable AI money is still advertising. Meta's AI-driven Advantage+ suite runs at $75bn a year R40, and we attribute ~$50bn of 2026 ad growth at Meta and Google to AI A Low confidence. That money funds capex directly, with no lab in between.
  4. Direct end-customer spending on generative AI is roughly tripling every year: ~$11bn (2024) to ~$37bn (2025) to ~$130bn (2026E), with a ~$200bn direct exit run-rate A. No software category has grown this fast.
  5. It still does not pay for the build-out. About $1.0tn of Western AI capex in 2026 runs ~5x ahead of all end-customer AI money. On a depreciation basis the build-out needs ~$600bn a year of revenue; ~$280bn is arriving A. Closing the gap needs revenue to roughly double in 2027 and grow ~1.5x in 2028.
  6. Watch the Anthropic and OpenAI IPO filings (the first audited agent economics), Microsoft's Copilot seat count, and Ramp's monthly AI index, which in Aug–Sep 2026 showed the first "cracks": flat adoption and a 41% fall in the effective price per token E35.

01 · What this layer isThe storefront of the AI economy

This is the layer where someone outside the industry finally pays. Everything below it, from power plants to chips to clouds to model labs, is a supplier.

The products are familiar: a ChatGPT subscription, a Microsoft 365 Copilot seat, a Cursor license, an API bill for tokens, a per-ticket fee to a customer-service agent, or an Instagram ad whose targeting was chosen by a recommendation model. Unless money eventually arrives here, the build-out is financed only by investors' hopes and by hyperscalers' existing profits (see W02).

One distinction matters for every number on this page. Run-rate (or ARR) is the latest month or quarter multiplied up to a year; it is not calendar-year revenue. In a market growing 3–7x a year, the two differ a lot. Anthropic's $65bn run-rate at the end of July E sits alongside roughly $60bn of expected calendar-2026 revenue A.

Analogy

Think of the electricity grid around 1900. Power plants and transmission lines (chips, data centers, clouds) were built ahead of demand. What households and factories actually paid for were appliances. The appliance makers were a mix of the power companies themselves (GE made generators and light bulbs), incumbents who electrified their existing products, and new firms. Chatbots are the light bulb: the first, obvious appliance. Agents are the electric motor: the appliance that reorganizes the factory, and so the one that eventually justifies the grid.

Explain like I'm new: what is an "agent", and why does it change the money?

A chatbot answers a question. An agent is given a goal ("fix this bug", "resolve this customer's refund") and works on it by itself: it plans, uses software tools, checks its own work and tries again, sometimes for hours.

That changes the money in two ways. First, an agent uses far more computing per task (4–15x the tokens of a chat R55), so a customer's bill moves from $20 a month toward hundreds or thousands. Second, because an agent does work a person used to do, it can be sold against a company's labor budget, which is many times larger than its software budget.

Five doors for end-customer money

Money enters the AI economy through five doors. The first four are business models; the fifth, AI-native startups, cuts across seats and usage.

02 · Where fresh money entersWhere fresh money enters, and how much of it is really fresh

About $176bn of end-customer money reaches AI products in 2026 through W10's doors: ~$126bn paid directly for AI products, plus ~$50bn of advertising that AI made more effective A. W11 adds ~$20bn of enterprise AI cloud spending for a canonical total of ~$200bn.

The breakdown below follows each payer to the first vendor it pays. Enterprises are the biggest payer, and most of their money goes straight to the model labs, not to independent apps.

Is it fresh money?

Not every dollar that reaches a lab is new money entering the industry. Some lab API revenue is paid by venture-funded app startups that lose money on tokens: Cursor was only "slightly gross-margin positive" in April 2026 E20, so part of what it paid Anthropic and OpenAI was its investors' capital passing through. Truly fresh money comes from three places: household wallets, enterprise operating budgets (increasingly labor budgets rather than IT budgets) and advertisers.

Zoom out and the picture changes. W11 traces all ~$1.1tn that flows into the Western build-out in 2026. End-customer AI money is less than a fifth of it; Big Tech's existing profits from search, social, retail and Office pay for about half.

Key insight

Agents are where the growth in fresh money is: agentic usage is ~45% of 2026 direct spend (~$60bn) but ~55–60% of its growth A (W11 §12). They are not yet where most of the money that pays for the build-out comes from. Right about the growth engine, early on the fuel tank.

The anatomy of an agent

Four ingredients make something an agent: tool use (the model emits structured calls such as "run this command" and reads the result), planning (it breaks a goal into steps and revises the plan), autonomy (it loops without a human approving each step) and long-horizon coherence (it keeps state across dozens to thousands of steps). The diagram shows how the pieces connect: a model at the center, memory feeding it, tools acting on the world, and a loop that repeats until the goal is met.

The fourth ingredient is what arrived in 2025–26. METR measures it as the "50% time horizon": the length of human-expert task a model completes half the time. In March 2025 it was about 1 hour (Claude 3.7 Sonnet), and it had been doubling roughly every 7 months for six years R51. That exponential is the technical reason agent revenue arrived when it did: tasks crossed the point where delegating beats doing. (W09 tracks the latest horizons; see W09.)

Why agents burn 10–100x more tokens, and why that bends the revenue curve

  • Context re-reading. An agent re-sends its growing context (code files, tool outputs, earlier steps) on every loop. OpenRouter's 100-trillion-token study found average prompt length rose ~4x in 2025 (~1.5K to 6K+ tokens); programming prompts regularly exceed 20K tokens, and programming went from ~11% to >50% of all tokens E56.
  • Reasoning tokens. "Thinking" models write hidden chains of thought. About half of OpenRouter tokens now run through reasoning-optimized models E56.
  • Duration. Claude Code users averaged ~20 hours a week of use in early 2026 R4, and coding agents run in parallel across many copies of a codebase.
  • The result. Revenue per user moves from a flat $20 a month to hundreds or thousands. Ramp's top 1% of firms spend ~$7,200 per employee per month on AI E35. One anecdote has a single enterprise running up $500M of Claude usage in a month after neglecting caps E2 Single source.

The counterforce: deflation

The cost of a given capability falls about 10x a year (a16z's "LLMflation") E60, anywhere from 9x to 900x a year depending on the task (Epoch AI) E61. Ramp's effective blended price fell 41% in five months (Mar to Aug 2026) E35. Revenue grows only if volume outruns price: the Jevons question.

04 · Market size & growthHow big it is, and how fast it is growing

The revenue curves of 2025–26 have no precedent in software. On a log scale, where a straight line means constant percentage growth, Anthropic, Claude Code and Cursor all climb more than tenfold in about a year.

Anthropic: the fastest revenue ramp on record

Anthropic's run-rate went $1bn (Dec 2024) → $9bn (Dec 2025) → $30bn (6 Apr 2026) → $47bn (May) → $65bn (end-July) R42 E1. The $65bn is a press-reported investor-letter figure, not company-published; a mid-September report of more than $100bn is unverified Low confidence. Quarterly revenue went $0.79bn (Q2 2025) → $4.73bn (Q1 2026) → $11.5bn+ (Q2 2026, preliminary), and Q2 2026 reportedly brought a first positive adjusted operating income of about $0.56bn E67. About 70–75% of revenue is API and 10–15% subscriptions E5. More than 1,000 customers spend over $1M a year, a number that doubled in under two months (Apr 2026) R4; eight of the Fortune 10 are customers E5. By May 2026 commentators were projecting that Anthropic would out-earn every public software company except Microsoft by year-end E86.

OpenAI: consumer scale, re-accelerating on agents and ads

OpenAI's revenue was $3.7bn in 2024 and $13.07bn in 2025, with an operating loss of $20.9bn (leaked audited figures) E14. Its run-rate sat on a ~$25bn "plateau" from February to June 2026 E14, then jumped to $40bn with July revenue up 20% month on month, credited to Codex, business sales and ads E89. Q1 2026 revenue was $5.7bn at an adjusted operating margin of −122% E10; Q2 was ~$6.7bn E15.

Users went from 900M weekly actives (Feb 2026) R11 to more than 1bn (Aug 2026) R12; paid subscribers from 47M (end-2025) to 50M (Feb) to 55M (Q1) E1011. On the business side it had 1M+ business customers and 7M ChatGPT-for-Work seats in Nov 2025 R16, and 9M+ paying business users by Feb 2026 E15. Management targets enterprise at 50% of revenue by year-end E9.

The crossover

Anthropic's Q2 2026 revenue (~$11.5bn) was roughly 1.7x OpenAI's (~$6.7bn), and its July run-rate ($65bn) ~1.6x OpenAI's ($40bn) E. Two years earlier OpenAI was about eight times larger. The enterprise, API and agent customer, not the consumer, drove the reversal. Caveat: Anthropic reportedly books some cloud-marketplace sales (Bedrock, Vertex) gross while OpenAI's Microsoft revenue share is netted, and neither has published audited statements. The IPO filings should settle it.

How we estimate the AI-driven ad uplift

Meta's revenue went $134.9bn (2023) → $164.5bn (2024) → ~$201bn (2025) → ~$245bn (2026E; Q2 $60.8bn R, Q3 guidance $61–64bn G39). Growth over 2023 is ~$30bn, ~$66bn and ~$110bn. Meta credits its generative recommender and GEM ranking models with +15.7% conversions and +8.3% clicks R4041. Attributing ~40% of the growth to AI gives ~$12bn, ~$26bn and ~$44bn. We add ~$0–10bn for Google, where Search grew 17% in Q2 2026 alongside AI Mode R42; the attribution there is very uncertain. This is why the ad figure is kept separate and marked low confidence.

Verdict on "growing very fast"

Confirmed. Direct end-customer AI spend has grown about 3–3.5x a year for two years. For comparison, global SaaS took roughly 15 years to reach ~$300bn.

Coding agents: the killer app

Coding went first for five reasons. (1) The output is verifiable: tests pass or fail, so an agent can loop until green. (2) Developers are early adopters with self-serve budgets (product-led growth). (3) Code is text, the thing models are best at. (4) Engineers are expensive: a $150–250K engineer makes a $200–2,000-a-month tool an easy sell. (5) Labs train hardest on coding because it also speeds up their own research.

Menlo counted $4.0bn of enterprise coding spend in 2025, 55% of departmental AI E32. By mid-2026 the named coding products alone carry an estimated $19–27bn of run-rate A, roughly five times Menlo's full-year 2025 figure in about six months. 46% of new code pushed to GitHub is AI-generated E28.

The gap: end-customer AI money versus capex

This is the question the whole atlas turns on. Western AI data-center investment is about $1.0tn in 2026 A, including Big-4 guidance of ~$733bn G45, up from ~$413bn of Big-4 capex in 2025 46. All end-customer AI money, counting direct spend (~$130bn), enterprise AI cloud (~$20bn) and ad uplift (~$50bn), is about $200bn. Capex runs about 5x ahead of revenue, down from ~12x in 2024 A.

Direct revenue grows ~3.4x a year while capex grows ~1.8x, so the ratio is collapsing. Capex cannot keep growing 80% a year: it is constrained by power (W03), memory (W06) and balance sheets (W02). The open question is whether revenue can keep compounding at 2–3x once coding saturates.

The Sequoia "$600B question", updated to 2026

In June 2024 Sequoia's David Cahn asked how much end-user revenue the GPU spending implied: take NVIDIA's data-center run-rate, double it for the rest of the data center, and double again for the end user's 50% margin. That gave $600bn, against ~$100bn identified, a $500bn hole E48. Rerun with NVIDIA's ~$400bn run-rate (Q2 FY27 data-center revenue $89.0bn R; Q3 guidance $108bn total G47), the answer is ~$1.6tn A.

Our critique of the Sequoia math

It treats one year of GPU purchases as needing one year of revenue. GPUs last 5–6 years in hyperscaler books and data-center shells 15–20+, so the depreciation test above is the fairer one. Even so, both tests say the same thing: revenue must keep roughly doubling for two more years.

Bain's $2tn test. Bain estimates that $500bn a year of data-center capex requires $2tn of annual AI revenue by 2030, leaving an $800bn shortfall even after reinvesting AI-driven savings E49. From a ~$200bn direct exit-2026 run-rate, reaching $2tn by end-2030 needs a ~78% compound growth rate for four years A ((2000/200)1/4 = 1.78). That is slower than today's growth but unprecedented at that scale. Counting AI-driven ad revenue and labor-budget capture is how bulls get there; bears note that Bain's figure assumed ~$500bn of annual capex, which 2026 already exceeds.

11 · BottlenecksWhat limits the growth

  1. Compute availability. Rate limits and capacity shortages capped agent usage throughout 2025–26. Anthropic signed compute deals of up to 5 GW with Amazon, multiple gigawatts with Google/Broadcom, SpaceX's Colossus 1 (300 MW+, 220K+ GPUs), a ~$45bn Nscale deal (460 MW) and a 2 GW AMD MI450 partnership R3483. App growth is literally gated by layers W03–W08.
  2. Model dependence and platform risk. Most AI-native apps depend on two or three labs for 60–100% of their cost of goods. Labs can re-price (priority tiers), rate-limit or cut access: Anthropic cut Windsurf's direct access in Jun 2025 (older) and revoked OpenAI's access in Aug 2025 E84.
  3. Reliability and trust. Agents fail silently. The METR slowdown result 50, prompt-injection risk in computer-use agents and hallucination in regulated fields (legal, health) keep humans in the loop and slow outcome pricing.
  4. Security and misuse. Anthropic disclosed a threat actor (GTG-2002) that automated 80–90% of an espionage campaign with Claude Code E84. Security reviews lengthen enterprise sales cycles (Glean: 4–5 months 70).
  5. Integration and data access. Value needs access to systems of record (CRM, ERP, electronic health records). MCP (10,000+ servers) and A2A cut the plumbing cost 5859, but incumbents control the data and can charge for API access.
  6. Organizational change. MIT NANDA's "learning gap" 52 and Ramp's plateau at ~56% of firms 36 suggest diffusion is limited by management, not by models.
  7. Politics and regulation. The Pentagon labeled Anthropic a "supply-chain risk" and federal agencies were told to stop using Claude (Feb–Mar 2026); a federal judge enjoined the action on 26 Mar 2026 E83. China blocked Meta–Manus (Apr 2026) 80. The EU AI Act phases in over 2025–27 (older).
  8. Price deflation versus volume. Revenue depends on token volume outgrowing annual price declines of ~40–90%. Ramp's Aug–Sep 2026 data is the first sign this may not hold at every firm 35.

The "wrapper" risk, reframed

Thin wrappers (a UI on someone else's model) have been competed away. Image-generation apps fell as ChatGPT and Gemini added native image models; Midjourney dropped from the top 10 to #46 in a16z's consumer ranking E37. The survivors own at least one of: a proprietary data loop, a workflow system of record, distribution (Microsoft, Salesforce), a trusted position in a regulated industry, or a revenue model that is not tokens (ads). Cursor's journey shows that even the category leader needed a model and a compute parent.

Incumbent SaaS and the 2026 "SaaSpocalypse"

The seat-compression thesis says that if agents do the work, customers need fewer seats of Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, Atlassian, HubSpot and Adobe; Bain estimates AI could automate 30–50% of activity in key enterprise functions E81. Markets acted on it in early 2026. Triggers: Claude Cowork (12 Jan), Cowork plugins (30 Jan), a ~$285bn software market-cap drawdown on 3 Feb, a Citrini Research disruption scenario around the 23 Feb trough, and more agent launches on 24 Mar E81. Private software M&A multiples had already compressed from 6.7x EV/revenue (2021–22 peak) to 3.1x (H2 2025) 81.

The counter-evidence. Incumbents are booking AI revenue: Agentforce $1.5bn, ServiceNow $1bn, M365 Copilot ~$9–10bn implied. ServiceNow subscription revenue still grew 24.5% in Q2 2026 R31, and even after Salesforce was down 35% year to date in early July 82, it rose 22.6% on 27 Aug 2026 after raising guidance on Agentforce E30: "AI losers" can re-rate once agent revenue shows up. The market is separating incumbents with proprietary data and agent platforms from seat-based point tools; Adobe's CEO transition (announced 3 Sep 2026) is one visible symptom 85.

Our read

Seat compression is real at the margin (support, junior development, marketing operations), but systems of record remain the data layer agents need. The likely outcome is re-pricing from seats to consumption rather than extinction. That lowers growth visibility and multiples even if the revenue survives.

Disruptors to watch

  • Open-weight and Chinese models as low-cost substitutes: Kimi inside Cursor's Composer 2 21; ~13% Chinese share of OpenRouter tokens 56; only ~6% of US AI-paying firms use model-serving platforms so far 35.
  • Ad-funded agents (OpenEvidence, ChatGPT ads) that undercut subscription pricing.
  • Agentic commerce: Stripe's Agentic Commerce Suite (Etsy, Coach, Urban Outfitters) R64 and ChatGPT's planned transaction take rates 37 could open a sixth door for money: commerce take rates.

13 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

The fastest revenue ramp in history, now reaching labor budgets

  • It is real and cash-paid. Anthropic went from $1bn to $65bn in ~19 months 14 (reportedly >$100bn by mid-September, unverified), was around break-even in Q2 2026 7, and has 1,000+ customers paying over $1M a year.
  • Agents unlock labor budgets. Outcome pricing (Fin, Sierra) sells labor, not software. Support, junior coding, paralegal, medical scribing and back-office work are a multi-trillion-dollar wage pool.
  • Token volume beats price deflation. Agents use 4–15x the tokens 55; Google's API throughput rose from 16bn to 22bn tokens a minute in one quarter 44. Every 10x cost decline opens new uses.
  • Distribution is already there. ChatGPT 1bn+ weekly users, Gemini 950M, AI Mode 1bn+ monthly 1243; M365 Copilot net adds more than doubled. Monetization per user (ads, commerce, agents) is early.
  • The capex ratio halves every year (~12x → ~8x → ~5x on all end-customer money), so revenue can cover depreciation-based requirements around 2028.
  • Incumbent AI revenue is inflecting (Agentforce +240%, ServiceNow AI ACV $1bn): disruption is partly redistribution within software, not destruction of spend.

Bear case

Concentrated, fragile, low-margin, and still far short

  • Concentration. Two labs and a handful of coding tools carry most of the growth. Coding may saturate: there are only ~30M professional developers, and 46% of new code is already AI-written 28.
  • Fragile run-rate revenue. "Last month × 12" in a market with monthly swings, usage caps and a $500M one-month overspend 2 exaggerates durable revenue. OpenAI's run-rate plateaued at ~$25bn for Feb–Jun 2026 14.
  • Cracks in enterprise demand. Ramp: adoption +0.4 pt m/m; top-1% spend per employee −9.7%; resistance to frontier prices; token prices −41% in five months 3536.
  • Margins. OpenAI runs a −122% adjusted operating margin 10; fast AI apps have ~25% gross margins 62; some "demand" is VC money recycled through startups.
  • The gap is still enormous. ~$1.0tn of 2026 capex against ~$130bn of direct AI revenue; the Sequoia-style requirement is ~$1.6tn; Bain's $2tn by 2030 needs ~78% a year for four more years.
  • Deflation, open weights, circularity and politics. Open models can collapse price points (Cursor moved toward a Kimi base 21); vendor equity in labs inflates demand (W02); the Pentagon–Anthropic dispute and China's veto of Meta–Manus show political risk.

Analyst verdict

The thesis, "the application layer depends on agents; that is where fresh money is entering, and it is growing very fast", is correct on direction and speed. Agents, led by coding, are where new end-customer money is arriving, and it is compounding at ~3x a year. It needs four refinements: (i) the money accrues mostly to labs and incumbents, not an independent app layer; (ii) advertising is still the largest profitable AI monetization; (iii) a slice of "end" revenue is recycled investor capital; and (iv) the gap to capex has narrowed a lot but still needs ~2x annual growth through 2027–28 to close. Whether that happens depends on agents moving beyond coding into labor-budget categories (support, legal, health, back office). By Sep 2026 that is visibly starting, but it is still ~10x smaller than coding.

16 · China anglePointers for Track 2

  • A different money model. Chinese consumer AI (Doubao, DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen, Yuanbao) is mostly free; monetization runs through super-app ecosystems (ByteDance, Tencent, Alibaba) and ads and commerce, not $20 subscriptions.   • Founder-origin risk. Manus moved to Singapore, yet China's NDRC still blocked its sale to Meta, and the US Treasury reviewed Benchmark's investment under the outbound-investment program 80. Expect "China-origin" to become a diligence category for Western agent acquisitions.
  • Three ecosystems. a16z sees Western (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity), Chinese (DeepSeek, Doubao, Kimi) and Russian (Yandex Alice, 71M monthly users) spheres 37.

17 · GlossaryTerms on this page

Agent
An AI system that pursues a goal by planning, calling tools and iterating on its own over many steps.
Agentic Work Unit (AWU)
Salesforce's usage metric for agent actions (3.2bn in Q2 FY27).
A2A (Agent2Agent)
A Google-originated protocol for agents to discover and talk to each other.
ACV (annual contract value)
The yearly value of a signed contract; ServiceNow reports AI ACV.
ARR / run-rate
The latest month (or quarter) of revenue, annualized. It overstates calendar-year revenue in fast-growing markets.
Capex
Capital expenditure: money spent on long-lived assets such as data centers, chips and servers, expensed over years through depreciation.
Computer use
A model operating a graphical interface through screenshots, clicks and typing.
Containment / resolution rate
The share of customer conversations an agent completes without a human.
Context window
How much text (in tokens) a model can consider at once. Agents re-send it on every step.
Copilot
AI embedded in a human workflow that suggests; the human decides.
CPM
Cost per thousand ad impressions.
Gross margin
(Revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue. For AI apps, cost of goods is mostly tokens and inference compute.
Jevons paradox
When a resource gets cheaper, total consumption rises, sometimes enough to raise total spending. Here, the resource is tokens.
MCP (Model Context Protocol)
An open standard for connecting models to tools and data; now under the Linux Foundation.
MAU / WAU
Monthly / weekly active users.
Mtok
One million tokens, the unit in which API prices are quoted.
NRR (net revenue retention)
Revenue from existing customers this year ÷ last year. Intercom Fin: 146%.
Outcome-based pricing
Charging per result (a resolution, a completed task) rather than per seat or per token.
PLG (product-led growth)
Users adopt a product self-serve before any enterprise contract. 27% of AI app spend versus 7% for SaaS.
RCT
Randomized controlled trial: the gold-standard way to measure cause and effect.
Seat compression
Fewer software licenses needed because agents do work people used to do.
Time horizon (METR)
The length of human-expert task a model completes with 50% reliability; it has doubled about every 7 months.
Token
About three-quarters of an English word; the unit of model input and output and of API billing.
Vibe coding
Building software by describing it in plain language to an agent (Lovable, Replit, Bolt, v0).
Wrapper
An app that adds a thin interface on top of someone else's model, with little proprietary value.
