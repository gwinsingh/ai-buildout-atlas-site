The 60-second version

  1. AI data centers are sold out. Vacancy in the main North American markets is 1.4%, and 0.24% in Northern Virginia, the world's biggest hub. Supply roughly tripled in four and a half years, yet vacancy kept falling E 12.
  2. Demand is now counted in gigawatts. JLL counts 25 GW absorbed in North America in the first half of 2026 alone, and more than 66 GW under construction, 95% of it spoken for E 911. Rents have risen about 70% since 2020 after a decade of decline E 9.
  3. The building is the cheap part but the slow part. Shell, power and cooling cost ~$11–18M per MW, only ~20–30% of a ~$50–60bn gigawatt campus. But land, power hook-ups and permits take 2–4 years, so this layer sets the pace of the whole build-out EA 131581.
  4. The profit goes to whoever owns what's scarce. Electrical and cooling equipment makers earn 20–28% operating margins and rising; specialty contractors earn 12–23%; owners of powered land capture a ~2× "developer's spread". Per gigawatt, though, the building layer earns roughly a tenth of what silicon does RA.
  5. The big risk is a mismatch in time. Landlords sign 15–20-year leases for tenants whose GPUs wear out in 5–6 years. Tenant credit (or a Google or Microsoft backstop), not concrete, decides who gets financed.
  6. Watch vacancy and rents (CBRE/JLL, Feb 2027), hyperscaler lease pipelines in 10-Qs, equipment backlogs, Stargate site milestones, and local moratoria.

01 · What this layer isThe factory around the chips

An AI data center is a factory whose raw material is electricity and whose product is computation. The GPUs are the machine tools. This layer is the factory itself.

That means the land and the building, plus the equipment that brings in hundreds of megawatts of power, delivers it cleanly to every rack, and removes all of it again as heat. It also covers the companies that finance, build, own and lease these factories. The chips, servers and network gear inside the racks belong to W05 and W07; the power plants and grid behind the substation to W03; the financing structures to W02; and the clouds that rent the capacity to W08.

Analogy

Think of a GPU cluster as a fleet of Formula 1 cars. The data center is the racetrack, pit garage, fuel system and cooling tunnel. The cars are worth far more than the track and are replaced every few seasons. But without the track no car races. A track takes 18–36 months to build and cannot be moved, which is why owning a finished, powered track is one of the most valuable positions in the AI economy right now.

Three kinds of players

  1. Developers and owners (the landlords). Colocation REITs (Equinix, Digital Realty); private wholesale developers (QTS, Vantage, Aligned, Stack, CyrusOne, EdgeCore, Switch, CloudHQ, Related Digital, Crusoe, Tract); and converted bitcoin miners (Core Scientific, IREN, Cipher, TeraWulf, Hut 8, Applied Digital, Galaxy). They buy land, secure power, build and lease. The hyperscalers (Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta, Oracle, xAI) are also the biggest self-builders.
  2. Equipment vendors (the arms dealers). Power-chain makers (Vertiv, Schneider Electric, Eaton, ABB, Siemens, Legrand, nVent, plus transformer and generator makers such as Hitachi Energy, Cummins, Caterpillar and Rolls-Royce) and cooling makers (Vertiv, Schneider/Motivair, Trane, Carrier, Johnson Controls, Modine, nVent, CoolIT, LiquidStack, Boyd Thermal, now part of Eaton).
  3. Builders (the contractors). Electrical and mechanical specialists (Quanta, EMCOR, Comfort Systems, MasTec, Rosendin, Cupertino Electric), site and civil (Sterling), general contractors (DPR, Turner, Holder, Clayco, Fortis, Mortenson) and engineers (Jacobs, Burns & McDonnell, Black & Veatch).

Why it matters

Of roughly $50–60bn spent per gigawatt of AI capacity, only about $11–17bn goes into the building and its mechanical, electrical and plumbing (MEP) systems E 131581. But this layer has the longest lead time (land, power, permits: 2–4 years), the longest asset life (15–30 years) and the hardest physical bottlenecks (transformers, turbines, switchgear, electricians). GPUs can be made faster than the buildings to plug them into can be energized.

Definition

Critical IT megawatt: the power actually available to the servers. The utility ("gross") megawatts are higher by the cooling and conversion overhead, measured by PUE (1.1–1.3 for new liquid-cooled builds). Many cost and rent figures on this page are per critical-IT MW, which is why sources disagree.

Explain like I'm new: what does a data center actually do all day?

It does three things, all the time. It takes in electricity, so much that one AI campus can draw as much as a mid-sized city. It feeds that electricity, cleaned and converted, to racks of computers. And it removes the heat, because every watt the chips use comes back out as heat that would cook them in seconds.

Everything expensive in the building serves one of those jobs: the substation and switchgear (bring power in), the UPS, busway and power shelves (deliver it), and the chillers, dry coolers and liquid loops (take the heat out). The building shell is mostly a weatherproof box around that plumbing.

02 · How it worksInside an AI data-center campus

Follow the electricity in from the grid and the heat back out to the sky. Every box on that path is a product someone sells.

Power arrives at up to 500 kilovolts and passes through five to seven conversion and distribution steps before it reaches a GPU at about one volt. Then all of it has to leave again as heat, through a liquid loop at the chip, a coolant distribution unit (CDU), a facility water loop and dry coolers in the yard.

The electrical chain, and the shift to 800 V DC

Today power is distributed as AC and converted to ~54 V DC inside the rack. At 1 MW per rack, 54 V would need about 200 kg of copper busbar per rack, which cannot scale to a gigawatt. NVIDIA's 800 V DC ("HVDC") architecture converts once near the facility edge, possibly with solid-state transformers, and ships 800 V DC to the rack, in production in 2027 alongside Kyber racks 73.

Why 800 V DC matters for money

It is a platform shift. Power-electronics content per MW rises, while the old AC UPS and PDU value pool shrinks. Incumbents with DC power-electronics skills (Vertiv, Eaton, Schneider, Delta, ABB) are racing to defend share against newcomers rich in power semiconductors (Infineon, Navitas, onsemi; see W06).

  • UPS (uninterruptible power supply) rides through the ~10 seconds before generators start. Cloud halls pair UPS with gensets for "Tier III" 2N or N+1 redundancy. AI training halls are dropping some of this: Microsoft's Fairwater Atlanta has no UPS, no gensets and no dual-corded distribution, and relies on grid reliability instead 47. Where UPS remains, it increasingly smooths the violent power swings of synchronous training, when whole clusters ramp tens of MW in milliseconds.
  • Busway (overhead copper or aluminum bars) plus rack-adjacent "power sidecars" are the norm at 130+ kW per rack.

Cooling: from air to liquid

Every watt that goes in comes out as heat. Cooling is ~15–20% of shell and MEP cost E conf: med and the fastest-changing part of the building. The industry has moved from blowing cold air at racks to piping liquid straight onto the chips.

Heat rejection outside the building. Direct-to-chip loops run warm water (~30–45 °C), so hyperscalers increasingly use dry coolers or air-cooled chillers with free cooling instead of evaporative towers. Epoch counts 172 air-cooled units at Fairwater Wisconsin, with ~1,900 planned 46. Closed-loop designs cut water use sharply: Stargate plans closed-loop liquid cooling at 6+ sites 34, and Vertiv's PurgeRite technology cuts start-up water use by up to 90% 19. CDUs are the new hot product.

Waste heat into cooling. On gas-powered campuses, absorption chillers can turn turbine exhaust heat into cooling. Johnson Controls' reference design says this frees enough power on a 1 GW site for up to 97 MW of extra compute 29.

03 · Market sizeA supply boom that still can't catch demand

Two research houses measure this market, and their numbers differ by more than 10×. Both are right; they measure different things.

JLL: all of North America, including hyperscaler self-build and frontier markets 39 GW of active capacity at YE2025, roughly half owned by hyperscalers E 12.
  • >66 GW under construction (mid-2026), 95% pre-committed, 77% in frontier markets 911
  • 25 GW absorbed in H1 2026, counting leases, self-build and pre-commitments 9
CBRE: leased colocation in eight "primary markets" N. Virginia, Atlanta, Chicago, Dallas–Fort Worth, Hillsboro, NY Tri-State, Phoenix, Silicon Valley. A clean rental-market measure that excludes self-build and most frontier sites E 1.
  • 10,903 MW inventory; 1.4% vacancy (H1 2026)
  • 7,481 MW under construction, 80.4% preleased
  • 1,456 MW absorbed in H1 2026

So "1,456 MW absorbed" (CBRE) and "25 GW absorbed" (JLL) in the same half-year do not contradict each other. The first is the rental market in the legacy hubs. The second counts every commitment, including self-build, pre-leases and frontier megasites. JLL has not published its method, so treat 25 GW as JLL's claim conf: med.

Absorption: from about 1 GW a year to 25 GW in a half-year

In one line: the North American market went from absorbing roughly 1–2 GW a year of mostly leased cloud capacity in 2020–21 to ~25 GW in a single half-year of mostly AI capacity in H1 2026, and Texas replaced Virginia as the center of gravity EA 912.

Rents are rising for the first time in a decade

Before 2021, colocation rents fell for most of a decade as hyperscalers used their scale to push wholesale prices down. Since then rents have risen ~63% (≈$120 → ~$196/kW/month, H2 2021 → H2 2025) A, and volume discounts for large deals have been "significantly reduced or eliminated" 5. The biggest (10 MW+) deals have seen the fastest increases: Silicon Valley +19% and N. Virginia +13.8% in H1 2025 4; NY Tri-State +19%, Atlanta +14.5% and Chicago +9.7% in H1 2026 12. JLL puts rents up ~70% since 2020 and expects ~7–9% a year through 2030 E 912.

The labor constraint

A gigawatt campus can need thousands of craft workers at peak, mostly electricians and pipefitters. The US electrician workforce (~800k, BLS; prior knowledge) grows slowly, apprenticeships take 4–5 years, and much of the workforce is near retirement. Cushman & Wakefield names "skilled labor shortages… particularly affecting MEP trades in an aging workforce" as a key driver of +21% YoY cost inflation E 15. 10 · Unit economicsWhat a megawatt costs, what it earns, and where $1 goes

Where $1 of data-center spend goes

Start from zero and add each slice of a gigawatt AI campus. The chips, systems and networking alone take ~64¢; all IT together ~73¢. The building, its power and cooling, the land and the fees, everything this page is about, come to roughly a quarter A.

Now zoom into the quarter that is the building. Electrical is the biggest bucket, which is why electrical vendors and electricians are the tightest bottleneck and have captured the most pricing power E conf: med.

The duration mismatch

About 75% of the campus dollar (the IT) depreciates over ~5–6 years. The ~25% building dollar lasts 15–30 years. Landlords therefore underwrite 15–20-year leases for tenants whose revenue-earning equipment will be replaced two or three times during the lease. The tenant's credit, or a guarantor's such as Google behind Fluidstack, matters more than the asset.

Development yield, cap rates and the developer's spread

  • Yield on cost. Rent is ~$1.7–2.0M per MW-year. With triple-net leases, landlord opex is ~15–20% of rent, so NOI ≈ $1.4–1.7M. At $12M/MW of cost that is a ~12–14% development yield; at $17.6M/MW, ~8–10%. Digital Realty targets 10%+ stabilized yields 31. Atlas range for good 2026 projects: 9–13% A conf: med.
  • Cap rates. Stabilized hyperscale assets with IG tenants traded around ~5–6.5% in 2024–26 (prior knowledge, conf: low-med). Value per MW at 6% ≈ $1.5M ÷ 0.06 ≈ $25M, close to Digital Realty's ~$27M/MW N. Virginia deal 31.
  • So the developer's spread is ~2×. That is why private equity, sovereigns and REITs are fighting over platforms (Aligned at $40bn 33) and why miners with powered sites re-rated.
  • Leverage. With IG tenants, lenders offer up to 85% loan-to-cost at spreads in the low-200 bp range; non-credit tenants get 70–80% and spreads 200–300 bp wider 11. Meta's Hyperion SPV bond ($27.3bn, A+) priced at ~T+225 bp, a 6.58% coupon 41.

Equipment content per megawatt

  • Electrical (substation to rack): ~$4–6M/MW of 2026 cost, of which perhaps $2.5–4M is OEM equipment (switchgear, transformers, UPS, busway, gensets) and the rest installation labor A (40–45% × $11–14M).
  • Thermal: ~$2–3.5M/MW, of which chillers or dry coolers plus CDUs and manifolds are perhaps $1–2M A.
  • 800 V DC and liquid cooling raise content per MW for power electronics and CDUs but may remove UPS and PDU content. The net effect for incumbents is unclear conf: low-med.

Where the profit sits

  1. Equipment vendors are the clearest winners. Order books ran ahead of shipments (Vertiv's 2.9× book-to-bill, Trane backlog +70%, Eaton electrical backlog +43%), customers prepay (Vertiv's deferred revenue doubled) and price realization is positive (Schneider ~4 points of price in Q2 21; Vertiv "positive price-cost" 19). Margins are expanding 100–400 bp a year.
  2. Powered land and development platforms capture the scarcity rent. The ~2× developer's spread and the prices paid (Aligned at ~$6M/MW of mostly planned capacity; DLR at ~$27M/MW stabilized) show that the scarcest input, energized land with permits, earns the rent, not the concrete. The miners' equity re-rating is the same phenomenon.
  3. Specialty contractors are no longer commodity. Comfort Systems at 17% and Sterling E-Infra at 23% operating margins are far above historical contractor norms (mid-single digits; prior knowledge). But over a full cycle these margins are the most exposed to normalization once backlogs burn off.
  4. General construction and commodity components (concrete, steel framing, generic HVAC) earn the least and have no durable pricing power.
  5. Landlords' returns are bond-like once leased. A 9–13% unlevered yield on a 15-year IG lease is excellent real-estate economics, but capped: rent is fixed with escalators, and the upside went to whoever owned the site early. Over-built or obsolete shells (air-cooled, low-density, poorly powered) are the value trap.
  6. Versus the rest of the stack. Of ~$55bn per GW, the W04 building-and-MEP pool is ~$12–17bn at ~10–25% operating margins: roughly $2–3bn of operating profit per GW A. NVIDIA's ~$35bn per GW at ~60%+ operating margin is ~$20bn+ per GW A. By this crude measure silicon captures ~7–10× the building layer's profit per GW conf: med.

13 · Competitive dynamicsLease or build, miners or developers, and who wins the 800 V race

  • Self-build vs lease. Hyperscalers own roughly half of North American capacity 12 but lease heavily for speed. Microsoft's $196.6bn and Oracle's $288bn of not-yet-commenced leases 6970 show that leasing, not owning, is the marginal source of AI capacity for everyone except Meta, Google and xAI. Meta uses off-balance-sheet JVs instead (Hyperion) 41.
  • Wholesale developers vs miners vs neocloud-owned builds. Miners won 2025–26 on time-to-power. Crusoe shows the next model: vertical integration of power, building, manufacturing and cloud 38. CoreWeave tried to buy its landlord (Core Scientific) and failed 83. Chipmakers are now equity-linked anchor tenants: AMD holds warrants at Core Scientific 52; NVIDIA reportedly put a $150m deposit on Abilene 36.
  • Vendor consolidation. Power companies are buying thermal companies (Eaton–Boyd, Schneider–Motivair, Vertiv–CoolTera and others) to sell "grid-to-chip" or "power + cooling" bundles. The next fight is for 800 V DC and solid-state-transformer leadership, where Delta, ABB, Eaton, Schneider, Vertiv and power-semiconductor firms (Infineon, Navitas, onsemi; W06) meet 73.
  • Prefab and "AI factory" reference designs. NVIDIA publishes reference designs that co-specify power and cooling (prior knowledge). Vendors in the reference design win share, which tilts the field toward the largest incumbents.
  • Disruptors that could shrink this layer's value. Grid-only, no-UPS training halls (Fairwater Atlanta 47) remove a traditional high-margin product. Tents and rapid structures (Prometheus 42) commoditize the shell. Higher chip efficiency reduces MW per unit of compute, though so far it has triggered more demand (Jevons). Space-based or offshore compute remains speculative.
  • International. The Gulf (Stargate UAE, Humain), India (Google Vizag, 1 GW 49), the Nordics, Malaysia/Johor and Alberta 2 compete for projects, mostly as sites for US hyperscalers.

14 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

Pre-committed, investment-grade demand meets supply that cannot be rushed

  • Demand is pre-committed. 95% of the 66 GW pipeline is spoken for 9; the biggest tenants are the largest cash generators on earth, with ~$570bn+ of uncommenced leases on their balance sheets 697071.
  • Scarcity persists to 2028+. JLL projects vacancy "near zero through 2028" 11; power, permits and labor cannot be expanded quickly.
  • Rents are rising and volume discounts are gone, reversing a decade of decline 512.
  • Vendors have multi-year visibility: Vertiv's $15bn backlog, Schneider's 2–3-year GW forecasts, Eaton's 307 GW tracked pipeline 172224.
  • Platform shifts add content. Liquid cooling and 800 V DC create new product cycles for incumbents.

Bear case

Long leases on short-lived tenants, built at inflating cost

  • Duration mismatch and tenant concentration. 15–20-year leases are only as good as OpenAI, Anthropic, xAI and the neoclouds. The Abilene expansion's collapse when financing talks failed 36 previews what happens when a tenant's economics wobble.
  • Speculative supply. 66 GW under construction is multiples of actual 2025 deliveries. If AI revenue (W10) lags, frontier campuses without IG tenants face lease-up risk.
  • Execution strain. Vertiv's Q2 miss 1620, Modine's margin squeeze 30, Abilene's winter liquid-cooling damage 36 and xAI's permitting fights 44; costs inflating 6–21% a year 1315.
  • Opacity. Vertiv stopped disclosing quarterly orders 18; off-balance-sheet JVs (Hyperion, $46bn max exposure 43) and private credit hide leverage.
  • Politics and obsolescence. Opposition, incentive rollbacks and ratepayer backlash (W03); 130 kW halls may need costly retrofits for 600 kW–1 MW racks and 800 V DC in 2027–28 73.

17 · China angleA preview of C04: chips, not power, are the constraint

Teaser for Track 2 (C04, coming soon)

  • Different binding constraint. China's limit is chips, not power. State Grid has ample generation and fast interconnection in western provinces, and the "East Data, West Computing" program steers DCs to eight national hubs (Inner Mongolia, Ningxia, Gansu, Guizhou…). Expect higher reported vacancy, the reverse of the ~1% US figure (prior knowledge).
  • Developers. GDS (and its international spin-off DayOne), VNET, Chindata, the China Telecom/Mobile/Unicom IDC arms, and self-builds by Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Baidu.

  • Metrics to compare: vacancy/utilization, rent per kW, cost per MW, PUE mandates (≤1.25–1.3 in hubs), liquid-cooling share and power price per kWh.

18 · GlossaryTerms on this page

800 V DC
NVIDIA-led architecture that distributes high-voltage DC to the racks from 2027, replacing AC distribution and in-rack AC-to-DC conversion.
Absorption
Net change in occupied or committed capacity over a period. CBRE measures leased colo in primary markets; JLL includes self-build and frontier markets.
ABS / CMBS / SASB
Asset-backed securities / commercial mortgage-backed securities / single-asset single-borrower CMBS: bonds backed by a data center's lease cash flows.
AI factory
NVIDIA's term for a data center built mainly to produce tokens (training or inference).
Book-to-bill
Orders received divided by revenue billed in a period. Above 1 means the backlog is growing.
BTM
Behind the meter: power generated on site (gas turbines, fuel cells, batteries) rather than bought through the utility grid.
Busway
Overhead copper or aluminum bars that carry power to racks, replacing bundles of cable.
Cap rate
Stabilized net operating income divided by asset value. A lower cap rate means a higher valuation.
CDU
Coolant distribution unit: pumps plus a heat exchanger linking the rack's liquid loop to the facility water loop.
Critical IT load
Power available to IT equipment, in MW. Gross or utility MW is higher by the PUE and redundancy overhead.
DLC
Direct-to-chip liquid cooling: liquid-filled cold plates sit on the processors.
Dry cooler
Rejects heat to outside air without evaporating water (also: air-cooled chiller or condenser).
Frontier market
A region with little prior data-center development, e.g. West Texas, Louisiana, North Dakota (JLL's term).
Hyperscaler
Amazon, Microsoft, Google, Meta, Oracle (and increasingly xAI), building at gigawatt scale.
IG / HY
Investment grade / high yield: credit ratings above or below BBB-. IG tenants make leases cheap to finance.
Interconnection
In power: the utility connection and grid upgrades needed to serve a load. In colo: cross-connects between networks.
Loan-to-cost
Debt as a share of a project's total development cost; 80–85% for IG-tenant data centers.
MEP
Mechanical, electrical and plumbing systems: everything in the building except the shell and the IT.
Neocloud
A GPU-cloud specialist such as CoreWeave, Nebius, Crusoe Cloud, Lambda, Fluidstack or IREN.
NNN
Triple-net lease: the tenant pays power, taxes, insurance and maintenance on top of base rent.
NOI
Net operating income: rent minus the landlord's operating costs, before interest and depreciation.
PDU / RPP
Power distribution unit / remote power panel: distribute power to rows and racks.
Powered shell
A building with utility power delivered but without the tenant's MEP fit-out.
Preleasing
Capacity leased before construction completes.
PUE
Power usage effectiveness: total facility power divided by IT power. 1.1–1.3 is typical for new liquid-cooled builds.
RDHx
Rear-door heat exchanger: a water coil on the back of a rack, used to retrofit air-cooled halls for denser racks.
RVG
Residual value guarantee: the tenant promises the lessor a minimum asset value at lease end (as Meta does at Hyperion).
SST
Solid-state transformer: a power-electronics replacement for a copper-and-iron transformer that can convert MV AC to 800 V DC.
Switchgear
Breakers and switches that route and protect medium- and low-voltage power.
Take-or-pay
The tenant pays whether or not it uses the capacity. CBRE notes 60–85% minimum-utilization floors appearing in colo deals.
TCV
Total contract value: the sum of payments over a contract's full base term.
UPS
Uninterruptible power supply: batteries plus power electronics that bridge outages until generators start.
xScale
Equinix's hyperscale joint-venture platform.
Yield on cost
Stabilized NOI divided by total development cost.
