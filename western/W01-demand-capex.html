The 60-second version

  1. The spending has doubled again. Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon and Meta guide to ~$733bn of 2026 capex G, ~1.8× their ~$413bn of 2025. Every one of them raised its plan during the year, and Wall Street's December forecast was only $527bn E39.
  2. It now eats nearly all their cash. In Q2 2026 the Big 4 spent $165bn, or 96% of their operating cash flow A35. Alphabet had its first negative free-cash-flow quarter since its IPO, Amazon's trailing FCF is below zero, and Oracle is deeply negative. The gap is being filled with bonds, leases and customer prepayments (W02).
  3. The demand evidence is strong but concentrated. ~$2.46T of contracted backlog, cloud growth of +37% to +121%, and Google tokens up 3× in three quarters R. But we estimate ~43% of that backlog is owed by just two cash-burning labs, OpenAI and Anthropic A.
  4. About two-thirds of each dollar buys chips and servers; one-third buys buildings and power. Nvidia alone receives roughly 37¢ of the ~$950bn W01 tallies for 2026 A. Memory-price inflation is now a real line item: ~$25bn of Microsoft's plan is component pricing, not extra capacity G.
  5. The next test is depreciation. Big-4 D&A could roughly double from $139bn (2025) to ~$250–300bn in 2027 A, just as physical limits bite: Texas halted new permits, Loudoun County paused applications, and Oracle declared force majeure on a 2.5 GW Stargate site.
  6. Watch: the late-October earnings round (2027 capex framing), Oracle's financing in December, and the labs' IPO filings, the first audited look at whether their revenue can carry their commitments.

01 · What this layer isThe buyers: who decides to pour the concrete

W01 is the demand end of the AI build-out. It covers the companies that decide to buy GPUs, pour concrete and sign 15-year power contracts, and the evidence that someone will eventually pay for what they build.

Every dollar recorded here becomes revenue for another layer: chips (W05), memory and foundry (W06), networks and servers (W07), construction and cooling (W04) and power (W03). Two questions decide the whole cycle: does this layer keep spending, and do its customers (W08 to W10) earn enough to justify it?

Analogy

Think of the hyperscalers as airlines ordering aircraft years ahead of demand. Capex is the order book for planes and airports. Backlog is tickets already sold for future flights. Depreciation is the wear on the planes that shows up in every future year's profit and loss. Free cash flow is the cash left after paying for new planes. The 2026 debate is whether the airlines are ordering too many planes because two very large charter customers, OpenAI and Anthropic, have booked a huge share of future seats and have not yet earned the money to pay for them.

How a capex dollar moves through the accounts

Four numbers explain almost everything on this page: capex, operating cash flow, backlog and depreciation. They live in different financial statements and move on different clocks. Cash leaves the moment a GPU is bought; the cost reaches reported profit slowly, over five to six years; and the revenue that pays for it was often contracted years earlier.

Why does free cash flow collapse while profits still look fine?

Because cash and profit run on different clocks. When Alphabet spends $44.9bn in a quarter, all of it leaves the bank that quarter. But on the income statement only one year's depreciation shows up, about a sixth of the server cost. So reported profit keeps growing while free cash flow can go negative.

The bill arrives later: every quarter of heavy capex adds a slice of depreciation to the next six years of profit. That is why the "depreciation wave" (section 09) is the thing to watch in 2027.

Why it matters

This layer is the top of the money funnel for the physical build-out. If the buyers keep spending, every supplier layer grows. If they pause, the slowdown ripples down the stack within a quarter or two, starting with the highest-margin suppliers.

02 · How it worksA primer on capex, leases, backlog and depreciation

What is inside the headline capex number

  • Cash capex ("purchases of property and equipment" on the cash-flow statement) is the money actually spent this quarter on servers, chips, buildings and land.
  • Finance leases. When Microsoft or Meta leases a whole data-center building for most of its useful life, accounting treats it almost like buying it with borrowed money. Microsoft and Meta include these in the capex they talk about; Amazon and Alphabet talk about cash capex. At Microsoft the gap is large: FY26 Q2 capex of $37.5bn included $6.7bn of finance leases R4.
  • Operating leases (a colocation contract or a shorter lease) are not capex; they show up as rent. This is why Microsoft's July-2026 accounting change matters: with buildings now depreciated over 25 years instead of 15, more new leases fall below the finance-lease test and drop out of "capex", even though the spending is unchanged G27.
  • Customer-funded capex is a new wrinkle. Oracle's big AI contracts increasingly use prepayments or bring-your-own-hardware deals: Oracle reported $75bn of such commitments at May 2026 R30. Its Q1 FY27 gross capex was $28.5bn, but net cash capex was ~$18bn after prepayments R29.

Short-lived versus long-lived assets

Short-lived assets (GPUs, accelerators, CPUs, memory, storage and network gear) are depreciated over about 5–6 years. They start earning quickly and become obsolete quickly. Long-lived assets are land (not depreciated), buildings (15→25 years at Microsoft; "30-plus years" at Amazon R21) and electrical and cooling plant (about 10–20 years). The rule of thumb today is about 2/3 short-lived and 1/3 long-lived: Microsoft says ~2/3 of its capex goes to "short-lived assets, primarily GPUs and CPUs" R24, and Alphabet's Q2 2026 capex was ~60% servers and ~40% data centers and networking R10.

Backlog: why it counts as evidence of demand

RPO (remaining performance obligation) is revenue already contracted but not yet recognised, disclosed under US accounting rules (ASC 606). AI capacity contracts are typically take-or-pay or committed-spend deals over 2–7 years; Amazon says its AI contracts have "at least five-year terms" R21.

Three caveats on backlog

  1. RPO can include multi-year software contracts: Microsoft's commercial RPO includes Office and Microsoft 365.
  2. Contracts can be renegotiated, and the customer's credit matters.
  3. Some backlog depends on capacity that has not been built yet. Backlog is a claim on future capacity, not money in hand.

Depreciation and the useful-life lever

Capex reaches the income statement slowly, as depreciation. A $10bn GPU purchase depreciated over 6 years costs ~$1.67bn a year; over 4 years it would cost $2.5bn a year. So extending an asset's useful life raises reported profit today, and shortening it does the reverse. Between 2020 and 2025 the hyperscalers mostly extended server lives (4→6 years), adding several billion dollars a year to operating income. Critics, notably Michael Burry in November 2025, argue GPUs become economically obsolete faster than 5–6 years because Nvidia now releases a new generation every year.

The counter-evidence so far: Oracle said it re-priced 4-plus-year-old GPUs at a ~20% premium on renewal, with utilisation of 97.9% R29; Amazon says servers break even in "a little less than three years" R21.

Free-cash-flow arithmetic

FCF = operating cash flow − cash capex (Meta and Amazon also subtract finance-lease principal). When the capex-to-OCF ratio approaches 100%, free cash flow goes to zero, and anything spent on buybacks or dividends must come from the balance sheet or new debt (see W02).

Why compute demand compounds

  • Training compute scales with model size × data. Frontier runs now use ~100k to 1M+ H100-equivalents at single sites; Epoch AI tracks Colossus 2 at 1.1M H100-equivalents and 946 MW of IT power E46.
  • Inference scales with users × queries × tokens per query. Reasoning models and agents multiply tokens per task, often by 10–100× compared with a simple chat reply (industry estimates, medium confidence).
  • Price elasticity. Cost per token falls ~10× a year for a given capability, which expands usage. Google says Gemini 3 cut the cost of core AI responses by more than 30% R12. Total spend grows as long as volume grows faster than price falls: the Jevons paradox argument for continued capex.

03 · How muchHow much is being spent, and how fast it is growing

Big-4 cash capex went from $151bn in 2022 to $376bn in 2025, and guidance puts 2026 at ~$733bn on the companies' own basis. Q2 2026 was the heaviest quarter on record.

Year-on-year growth in Big-4 cash capex: 2023 −3%, 2024 +55%, 2025 +65%, and H1 2026 vs H1 2025 +84% A35.

Key insight: guidance implies acceleration, not a plateau

On the companies' own basis, H1 2026 spending was Microsoft $72.9bn + Alphabet $80.6bn + Amazon ~$96.3bn net + Meta $50.9bn = ~$301bn. Full-year guidance midpoints are 175 + 200 + 220 + 137.5 = ~$733bn. That leaves ~$430bn for H2, or ~$215bn a quarter, about 30% above the record Q2 A.

How the backlog grew

Oracle's RPO went from $138bn (May 2025) to $455bn (Aug 2025, +359%) on OpenAI's reported ~$300bn, ~4.5 GW, five-year deal starting in 2027, plus contracts with Meta, xAI and others R33. Google Cloud's backlog nearly doubled in Q1 2026, from $240bn to ~$460bn, then added another $50bn; "just over half" converts within 24 months R1012. AWS's backlog is "growing triple digits", from $195bn at Jun 2025 R21. Microsoft's RPO excluding OpenAI grew ~25% R6, a useful check on non-lab demand.

Usage: tokens are compounding

Google's first-party API traffic went 7bn → 10bn → 16bn → 22bn tokens per minute over four quarters R11121314. Across all Google surfaces, monthly tokens reached ~1.3 quadrillion in Oct 2025, up from 980T in Jul 2025 and ~480T in May 2025. The Gemini app had 950M monthly users, AI Mode in Search more than 1bn, and ~500 Cloud customers each process over 1T tokens a year (up from 330 a quarter earlier) R1112. Microsoft reports customers at a trillion-token annualised run-rate up 4× y/y R6.

Revenue: cloud is accelerating, and margins are expanding

Google Cloud grew +82% to $24.8bn with a 35.6% operating margin (vs 20.7% a year earlier) R15. AWS grew +36.7%, its fastest in 18 quarters, to $42.2bn with a 39.4% margin R202122. Azure grew +43% and passed $100bn of annual revenue R16; Oracle's OCI grew +121% R28. Nvidia's data-center revenue was $89.0bn in the July-2026 quarter (+117% y/y) R36, a real-time read of what the buyers are ordering.

Key insight

Cloud margins are rising while capex explodes. That is strong evidence that AI capacity is being rented out at a profit today. The open question is not years 1–3 of each server's life but years 4–6, when newer chips arrive and prices for older capacity fall.

08 · Unit economicsDoes a dollar of AI capex pay back?

Today, yes, comfortably. Cloud segment margins are rising, servers pay back in under three years, and old GPUs are being re-priced upward. The risk sits in years 4–6 of each server's life.

The worked example A. Take $1 of capex. About $0.67 goes to servers depreciated over 6 years ($0.11 a year) and $0.33 to buildings depreciated over ~20 years ($0.017 a year), so depreciation is ~$0.13 a year. Assume power, operations and staff cost ~$0.05 a year per capex dollar. If the capacity earns $0.35–0.45 a year of revenue, consistent with a ~3-year server payback and AWS's margins, the operating margin on that capacity is ~50–60% at maturity. This is why cloud margins are rising even while capex explodes.

09 · Profit captureWho profits, and the depreciation wave that is coming

  • The buyers are still very profitable. Combined Big-4 TTM operating income is ~$480bn A35. Cloud-segment margins are expanding (Google Cloud 35.6%, AWS 39.4%) R1520. So far AI capacity is being rented out at attractive margins.
  • But the profit pool is shifting upstream. Nvidia runs a 75% gross margin on $96.2bn of quarterly revenue R36, against hyperscaler corporate gross margins in the mid-60s% (Microsoft 67%) R6, and falling: Microsoft Cloud's gross margin slipped to 65% from 66% R6. Memory vendors are capturing price inflation.
  • Who earns on the build itself: chips and memory (W05, W06) earn high margins now. Hyperscalers earn later, and only if utilisation holds. Labs (W09) currently lose money on compute but own the customer relationships.

The depreciation math A

Big-4 quarterly D&A was ~$45.1bn in Q2 2026 (Microsoft 11.3 + Alphabet 7.5 + Amazon 20.0 + Meta 6.4) R35, or ~$180bn annualised. Each quarter of ~$200bn capex adds roughly $5–6bn of quarterly D&A once in service (≈ $133bn short-lived ÷ 24 quarters + $67bn long-lived ÷ 80 quarters). After six more such quarters (through Q4 2027), quarterly D&A would be ~$75–80bn. Full-year 2027 D&A of ~$250–300bn vs $139bn in 2025 means an extra $110–160bn of annual expense against ~$480bn of operating income. Oracle's FY26 depreciation already roughly doubled to $7.6bn R25.

12 · Competitive dynamicsWhat could change who buys, and how

  1. Self-supply of silicon. Google (TPU), Amazon (Trainium) and Meta (MTIA) are shifting capex dollars from Nvidia to their own chips. More than 50% of the AI chips Amazon landed in the past 12 months were Trainium R23. This lowers W01's cost per unit of compute and moves margin from Nvidia to the hyperscalers.
  2. Neoclouds as capacity shock absorbers. Hyperscalers rent from CoreWeave, Nebius and Nscale to move faster and keep assets off their balance sheets; neoclouds are the fastest-growing capex segment E44.
  3. Labs becoming builders. OpenAI (Stargate, Pike County) and Anthropic (Fluidstack, Nscale, TeraWulf) now contract for powered shells directly, bypassing the hyperscaler margin. Over time this threatens hyperscaler cloud share.
  4. Oracle as the leveraged marginal builder. The fastest-growing backlog but the weakest balance sheet among the majors (negative FCF, heavy issuance): the bellwether for financing stress.
  5. Efficiency shocks. Cheaper models (the DeepSeek moment of Jan 2025; Gemini 3's >30% cost cut R12; Claude Opus 5.5 at "40% less to run" per Anthropic's Sep-2026 release 68) cut cost per token. So far that has raised total demand. A plateau in model usefulness would reverse the effect.
  6. Smaller, distributed inference sites. OpenAI and Anthropic are seeking 20–30 MW inference deployments in the US, UK and Nordics (Sep 2026) E57. Demand is broadening beyond giant training campuses.
  7. Sovereign demand (Gulf, Europe, India, Japan) diversifies the buyer base away from the US Big 4 and the labs.

13 · Bull vs bearIs capex chasing demand, or outrunning it?

Bull case

Capex is chasing demand, not the other way round

  • Backlog exceeds spend. ~$2.46T of disclosed backlog vs ~$1.0T of 2026 AI capex. Every major cloud says it is capacity-constrained: "demand continues to exceed available supply" (Microsoft) 2; capacity "largely reserved" (AWS) 21; "nowhere near enough compute" (Meta) 19.
  • Revenue is accelerating with the spend. Google Cloud +82%, OCI +121%, AWS +36.7% (up from 17% a year earlier), Azure +43% R. Cloud margins are expanding, not collapsing 1520.
  • Tokens are compounding. Google API tokens per minute rose 3.1× in three quarters 1114; reasoning and agentic workloads multiply tokens per task.
  • The funding is there. Big-4 operating cash flow is still ~$650bn+ TTM; labs raised ~$200bn+ of equity in 2026 E. At ~0.8–1% of GDP, AI capex remains below the 1.5%+ peaks of past tech cycles 39.
  • The hardware lasts. Oracle's +20% renewal premium on 4-year-old GPUs and 97.9% utilisation 29 support 5–6-year depreciation schedules.

Bear case

Capex is outrunning cash, and the backlog is circular

  • FCF has collapsed. Big-4 capex/OCF went from ~41% (2023) to 96% (Q2 26). Alphabet and Amazon are FCF-negative, Meta's FCF is ~0, Oracle is deeply negative R. H2-26 guidance implies aggregate FCF of zero or below A.
  • Concentration. OpenAI was ~45% of Microsoft's RPO 4 and very likely ~40–50% of Oracle's E; with Anthropic, perhaps ~43% of all disclosed backlog A. Both labs lose money and depend on mega-rounds, some funded by their own suppliers. Their combined revenue run-rate (~$105bn, Jul 2026, see W09) is a fraction of their commitments.
  • The revenue gap. Bain's $2T-by-2030 requirement and $800bn shortfall 41. Sequoia-style math A: ~$365bn of CY2026 Nvidia DC revenue × 2 × 2 ≈ ~$1.5–1.6T of annual end-customer revenue needed, against a few hundred billion of identifiable AI revenue today Low confidence.
  • Depreciation cliff. Big-4 D&A roughly doubles by 2027 A. Useful-life extensions flatter near-term earnings, and Meta's operating income is already falling despite +28% revenue 18.
  • Price, not volume. ~$25bn of Microsoft's plan is memory inflation 3: part of the capex "growth" buys no extra capacity.
  • Physical and political limits. Texas, Loudoun, New York and New Mexico could strand money already committed or delay revenue.

Digestion or acceleration? Our read, September 2026

  • Signs of acceleration (dominant): every 2026 guide was raised; Microsoft guides Q1 FY27 above $50bn; Alphabet says 2027 will be "significantly" higher; Oracle's FY27 gross guide is $90–95bn; Nvidia guides Q3 to $108bn (+12% q/q) G36; Dell'Oro reports Q2 2026 data-center capex +92% E44.
  • Signs of digestion or strain (emerging): Meta narrowed its range (raising the low end, not the top) 18; Microsoft's lease reclassification lowers headline capex; the stock market punished capex raises (Alphabet −4–5% after its Q2 raise) E17; Oracle's stock fell on its financing plans E31; the Oracle–Blue Owl force majeure 54; permit freezes 5556.

Our judgement A

2026 is locked in by committed orders. 2027 is likely to be another +30–50% year (the Street expects ~$1.0–1.14T) if power and permits allow. The first real digestion risk is 2028, when the Oracle, OpenAI and Stargate "peak capex years" end and lab revenue must carry the contracts. The Atlas base case for 2027 Western AI investment is ~$1.35T (bull $1.5T, bear $1.2T; see W11).

17 · GlossaryTerms on this page

Capex (capital expenditure)
Spending on long-lasting assets such as servers, chips, buildings and land. It is capitalised on the balance sheet and depreciated over time rather than expensed at once.
Cash capex / purchases of PP&E
Capex actually paid in cash in the period, shown as 'purchases of property and equipment' on the cash-flow statement.
PP&E
Property, plant and equipment: the physical assets (servers, buildings, land, electrical plant) a company owns and depreciates.
Finance lease
A lease that covers most of an asset's economic life. It is booked like a debt-financed purchase; Microsoft and Meta include it in the capex they talk about.
Operating lease
A shorter lease (for example a colocation contract) expensed as rent in operating costs. It is not capex.
Useful life
The period over which an asset is depreciated. A longer life means lower annual depreciation and higher reported profit today.
D&A
Depreciation (of physical assets) and amortisation (of intangibles). Non-cash expenses that spread the cost of capex over the asset's life.
OCF (operating cash flow)
Cash generated by the business in the period before any investment spending.
FCF (free cash flow)
Operating cash flow minus capex (Meta and Amazon also subtract finance-lease principal). What is left for buybacks, dividends or debt repayment.
RPO (remaining performance obligation)
Revenue already contracted but not yet recognised, disclosed under US GAAP (ASC 606). 'Backlog' is the looser term AWS and Google use.
Backlog
Contracted future revenue. For cloud providers it is mostly multi-year committed-spend or take-or-pay capacity contracts.
Take-or-pay
A contract where the customer pays for committed capacity whether or not it uses it.
BYOH (bring your own hardware)
The customer buys the GPUs and the cloud provider operates them (used by Oracle), which moves capex onto the customer.
Hyperscaler
A cloud or platform company operating at global scale: Microsoft, Amazon, Google, Meta and Oracle.
Neocloud
A GPU-specialist cloud provider such as CoreWeave, Nebius, Nscale, Lambda or Crusoe.
Big 4
Microsoft, Alphabet (Google), Amazon and Meta: the four largest AI capex spenders. Oracle is tracked separately because its fiscal year ends in May.
GW / MW (IT vs facility)
Units of power. IT power is what the chips draw; facility power adds cooling and electrical losses (see PUE). 1 GW = 1,000 MW.
PUE
Power usage effectiveness: total facility power divided by IT power. A PUE of 1.2 means 20% extra power for cooling and losses.
H100-equivalent
Epoch AI's unit for comparing installed compute across chip generations, expressed as the number of Nvidia H100 GPUs that would deliver the same performance.
Token
A chunk of text, about three-quarters of a word, processed by a model. The basic unit of AI usage and pricing.
Training vs inference
Training builds a model from data; inference runs the finished model to answer queries. Inference now drives most growth in compute demand.
Reasoning model / agent
Models that generate long internal chains of thought, or act in multi-step loops with tools. Both multiply the tokens used per task, often 10–100x.
Jevons paradox
When something gets cheaper to use, total consumption can rise enough that total spending goes up. Applied to AI: cheaper tokens lead to more total compute demand.
SPV (special-purpose vehicle)
A separate legal entity that owns and finances an asset, keeping the debt off the sponsor's balance sheet (e.g. Meta and Blue Owl's Hyperion joint venture).
ATM (at-the-market) offering
Selling new shares gradually into the market at prevailing prices rather than in one large deal.
Force majeure
A contract clause that excuses a party from performing when extraordinary events outside its control prevent it.
Short-lived assets
Microsoft's term for GPUs, CPUs, servers and network gear, depreciated over about six years. Long-lived assets are buildings and electrical plant (15–30+ years).
Capex / OCF ratio
Capex divided by operating cash flow. At 100%, every dollar the business generates is reinvested and free cash flow is zero.
TTM
Trailing twelve months: the sum of the last four reported quarters.
Stargate
The OpenAI-led data-center programme with SoftBank, Oracle and MGX, announced Jan 2025 at $500bn and 10 GW, now spanning sites in Texas, New Mexico, Ohio and abroad.
ERCOT
The Electric Reliability Council of Texas, which runs most of the Texas grid and its queue of requests to connect large new loads.
CCGT
Combined-cycle gas turbine: a gas power plant that reuses exhaust heat to run a steam turbine. The workhorse of new behind-the-meter data-center power.
Sovereign AI
National AI compute programmes funded or directed by governments or state funds, such as Stargate UAE or Saudi Arabia's Humain.
