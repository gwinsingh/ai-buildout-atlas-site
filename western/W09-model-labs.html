01 · What this layer isWhat a model lab is, and how it makes money

A model lab trains large AI models, tunes them until they are useful and safe, and sells access to them.

It sells in three ways. Developers pay per token through an API. Consumers and businesses pay per seat for chat apps. And increasingly, customers pay for agents that do a whole task: write the code, run the spreadsheet workflow, operate the browser. Labs sit between the cloud and chip layers that sell them capacity (W05–W08) and the application layer (W10), where most end-customer money enters.

Analogy

A lab is a pharmaceutical company welded to a power utility. Like pharma, it spends huge sums up front on R&D: the training run is the clinical trial. Most experiments never ship, and one blockbuster (GPT-4, Claude Code) can pay for years of failures. The protection is brief, because competitors reproduce a capability within 6–18 months.

Like a utility, once the model is trained, every request burns metered compute. The gross margin is the spread between the price per token and the cost of the GPU-seconds needed to make it. Scale, utilization and efficiency decide who makes money.

What are a token, a price per million tokens, and a "run-rate"?

A token is roughly ¾ of an English word. Labs price in dollars per million tokens, with separate prices for input (what you send) and output (what the model writes). Output usually costs 4–8× more.

Run-rate revenue (or "ARR") is the latest period's revenue annualized, for example July revenue × 12. For a usage-based business it is not contracted recurring revenue. It can fall as fast as it rose, which is why this page treats it with care.

02 · How it worksWhy labs keep spending: scaling, reasoning and agents

In 2020 OpenAI researchers found that a model's error falls as a smooth power law in parameters, data and compute.91 DeepMind's Chinchilla paper (2022) refined the best mix to roughly 20 training tokens per parameter.92 The practical lesson is that 10× more compute buys a predictably better model. That predictability is what lets labs raise money against future capability.

Three stacked ways to scale

  1. Pre-training compute, still growing ~5×/yr at the frontier E.73
  2. Post-training reinforcement learning (RL), scaled deliberately since OpenAI's o1 (Dec 2024) and now a large share of frontier training budgets.
  3. Test-time compute: letting the model "think" longer on each query.

Efficiency compounds on top. Epoch estimates that pre-training algorithms get ~3× more efficient each year, and that AI-chip performance per dollar improves ~1.5×/yr (+49%/yr since 2023) E.73

What a frontier model costs to train

The cost of the final training run has grown ~2.4×/yr since 2016 and would pass $1bn per run around 2027 if the trend held E.71 But the final run is a minority of spend. Of OpenAI's ~$7bn of 2024 compute, Epoch estimates ~$5bn went to R&D compute (research, experiments and unreleased models) and only ~$1.8bn to inference E.74 So a "$1bn training run" usually sits on top of $3–5bn of experiments. The honest 2026 answer to "what does a frontier model cost?" is several billion dollars per generation, all-in A.

Reasoning models multiply tokens per task

Starting with o1 (Dec 2024), models are trained with RL to write long internal chains of thought before answering. Accuracy on hard math, code and science rises with the number of thinking tokens. Every lab now ships a reasoning mode: OpenAI "high/xhigh/max",78 Claude extended thinking and max effort, Gemini Deep Think, and Meta Muse Spark's "Contemplating" mode.56

The economics follow. One reasoning answer can use 10–100× the tokens of a plain chat reply, and agents chain hundreds of steps. So even as the price per token falls, tokens per task rise sharply. The same model at "max" effort can cost ~4× what it costs at "medium",78 and buyers increasingly compare cost per completed task.2

Inference is becoming the bulk of compute, with a caveat

The structural argument: training is a fixed cost per model generation, while inference scales with users × tasks × tokens per task, and all three are compounding. Once a model serves a billion users, serving dwarfs the one-off training job in FLOPs.

What the dollars actually show: in OpenAI's 2025 audited P&L, R&D ($19.2bn, mostly training and research compute) was still ~2.5× cost of revenue ($7.5bn, mostly inference) E. Of the $17.2bn OpenAI paid Microsoft, $10.6bn was booked as R&D and $6.0bn as cost of revenue.23 OpenAI's 2026 plan has training at $32bn vs inference at ~$14bn E.2549 So at the frontier labs, training and research compute still exceed inference in dollars in 2025–26. "Inference is the bulk" is true for FLOPs executed across the whole industry (including Copilot, Search AI Overviews and Meta feeds) and for the direction of travel.

At Anthropic, Q2 2026 compute cost ~56¢ per revenue dollar, down from 71¢ in Q1 E.7 Revenue growing faster than training spend is what an inference-led mix looks like.

Our read

By 2027, inference (including RL rollouts, which behave like inference) will be the majority of lab compute dollars at Anthropic and probably at OpenAI A. At OpenAI it depends on whether the 2027 training budget really doubles to $65bn.

Why OpenAI plateaued, then jumped

Consumer subscriptions were ~60% of OpenAI's mix entering 2026, and they grew slowly as ChatGPT's user growth decelerated (800m weekly users in Oct 2025 → 900m in Feb 2026 → >1bn in Aug 2026).11 The summer jump came from enterprise: GPT-5.6 was "54% more efficient on agentic coding," business customers grew 32% in July, and enterprise overtook consumer revenue for the first time E.11

Why Anthropic went vertical

Agentic coding came first: Claude Code, and Claude inside Cursor, GitHub Copilot and other tools. Cowork (knowledge-work agents) followed. Both are priced per token at high intensity, so revenue scales with tokens per developer per day. That went up by orders of magnitude once agents started running for hours E. The run-rate grew ~100× in 20 months, and ~10× in the 9 months of 2026 alone A.38

Quarterly revenue, not run-rate

Booked quarters tell the same story with less noise. In H1 2026 Anthropic's revenue went from ~$4.8bn (Q1) to $10.9–11.5bn (Q2), and it turned an operating profit of ~$559m. OpenAI's went from ~$5.7bn to ~$6.7bn, with a Q2 operating loss reported at ~$12.3bn E.2748

The three-chip strategy

Running on Nvidia GPUs, Google TPUs and Amazon Trainium is Anthropic's main structural difference from OpenAI. It gives Anthropic bargaining power on price and lets it put each workload (training or serving, each latency tier) on the cheapest silicon. The cost is engineering across three compiler stacks.

08 · Compute commitmentsWhom the labs owe: over a trillion dollars of compute

OpenAI and Anthropic, two private companies, have signed well over $1tn of multi-year compute contracts between them A.

OpenAI's contract values sum to ~$820bn (and its own spending plan is ~$600bn through 2030); Anthropic's dollar-denominated deals sum to ≥~$280bn, and plausibly $400bn+ once gigawatt-only deals are priced; The Information's tally of everything Anthropic signed in the 11 months to Sep 2026 is $517bn across 14.8 GW E.101 Oracle, CoreWeave, AMD, Broadcom and Nvidia backlogs are all partly lab backlogs. Across the five biggest cloud backlogs (~$2.46tn), roughly 43% (~$1.05tn) is owed by OpenAI and Anthropic A medium confidence (range 40–50%; see W01).

Why the headline numbers differ

"Commitments" mix three things. (1) Take-or-pay cloud contracts (Oracle, Azure, AWS, CoreWeave), which are real liabilities. (2) Non-binding gigawatt letters of intent (Nvidia 10 GW, Broadcom 10 GW, AMD 6 GW), which set a ceiling, not a liability. (3) Campuses financed by third parties (Stargate, Ohio). OpenAI's $1.4tn was a sum of gigawatt aspirations; the ~$600bn is closer to what it expects to actually pay through 2030 A; the FT's ~$856bn uses a broader "compute infrastructure" definition.2229

The systemic mismatch

These are multi-year, take-or-pay, gigawatt-scale contracts. The revenue that pays for them is usage-based and month to month. SpaceX's Anthropic contract can be ended on 90 days' notice by either side (per S-1 summaries, medium confidence), and its Google contract on 90 days' notice after 31 Dec 2026.19103 That mismatch is the single biggest systemic exposure in the layer (see W11).

Where the money in a token goes A

Illustrative serving economics for a frontier model on rented Blackwell-class GPUs low confidence:

  • Rent: ~$3–6 per GPU-hour (W08). A large mixture-of-experts model at interactive speed produces on the order of 1–5m tokens per GPU-hour, blended across input and output, depending on batch size, context length and reasoning share.
  • Cost ≈ $1–5 per million tokens blended. At a list price of $4/$20 (Opus 5.5), typical agentic traffic of ~85% input (much of it cached) and 15% output gives a blended realized price of ≈ $3–5 per million.

That is why Anthropic's multi-silicon approach and Google's TPUs matter, and why OpenAI's 2025 gross margin (~33%) was worse than 2024's (~40%): heavy free-tier usage, the Microsoft revenue share and rented Nvidia capacity.23

  • Capability keeps compounding. METR horizons double every ~4 months.76 Each doubling unlocks longer, more valuable tasks (hours → days of expert work), moving the market from software seats toward labor spend.
  • AGI timelines drive capex. Lab leaders expect transformative AI within a few years. Altman's UN remarks cite a model solving a Millennium Prize problem89 unverified. If that is even half right, today's compute commitments are too small.
  • IPOs unlock permanent capital. Anthropic (targeting Nov 2026, up to ~$100bn raised) and OpenAI (not before 2027) moving to public markets reduces refinancing risk for the whole stack.9897

Bear case

The math, the run-rates and the concentration

  • OpenAI's math still doesn't close. $278bn of negative free cash flow in 2026–30; the March raise exhausted by 2028; a 2025 operating loss of 160% of revenue; a Q2 2026 operating loss nearly 2× revenue. Each projection has been worse than the last.2123252648
  • Run-rate is not revenue. Usage-based run-rates can reverse fast. Anthropic's reported jump from $65bn to a >$100bn pace in ~2 months (press-reported, not company-confirmed) may include one-off agent bursts, pricing changes (Fable at $50 per million output tokens) and concentrated customers. Its biggest coding channel, Cursor, is now owned by a competitor.316
  • Concentration is systemic risk. The two labs have committed ≥~$1tn of compute A. Oracle, CoreWeave, AMD, Broadcom, Nvidia (credit support for the Ohio PORTS-Pike campus, capped at $105bn and contingent), SpaceX ($15bn a year from Anthropic, with a 90-day termination clause per the S-1, medium confidence) and part of Google Cloud's backlog depend on 2–3 private customers.1922115120
  • Circular financing flatters demand. ~40–60% of 2026 lab equity came from compute vendors A (§09): revenue those vendors partly paid for themselves.
  • Commoditization. Fixed-capability prices fall ≥10× a year;72 Chinese open weights are near the frontier;84 big tech can bundle "good enough" AI for free (Gemini in Android and Siri).70
  • Policy and legal risk. Pentagon vs Anthropic; copyright ($1.5bn settlement); Musk's appeal.3536 The Microsoft AGI clause was reportedly dropped in Apr 2026, which lowers one risk.

Our judgment A

The industry bull case, demand for tokens, looks more secure in Sep 2026 than at any point before. The company-specific risk has shifted: Anthropic now carries execution and valuation risk, OpenAI carries financing risk, and the big-tech labs carry little existential risk. The biggest systemic exposure is the mismatch between multi-year, take-or-pay gigawatt contracts and usage-based, month-to-month revenue.

19 · GlossaryTerms on this page

Agent
A model that runs multi-step tasks using tools (browser, terminal, APIs) with little human input.
AGI clause
A contract term (Microsoft–OpenAI) that changes rights and revenue share once "artificial general intelligence" is declared and verified by an expert panel. Reportedly dropped in Apr 2026.
ARR / run-rate
The latest month's (or week's) revenue × 12 (or × 52). Not contracted revenue for usage-based businesses.
Base model
The output of pre-training, before instruction tuning.
Chip-backed SPV
A special-purpose vehicle that raises debt and equity to buy AI chips and lease them to a lab; the chips are the collateral (e.g. Apollo and Blackstone's ~$35bn vehicle for Anthropic).
Cost of revenue (COGS)
For labs, mainly inference compute plus hosting, revenue share and payment fees.
Credit support (guarantee)
A promise to pay a borrower's or tenant's obligations if they cannot. A contingent liability, not cash (e.g. Nvidia's support, capped at $105bn, for OpenAI's PORTS-Pike campus).
Distillation
Training a smaller or cheaper model on the outputs of a stronger model.
Elo (LMArena)
A relative-preference rating derived from head-to-head human votes.
FLOP
Floating-point operation. Frontier training runs are ~10^26–10^27 FLOP.
Frontier model
Among the most capable models available at a point in time.
GW (gigawatt)
The unit labs now use for compute deals. 1 GW ≈ 400–500k Blackwell-class GPUs including facility overhead (rule of thumb, medium confidence).
HBM
High-bandwidth memory stacked next to accelerators. The key inference bottleneck.
Inference
Running a trained model to produce outputs for users.
METR time horizon
The length of task, in expert-human time, that a model completes with 50% (or 80%) reliability.
MoE (mixture of experts)
An architecture that activates only a subset of parameters per token (e.g., DeepSeek V4-Pro: 1.6T total, 49B active).
Open weights
Model parameters published for download. Not necessarily "open source": licenses vary.
PBC (public benefit corporation)
A for-profit corporate form with a stated public mission (OpenAI Group PBC, Anthropic PBC).
Post-training / RLHF / RLVR
Tuning after pre-training, using human feedback (RLHF) or verifiable rewards such as math and code graders (RLVR).
Pre-training
Next-token prediction on massive text, code and media corpora.
Prompt caching
Reusing already-processed input tokens at a fraction of the price (10% on most models; 5% on Opus 5.5; 2.5% on Fable/Mythos 5.1).
Reasoning model / test-time compute
A model that "thinks" (generates hidden tokens) before answering. Quality rises with the thinking budget.
Scaling laws
Empirical power-law relationships between compute, data, parameters and model loss.
Take-or-pay
A contract where the buyer pays for committed capacity whether or not it uses it.
Token
The unit of text a model processes, roughly ¾ of an English word.
TPU / Trainium
Google's and Amazon's custom AI accelerators.
Vendor (circular) financing
A supplier investing in its customer, which then spends the money on the supplier's products.
Warrant
The right to buy a company's shares at a set price. Akamai gave Anthropic warrants for up to ~5% of Akamai that vest as Anthropic buys Akamai services.
