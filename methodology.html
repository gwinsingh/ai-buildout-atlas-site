The 60-second version

  1. Primary sources first. Filings, earnings releases and calls, regulator data and company posts come first. Reputable press and industry research come second. Everything else is flagged.
  2. Every number is tagged R reported, G guidance, E third-party estimate or A our estimate. Each one carries a date or fiscal period, and a confidence tag where it is contested.
  3. The atlas was built in stages: ten analysts researched the layers in parallel, two independent fact-checkers checked 311 claims, a synthesis pass reconciled 36 disagreements into one set of canonical numbers, and HTML engineers built and tested the pages. An Edition-2 re-check on 25 Sep 2026, this time with working web access, re-verified W01–W05 (238 rows), filled thin spots in W03, W04, W06 and W07, and swept the news since mid-September.
  4. The fact-checks found one likely fabrication and a few dozen errors. A claim that NVIDIA would buy MediaTek for $110bn was removed; the real deal is a $3.5bn convertible-bond purchase. The re-check also reversed two Edition-1 "corrections" about Meta's Hyperion financing and confirmed most of 2026's extraordinary events. Across the three passes, 22 claims were contradicted and corrected.
  5. The China track had its own deep check. One independent pass checked 543 claims across C01–C10: 428 confirmed, 73 partial, 27 contradicted and corrected, 15 unverified and none likely fabricated. A consistency pass then carried 21 corrected facts into the other China dossiers. China figures stay in RMB and convert to US$ under one rule (§02).
  6. The biggest open number is Anthropic's run-rate. The last firm figure is >$65B at end-July. The New York Times reported a >$100B pace on 18 Sep. Anthropic has not confirmed it, so the atlas shows it as a press report (conf: med).
  7. The main weaknesses are known. W06–W10 were not re-checked row by row. Some filings (Nscale's S-1, SpaceX's S-1) were read only through press summaries. Private-company figures come from leaks and press reports, and the "AI-attributable" splits are our own judgment.

01 · Research standardWhat counts as evidence

The analyst's job is to let a reader answer five questions about any layer: who pays whom, how much, for what, at what margin, and what could break it.

Sources are ranked. When they disagree, the dossier shows the range and explains why the numbers differ rather than picking the convenient one. Important numbers are triangulated. For every company, the dossier says which fiscal period each figure comes from.

  1. TIER 1

    Primary filings and disclosures. 10-K, 10-Q and 20-F filings, earnings releases and call transcripts, investor-day decks, S-1s, regulator data (EIA, FERC, ISO/RTO queues and auctions), and official company blogs and press releases.

  2. TIER 2

    Credible reporting and research. Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, The Information, CNBC, SemiAnalysis, Epoch AI, IEA, LBNL, Dell'Oro, TrendForce, Synergy, IDC, Menlo Ventures, a16z, Sequoia.

  3. TIER 3

    Everything else, clearly flagged. Aggregators, encyclopedias, newsletters and headline-only listings. Where an entry was cited from prior knowledge or a search listing, the sources page marks it "not re-fetched".

Definition

Recency rule: always use the latest print. The default "as of" date is the research date (25 Sep 2026 for this edition). Calendar Q2 2026 results, reported in July and August 2026, are the latest full quarter for most companies.

02 · Provenance and confidenceHow every number is labelled

A provenance badge follows every key number. It tells you what kind of claim the number is, which is a different question from how reliable it is. A reported figure can come from a secondary source. An estimate can be very solid. Confidence tags cover the second question.

Currency: the China FX rule

China figures are reported in renminbi, and the China dossiers keep RMB as the primary unit. Every US-dollar figure next to them is a conversion under one rule, set in C11 and used by the China pages, the comparison (X00) and the reference pages:

Some layer dossiers converted at the rate of the period (C01–C05 used 6.71 for stocks and plans and 6.83 for June-quarter flows). The fact-check kept each dossier's local convention, and C11 re-converts every dollar figure it uses, so a US$ value can differ by 1–2% between a layer page and the synthesis. The RMB figure is the one to quote.

Why canonical numbers

Ten analysts working in parallel produced different values for the same metric. Western AI capex for 2026 came out at $0.95T, $1.075T and $0.9T, because each analyst drew the scope a little differently. The synthesis pass picked one value for each contested metric and explained why (W11 §9). The results are stored in data/western/_canonical.json, and every page must use them. 03 · ProductionHow this edition was produced

The atlas is written by Claude working in two roles (see CLAUDE.md). The Senior Analyst does primary-source research and quantifies everything. The HTML Engineer turns the research into visual pages. Large jobs are split across parallel agents, one layer each, and then pass through an independent fact-check and a synthesis. Edition 1 ran steps 1–5. The Edition-2 re-check (steps 6–7, 25 Sep 2026) went back over the Western track with working web access.

Feedback loop. Fact-check corrections go straight back into the dossiers and data/ files. The synthesis then applies the corrections that cut across layers (for example, Microsoft's calendar-2024 capex including leases was $75.6B, not $55.7B). Pages read their headline numbers from the canonical file, so a correction reaches every page that uses the number.

What the analysts were told

  • Use the latest data and never invent a number. If something cannot be verified, say so and mark it (conf: low).
  • Make the money explicit. Name the payer and the payee, the amount, what it buys, and the margin.
  • Present bull and bear cases with equal rigor. Don't add investment-advice disclaimers.
  • Use independent judgment. If something important is missing from the layer's scope, add it.
  • Don't work around blocked sites with scripts. Use another source instead.
  • Stay inside a search budget (Edition 2). Each re-check agent had its own WebSearch cap (22–45) and fetched primary pages directly where it could.

Three passes, three very different conditions

Pass 1 · W01–W05

No source could be fetched

Every fetch returned a proxy HTTP 429 (rate limit) for about 25 minutes of retries, and web search was already exhausted. So the verdicts rest on the checker's knowledge up to early 2026 and on re-run arithmetic and cross-dossier comparison. About half the confirmations are arithmetic.

The pass's value was in catching internal conflicts, such as fiscal versus calendar years, sums that don't add up and one metric stated three ways. It could not test events after about March 2026.

Pass 2 · W06–W10

About 60 sources fetched

The rate limit lifted after about 45 minutes, and the checker fetched company releases, SEC exhibits, company blogs, TrendForce, SEMI, Synergy, Menlo, Ramp and others. About 85 of its 107 confirmations rest on a fetched source.

It confirmed most of 2026's extraordinary claims: SpaceX absorbing xAI and its IPO, the $60B Cursor deal, Anthropic's $965B valuation and $65B run-rate, OpenAI's $852B round, Micron's 84.9% gross margin, and Nvidia's $89.0B data-center quarter.

Pass 3 · W01–W05 re-check · Edition 2, 25 Sep 2026

Working web access, primary sources first

The first pass could not fetch a single page, so Edition 2 re-ran it. Two checkers (FC1 for W01–W02, FC2 for W03–W05) used 34 searches and about 177 fetches: SEC EDGAR full-text search and XBRL pages, company releases and transcripts, regulators and grid operators. There were no rate-limit errors. They re-checked every TL;DR number, every Edition-1 row marked unverified, partial or contradicted, and every (conf: low, unverified) tag. The full log is factcheck-W01-W05-v2.md. Thin-spot research and the news sweep are in hardening-2026-09-25.md.

What the re-check found

Of 238 rows, 206 were confirmed, 19 partly right, 9 contradicted and 3 left open. One was judged likely fabricated. Every extraordinary 2026 event flagged in Edition 1 turned out to be real.

The claim was deleted from the dossier, the data and the page, and replaced with the real deal. It was never used in any W11 number.

06 · LimitationsKnown limitations

These are the ways the atlas could still be wrong after the Edition-2 re-check. They are listed in rough order of how much they could move the conclusions.

  1. The re-check was uneven across layers. In Edition 1 the web-search budget ran out partway through the research, and the first fact-check pass could not open any source. The Edition-2 re-check fixed that for W01–W05, which were re-verified row by row against fetched sources. W06–W10 rest on the second Edition-1 pass (about 60 sources fetched), plus targeted thin-spot research and a news sweep; they were not re-checked row by row. Some 2025-era facts still come from the analysts' prior knowledge of filings and are labelled "(prior knowledge)" or "not re-fetched" in the dossiers and on the sources page.
  2. Some filings were read only through summaries. Nscale's S-1 figures come from press write-ups, and SpaceX's compute contracts from summaries of its S-1. Bloomberg, Seeking Alpha, DigiTimes and some LightCounting pages were blocked, so a few figures rest on headlines or reprints. Each carries a confidence tag.
  3. Private-company financials come from leaks and press reports. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has a public S-1 yet. Anthropic is targeting a November 2026 IPO; Altman has said OpenAI will not list in 2026. OpenAI's 2025 figures ($13.07B revenue, −$20.9B operating loss) come from leaked audited statements that the FT confirmed. Lab run-rates come from Bloomberg, CNBC and the NYT (via Axios). These figures are tagged E and can move by billions when filings appear.
  4. "AI-attributable" is our judgment. No company reports "AI profit". The atlas attributes slices itself: TSMC ~35% of revenue, memory's AI-direct ~$149B against an AI-induced ~$390B windfall (conf: low), and ad uplift ~$50B, which assumes ~40% of Meta's post-2023 growth came from AI. The layer profit pools are right in order of magnitude, not precise.
  5. The legacy-cash figure is a residual. The ~$547B from Big Tech's existing businesses is whatever the money map needs to balance, so errors elsewhere land there. Its range is about $480–600B.
  6. Calendar-year conversions add error. Fiscal years are shifted to calendar years (Nvidia's FY27 ≈ CY2026 less a month). Growth is so fast that this adds about ±5% even to reported figures.
  7. Lab operating results extrapolate from one or two quarters of leaked data (OpenAI ~−$37B, Anthropic ~+$3.5B, xAI ~−$10B in 2026E).
  8. Scenario probabilities are judgment, not models (base 50%, bear 30%, bull 20%). The 2024 profit pools for W03, W04 and W07 are rough back-casts.
  9. China discloses less, and the China track leans harder on estimates. ByteDance, Huawei and most labs are private, the state's spending is inferred, and many figures reach the atlas through the Chinese financial press rather than filings. The China fact-check confirmed 79% of the claims it checked, but it could not check what was never published; see China's open items.
  10. "Western" is a convention. It means non-China. Nvidia sales to India and Southeast Asia count as Western. The effect is small.
  11. Some sources are weak by design. These include aggregators (StockTitan, BigGo, Investing.com summaries), Wikipedia, and title-only or headline-only listings. They are marked in the source lists and were deprioritized in the fact-checks.

What would change the story

If Anthropic's September run-rate proves to be ~$65–80B rather than the NYT-reported >$100B pace, lab revenue in 2026 falls by ~$4–8B (Anthropic toward ~$52–56B) and the exit-2026 run-rate of end-customer AI money falls to ~$255–265B A. The broad conclusions still hold: suppliers mint the profit, labs lose money, and revenue must roughly double to justify what has been built (W11 §16).

07 · MaintenanceHow to update the atlas

The atlas is built to be refreshed without rewriting it. Numbers that feed charts live in data/, the IDs never change, and history is added to rather than overwritten.

  1. Refresh the dossier in research/<track>/Wxx-*.md: change its as_of date, add new facts with tags and [n] citations, and append new sources to the numbered list without renumbering the old ones.
  2. Update the data in data/<track>/Wxx-*.json. If a headline number changes, update _canonical.json as well, so every page picks up the same value.
  3. Fact-check the changes. Log the verdicts in methodology/qa/ and list any correction that affects other layers, with an old → new propagation list. The Edition-2 logs, factcheck-W01-W05-v2.md and hardening-2026-09-25.md, show the format.
  4. Rebuild the pages. Update the layer page in docs/, register any new page in docs/assets/js/nav.js, then regenerate this site's source list with python3 tools/build_sources.py (use --check to see counts first) and the companies, glossary and timeline pages with python3 tools/build_reference_pages.py.
  5. QA each changed page with python3 tools/qa_pages.py <page>. It opens the page in light and dark themes at 1440px and 375px and reports console errors, sideways scroll, SVG labels that overlap or spill out of their chart, and charts that failed to render. It exits with an error if anything fails, so it can gate a commit. Then look at screenshots of what changed. The full checklist is in methodology/html-style-guide.md §9.
  6. Work in your own lane. Parallel chats follow the working-copy protocol in CLAUDE.md: pull a tarball of the Mac repo into a cloud working copy, touch only the paths your chat owns, give scratch files a unique prefix, and push back a tarball of only those paths through .transfer/, committing only them on the Mac. Never overwrite another chat's files.
  7. Record the change in CHANGELOG.md and ROADMAP.md, and add an entry to "What's new" on the home page. Don't silently overwrite history: add dated timeline entries instead.
