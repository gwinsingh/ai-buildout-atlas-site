The 60-second version

  1. Primary sources first. Filings, earnings releases and calls, regulator data and company posts come first. Reputable press and industry research come second. Everything else is flagged.
  2. Every number is tagged R reported, G guidance, E third-party estimate or A our estimate. Each one carries a date or fiscal period, and a confidence tag where it is contested.
  3. The atlas was built in stages: ten analysts researched the layers in parallel, two independent fact-checkers checked 311 claims, a synthesis pass reconciled 36 disagreements into one set of canonical numbers, and HTML engineers built and tested the pages.
  4. The fact-checks found errors but no fabrications. They corrected 13 contradicted claims and 36 partly wrong ones, confirmed most of 2026's extraordinary events, and left 29 rows unverified. The most important of those is a report that Anthropic's run-rate passed $100B.
  5. The main weaknesses are known. The web-search budget ran out partway through the research. Private-company figures come from leaks and press reports. The "AI-attributable" splits are our own judgment.

01 · Research standardWhat counts as evidence

The analyst's job is to let a reader answer five questions about any layer: who pays whom, how much, for what, at what margin, and what could break it.

Sources are ranked. When they disagree, the dossier shows the range and explains why the numbers differ rather than picking the convenient one. Important numbers are triangulated. For every company, the dossier says which fiscal period each figure comes from.

  1. TIER 1

    Primary filings and disclosures. 10-K, 10-Q and 20-F filings, earnings releases and call transcripts, investor-day decks, S-1s, regulator data (EIA, FERC, ISO/RTO queues and auctions), and official company blogs and press releases.

  2. TIER 2

    Credible reporting and research. Reuters, Bloomberg, FT, WSJ, The Information, CNBC, SemiAnalysis, Epoch AI, IEA, LBNL, Dell'Oro, TrendForce, Synergy, IDC, Menlo Ventures, a16z, Sequoia.

  3. TIER 3

    Everything else, clearly flagged. Aggregators, encyclopedias, newsletters and headline-only listings. Where an entry was cited from prior knowledge or a search listing, the sources page marks it "not re-fetched".

Definition

Recency rule: always use the latest print. The default "as of" date is the research date (25 Sep 2026 for this edition). Calendar Q2 2026 results, reported in July and August 2026, are the latest full quarter for most companies.

02 · Provenance and confidenceHow every number is labelled

A provenance badge follows every key number. It tells you what kind of claim the number is, which is a different question from how reliable it is. A reported figure can come from a secondary source. An estimate can be very solid. Confidence tags cover the second question.

Mixed tags such as R/A mean the atlas combined reported inputs with its own arithmetic. An example is the ~$2.46T contracted backlog, which adds up five companies' disclosed backlog figures.

Why canonical numbers

Ten analysts working in parallel produced different values for the same metric. Western AI capex for 2026 came out at $0.95T, $1.075T and $0.9T, because each analyst drew the scope a little differently. The synthesis pass picked one value for each contested metric and explained why (W11 §9). The results are stored in data/western/_canonical.json, and every page must use them. The full reconciliation table is below.

03 · ProductionHow this edition was produced

The atlas is written by Claude working in two roles (see CLAUDE.md). The Senior Analyst does primary-source research and quantifies everything. The HTML Engineer turns the research into visual pages. Large jobs are split across parallel agents, one layer each, and then pass through an independent fact-check and a synthesis.

Brief→Research ×10→Fact-check ×2→Synthesis→Pages & QA

  1. 1

    Brief & taxonomy

  2. 2

    Layer research

    10 analyst agents in parallel

    One dossier per layer, W01 to W10: 9,000–16,000 words each, with markdown tables and a numbered source list, plus a JSON file of chart-ready datasets.

    • 811 numbered sources
    • research/western/W01–W10
  3. 3

    Fact-check

    2 independent checkers

    Each checker took the highest-impact and most surprising claims, checked them against sources and arithmetic, fixed the dossiers and JSON in place, and logged every verdict.

    • 311 claim rows
    • methodology/qa/factcheck-W01-W05.md
    • methodology/qa/factcheck-W06-W10.md
  4. 4

    Synthesis

    Lead analyst

    Connects the layers: the money map, profit pools, circular financing, the revenue gap and scenarios. It reconciles disagreements and fixes one canonical value for each headline number.

    • 36 disagreements reconciled
    • W11-synthesis.md, W00-overview.md
    • data/western/_canonical.json
  5. 5

    Pages & QA

    HTML engineer agents in parallel

    One page per layer from a shared design system. Each is tested in light and dark themes at 1440px and 375px wide, with no console errors and no sideways scrolling.

    • docs/western/*.html
    • docs/assets/ (css, js, nav)
    • tools/build_sources.py

Feedback loop. Fact-check corrections go straight back into the dossiers and data/ files. The synthesis then applies the corrections that cut across layers (for example, Microsoft's calendar-2024 capex including leases was $75.6B, not $55.7B). Pages read their headline numbers from the canonical file, so a correction reaches every page that uses the number.

What the analysts were told

  • Use the latest data and never invent a number. If something cannot be verified, say so and mark it (conf: low).
  • Make the money explicit. Name the payer and the payee, the amount, what it buys, and the margin.
  • Present bull and bear cases with equal rigor. Don't add investment-advice disclaimers.
  • Use independent judgment. If something important is missing from the layer's scope, add it.
  • Don't work around blocked sites with scripts. Use another source instead.

Two passes, two very different conditions

Pass 1 · W01–W05

No source could be fetched

Every fetch returned a proxy HTTP 429 (rate limit) for about 25 minutes of retries, and web search was already exhausted. So the verdicts rest on the checker's knowledge up to early 2026 and on re-run arithmetic and cross-dossier comparison. About half the confirmations are arithmetic.

The pass's value was in catching internal conflicts, such as fiscal versus calendar years, sums that don't add up and one metric stated three ways. It could not test events after about March 2026.

Pass 2 · W06–W10

About 60 sources fetched

The rate limit lifted after about 45 minutes, and the checker fetched company releases, SEC exhibits, company blogs, TrendForce, SEMI, Synergy, Menlo, Ramp and others. About 85 of its 107 confirmations rest on a fetched source.

It confirmed most of 2026's extraordinary claims: SpaceX absorbing xAI and its IPO, the $60B Cursor deal, Anthropic's $965B valuation and $65B run-rate, OpenAI's $852B round, Micron's 84.9% gross margin, and Nvidia's $89.0B data-center quarter.

06 · LimitationsKnown limitations

These are the ways this edition could be wrong. They are listed in rough order of how much they could move the conclusions.

  1. The web-search budget ran out partway through the research. Several analyst sessions (W01, W02, W04, W06, W09 among them) hit the limit, and sites such as openai.com, Wikipedia, CNBC, Bloomberg and some IR pages were rate-limited or blocked. Some 2025-era facts therefore come from the analysts' prior knowledge of filings. They are labelled "(prior knowledge)" or "not re-fetched" in the dossiers and on the sources page.
  2. The first fact-check pass could not open any source. Its confirmations rest on knowledge up to early 2026 and on arithmetic. Events after about March 2026 in W01–W05 were only partly re-checked by the second pass.
  3. Private-company financials come from leaks and press reports. Neither OpenAI nor Anthropic has a public S-1. OpenAI's 2025 figures ($13.07B revenue, −$20.9B operating loss) come from leaked audited statements that the FT confirmed. Lab run-rates come from Reuters, Axios and The Information. These figures are tagged E and can move by billions when filings appear.
  4. "AI-attributable" is our judgment. No company reports "AI profit". The atlas attributes slices itself: TSMC ~35% of revenue, memory's AI-direct ~$149B against an AI-induced ~$390B windfall (conf: low), and ad uplift ~$50B, which assumes ~40% of Meta's post-2023 growth came from AI. The layer profit pools are right in order of magnitude, not precise.
  5. The legacy-cash figure is a residual. The ~$547B from Big Tech's existing businesses is whatever the money map needs to balance, so errors elsewhere land there. Its range is about $480–600B.
  6. Calendar-year conversions add error. Fiscal years are shifted to calendar years (Nvidia's FY27 ≈ CY2026 less a month). Growth is so fast that this adds about ±5% even to reported figures.
  7. Lab operating results extrapolate from one or two quarters of leaked data (OpenAI ~−$37B, Anthropic ~+$3.5B, xAI ~−$10B in 2026E).
  8. Scenario probabilities are judgment, not models (base 50%, bear 30%, bull 20%). The 2024 profit pools for W03, W04 and W07 are rough back-casts.
  9. "Western" is a convention. It means non-China. Nvidia sales to India and Southeast Asia count as Western. The effect is small.
  10. Some sources are weak by design. These include aggregators (StockTitan, BigGo, Investing.com summaries), Wikipedia, and title-only or headline-only listings. They are marked in the source lists and were deprioritized in the fact-checks.

What would change the story

If Anthropic's September run-rate turns out to be ~$65–80B rather than above $100B, lab revenue in 2026 falls by ~$4–7B and the exit-2026 run-rate of end-customer AI money falls to ~$255–265B A. The broad conclusions still hold: suppliers mint the profit, labs lose money, and revenue must roughly double to justify what has been built (W11 §16).

07 · MaintenanceHow to update the atlas

The atlas is built to be refreshed without rewriting it. Numbers that feed charts live in data/, the IDs never change, and history is added to rather than overwritten.

  1. Refresh the dossier in research/<track>/Wxx-*.md: change its as_of date, add new facts with tags and [n] citations, and append new sources to the numbered list without renumbering the old ones.
  2. Update the data in data/<track>/Wxx-*.json. If a headline number changes, update _canonical.json as well, so every page picks up the same value.
  3. Fact-check the changes. Log the verdicts in methodology/qa/ and list any correction that affects other layers.
  4. Rebuild the pages. Update the layer page in docs/, register any new page in docs/assets/js/nav.js, then regenerate this site's source list with python3 tools/build_sources.py (use --check to see counts first).
  5. QA each page in light and dark themes at 1440px and 375px wide, with no console errors and no sideways scroll. The checklist is in methodology/html-style-guide.md §9.
  6. Record the change in CHANGELOG.md and ROADMAP.md, and add an entry to "What's new" on the home page. Don't silently overwrite history: add dated timeline entries instead.

Natural refresh points

  • Quarterly earnings (late Oct to Nov 2026 for calendar Q3): hyperscaler capex guides, Nvidia Q3 FY27 (guided to $108B ±2%), cloud backlogs.
  • Lab filings: the Anthropic and OpenAI S-1s would replace leaked figures with reported ones.
  • Monthly indicators: lab run-rates, DRAM contract prices, H100 and GB200 rental prices, and TSMC monthly revenue.
  • New tracks: add research/china/ and docs/china/ with C-prefixed IDs. The sources generator picks up new track folders automatically.
