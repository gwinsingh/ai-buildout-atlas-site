The 60-second version

  1. Training and running AI models is mostly matrix multiplication, and specialised chips do it 10–100× more efficiently than ordinary processors.
  2. One company sells most of those chips, and because demand has exceeded supply since 2023 it keeps roughly 75¢ of every dollar as gross profit Sample.
  3. The biggest buyers are building their own chips (custom ASICs) to cut that bill. That is the main threat to the leader's margin.
  4. Supply, not demand, has been the binding limit: advanced packaging and HBM memory (W06) cap how many accelerators can ship.
  5. Watch hyperscaler capex guidance, custom-chip volumes and the leader's gross margin.

01 · What this layer isWhat a compute chip actually does

An AI model is a very large pile of numbers. Training it, or asking it a question, means multiplying those numbers together trillions of times a second.

A GPU is a chip with thousands of small calculators working in parallel, which suits that job. A custom ASIC is a chip designed for one customer's workload; it gives up flexibility to gain efficiency. Both need very fast memory stacked right next to them (HBM) and are bolted together in an advanced package (CoWoS).1

Analogy

A CPU is a brilliant chef who cooks one complex dish at a time. A GPU is a stadium kitchen with ten thousand line cooks, each only able to chop, but all chopping at once. AI is a mountain of chopping.

Why can't ordinary processors (CPUs) do this job?

They can, just slowly and at far higher energy cost. A CPU spends much of its silicon on making one stream of instructions fast. An accelerator spends it on raw arithmetic units and memory bandwidth, which is what neural networks consume.

In money terms: the same training run on CPUs would need so many more chips and so much more power that it would not pencil out.

Definition

Accelerator: any chip built mainly to speed up AI math. The umbrella term for GPUs, TPUs and other ASICs.

Why it matters

Compute silicon is where most AI capex lands and where most of the build-out's profit is booked today. If you want to know whether the whole stack is sustainable, start by asking whether this layer's margins are.

06 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

Demand keeps outrunning supply

  • Inference demand compounds as agents do longer tasks.
  • Software lock-in keeps pricing power through two more product cycles.
  • Custom chips stay concentrated in a few buyers' internal workloads.

Bear case

Margins revert as buyers build their own

  • Top buyers shift a third of spend to custom silicon by 2028.
  • A capex pause at two hyperscalers leaves an inventory overhang.
  • Power, not chips, becomes the binding limit, so chip scarcity pricing fades.

09 · GlossaryTerms on this page

GPU
Graphics processing unit. A chip with thousands of parallel arithmetic units; the workhorse of AI.
ASIC
Application-specific integrated circuit. A chip built for one job or one customer, such as Google's TPU.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory. DRAM chips stacked vertically and placed beside the processor to feed it data fast.
CoWoS
Chip-on-wafer-on-substrate. TSMC's advanced packaging that joins the processor and HBM on one base.
