The 60-second version

  1. Alibaba Cloud is accelerating, and AI is now a quarter of it. Revenue grew +45% to RMB 48.4bn in the June 2026 quarter, and AI-related products were RMB 12.4bn, the 12th straight quarter of triple-digit growth R 1. Growth has risen every quarter since mid-2024, from +6%.
  2. The market is about a tenth of the world's, and it is thin on profit. Mainland cloud infrastructure was ~US$52bn in 2025 E 17. Alibaba's segment margin is 11.6% against AWS's 39.4% R 173. The whole layer earns ~RMB 25–55bn (~US$4–8bn) a year A.
  3. Token volume has run far ahead of revenue. Doubao alone handles 180 trillion tokens a day R 22, yet Volcano Engine's whole MaaS business earned only ~RMB 1.5bn in 2025, about RMB 1.56 per million tokens E/A 26.
  4. The price war has reversed. After two years of cuts that set token prices near the cost of the GPU, 2026 brought increases: the industry-average API price rose +48% (input) and +80% (output) versus Q1-2025, and DeepSeek raised V4 Pro output +350% at peak E/R 5660. Scarce Nvidia-class compute, not market power, is the cause.
  5. The biggest buyers are the sellers themselves, and the state runs a parallel cloud. Alibaba, ByteDance and Tencent burn much of their own compute in free consumer apps, funded by commerce, ads and games. The SOE carriers booked ~RMB 290bn of company-defined cloud revenue in 2025 A, mostly government work.
  6. Export controls push clouds to domestic chips and offshore capacity. Only ~20k licensed H200s had arrived by Aug-2026 (~13% of quota) E 69, and ByteDance rents ~36k B200s in Malaysia E 70. Ascend 910B rents for a fifth of an H100.

01 · Explain like I'm newWho owns China's AI computers, and how they rent them out

In China, as in the West, a handful of companies buy AI chips, put them in data centres and rent the capacity out. What they sell, and who buys it, looks very different.

They sell compute three ways. Raw GPU time, by the card-hour (卡时) or the server-month, is called "compute leasing" (算力租赁). Managed cloud services (IaaS and PaaS) add storage, networking and tools. And MaaS (model-as-a-service) sells tokens (词元) from a hosted model, priced per million. Three features make the Chinese version different:

  • The biggest sellers are also the biggest buyers. Alibaba, ByteDance, Tencent and Baidu each run free consumer AI apps (Qwen app, Doubao, Yuanbao, ERNIE) that burn much of their own compute. A token sold to an outside developer and a token burned inside Doubao come from the same fleet.
  • The state runs a parallel cloud. The three state-owned telecom carriers each report RMB 50–120bn of "cloud" revenue, most of it government and SOE business (政务云). Local governments have also built hundreds of intelligent computing centres (智算中心) that rent capacity, often at low utilization (C04).
  • The hardware is constrained. US export controls cap access to Nvidia's best chips, so fleets mix stockpiled or gray-market Hopper parts, the China-only H20, newly licensed H200s and domestic accelerators (Huawei Ascend, Baidu Kunlunxin, Alibaba T-Head's Zhenwu, Cambricon). Some training runs are rented abroad, in Malaysia, Singapore and Thailand.

Analogy: a shopping mall that gives away the food court

Alibaba, ByteDance and Tencent are like mall owners. The cloud, with its rent-paying shops, is one income stream, but the mall mostly exists to bring shoppers to the owner's own department stores: Taobao, Douyin ads, WeChat. So the food court (tokens) is priced near cost, or given away, to fill the mall. The state-owned carriers are municipal markets: their stalls are full because government agencies are required to shop there, but that is not where the growth is. The compute-leasing firms are landlords of the car park, renting space by the month to whoever needs overflow capacity, including the mall owners themselves.

Why would a cloud give tokens away?

Because the token is the bait, not the catch. A developer who builds on Qwen or Doubao through Alibaba's Bailian or Volcano's Ark platform also pays for storage, databases and GPU hours on that cloud, and hands the model maker usage data. For the parent company, cheap AI also feeds the real profit engine: better ad targeting on Douyin, AI shopping agents on Taobao, smarter WeChat search.

That logic held while compute was cheap and plentiful. In 2026 the constraint flipped: Nvidia-class capacity became scarce, and giving tokens away started to cost real training runs. So prices went up.

Definition

Cloud layer (C08): companies in mainland China that own AI compute and rent it out, by the GPU-hour, the server-month or the token. That includes internet hyperscalers, Huawei Cloud, the three carrier clouds, the listed neocloud Kingsoft Cloud, the A-share compute lessors, MaaS token businesses and Chinese firms renting compute abroad. Capex totals are in C01, data-centre shells in C04, chips in C05, servers in C07, labs in C09 and apps in C10.

Why it matters

The cloud layer is where China's AI capex is supposed to earn a return. If tokens and GPU-hours are sold near cost, the return has to come from somewhere else: the parents' ads and commerce, or the state. Whether the 2026 price rises stick decides whether this layer becomes a profit engine or stays a subsidised utility.

Western hyperscalers make most of their AI money by renting GPU capacity to a few labs on long take-or-pay contracts (W08). Chinese hyperscalers mostly rent to themselves and to the long tail. There are no disclosed multi-year, multi-billion-dollar lab contracts of the OpenAI–Oracle kind. The nearest equivalents are the A-share lessors' 3–5 year server leases to Tencent and ByteDance E 54 and ByteDance's offshore Blackwell lease E 70. No Chinese cloud discloses an AI backlog or RPO figure A.

The units: card-hours, server-months and tokens

China's rental market quotes three units, and converting between them is the fastest way to understand the economics. A card-hour (卡时) is one accelerator for one hour; spot indices (SMM, Mysteel) quote RMB per card-hour E 49. A server-month is the usual contract unit for an 8-card server. One-year contracts carry the highest price, three-year contracts are slightly lower, and five-year "closed" (闭口) contracts are cheapest; H200 PCIe servers have gone below RMB 100k per server-month on five-year terms E 4950. Tokens (词元, the official Chinese term since March 2026 66) are priced per million, with separate input and output prices, cache-hit discounts and, since DeepSeek started it in Feb-2025, peak and off-peak rates R 5860.

How a Chinese cloud makes money on a GPU, and why it often doesn't

The P&L is the same as in the West: revenue minus power, colocation and operations gives cash gross profit; minus depreciation gives gross profit; minus SG&A and R&D gives operating income. Six things differ:

  1. The price per GPU-hour is lower. An H100 rents for about RMB 11.6–14.3 per card-hour (US$1.73–2.14) in China, versus a US$3.39 median on-demand price in the US E 495073.
  2. Capex per GPU is often higher. Controlled parts arrive through licensed-but-taxed channels (the 25% H200 revenue share 68) or gray markets. An 8×H200 server cost RMB 3.5m (~US$522k at 6.70, ~US$65k per GPU) in May-2026, up from RMB 2.1–2.3m in Sep-2025 E 50, roughly double the likely US price (conf: med).
  3. Power and buildings are cheaper. Western-hub data-centre power costs RMB 0.20–0.35/kWh (3.0–5.2 US¢) on the Atlas canonical figure (C03); this dossier's opex model uses a more conservative RMB 0.3–0.5/kWh. State land and subsidies help too (C04).
  4. Domestic accelerators rent at a steep discount. Ascend 910B rents for about RMB 2.4–3.4 per card-hour, roughly 20–25% of an H100 E 49. The discount reflects lower throughput, the CANN software gap and a glut of state-built 910B capacity A (C05).
  5. The token layer is cross-subsidised. Consumer chat apps are free (C10). Enterprise tokens were priced at or below cost through 2025 to win share; Volcano priced at 20–30% of Alibaba's and Baidu's levels E 25.
  6. Depreciation lives are similar, financing is not. Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu depreciate servers over ~3–5 years (US Big-4: 5–6) A (conf: med). The A-share lessors finance servers with ~5-year financing leases, such as a 63-month lease at Xiechuang Data E 54: the same "financing business dressed as tech" pattern as CoreWeave.

03 · Market size & growthA tenth of the world's cloud market, re-accelerating on AI

China's cloud market had slowed to single digits in 2023–24. AI has pushed it back above 20% growth, but it is still about a tenth the size of the global market and growing more slowly than the West's.

Mainland cloud infrastructure services (Omdia)

Omdia's "cloud infrastructure services" is the closest match to Synergy's global IaaS + PaaS measure used in W08. It excludes most carrier project revenue, hardware sales and SaaS. Spending reached US$14.7bn in Q4-2025 (+26%) E 17, and the four 2025 quarters sum to ~US$52bn (+22%) A. Growth accelerated for four straight quarters, 16% → 21% → 24% → 26% 14151617, which Omdia attributes to AI and agent workloads. Omdia forecasts +26% for 2026, which gives ~US$66bn A. Resellers and integrators drive about 25% of the market E 1417.

Volcano Engine is not named in Omdia's top-3 releases. DCD cites IDC at ~13% for Volcano in 2025 E 18, roughly level with or above Tencent. China versus the world A: US$52bn is about 11% of the ~US$470–500bn global total (Synergy trailing twelve months at Q2-2026) 73. China grows ~26%; the global market grew +43% in Q2-2026. In the West, AI is accelerating the whole market; in China it is lifting a market that had slowed badly (Alibaba Cloud grew only +3–7% from 2023 to mid-2024) R 45.

The "AI cloud" market: small, but nearly tripling

Omdia also measures an "AI cloud" market: AI IaaS plus AI PaaS plus MaaS. It was RMB 56.7bn (~US$7.9bn at 7.18) in 2025, +172%, of which IaaS was RMB 39.2bn and MaaS RMB 17.5bn E 8. That beat Omdia's own Sep-2025 forecast of RMB 51.8bn by ~9% A 9. Alibaba held 38.1% (~RMB 21.6bn), up from 35.8% in H1; Volcano Engine, Baidu AI Cloud, Tencent Cloud and China Telecom ranked #2–#5. Alibaba also led the AI-IaaS sub-market (38.6%) and model-production services (MPS, 42.3%) E 8.

Omdia expects inference to rise from 55% to more than 80% of AI-cloud workloads by 2027, and model-consumption services (MCS, inference APIs) to grow at a 72% CAGR to 2030 E 8.

Reality check

The whole Chinese AI-cloud market in 2025 (RMB 56.7bn, ~US$7.9bn) was smaller than one quarter of Google Cloud revenue (US$24.8bn in Q2-2026) and smaller than Microsoft's AI run-rate (>US$37bn) R 73.

Public-cloud IaaS (IDC): the carriers appear

IDC measured China's public-cloud IaaS at RMB 120.7bn (~US$16.8bn at 7.18) in H1-2025, about +20% E 2021. Shares: Alibaba 26.8%, Huawei 12.9%, Tianyi Cloud 12.3%, Mobile Cloud 9.4%, Tencent 7.9%, with Volcano Engine showing "leap-frog growth". Of the RMB 72.8bn compute-resources segment, the "intelligent computing pool" was RMB 14.6bn (+122%, about one-fifth); GenAI IaaS, a separate cut, was RMB 16.7bn (+219%). IDC's view puts the carriers #3 and #4, unlike Omdia's, because it counts carrier public-cloud IaaS sold to government and SOE customers A.

Who is #1? It depends on the ruler

Every vendor picks the ranking that flatters it. Alibaba leads on revenue; Volcano Engine leads on tokens; Baidu leads on government model tenders; and on company-reported "cloud" revenue, two state carriers sit right behind Alibaba. There is no single audited "China cloud market" A.

Data-reliability flag

Omdia, IDC, Canalys and Frost & Sullivan use different perimeters. Companies, especially the carriers, report "cloud" with their own definitions, and several numbers circulate only through vendor-sponsored press. This page uses Omdia for revenue and IDC for token volume and treats any single "#1" claim with care.

Tokens: the headline growth metric of Chinese cloud

IDC counted 1,944 trillion public-cloud MaaS token calls in 2025, up 16× on 2024's 114 trillion; Volcano Engine handled 49.5% (46.4% in 2024) E 1213. IDC projects ~40,000 trillion in 2026, another ~20×. More than 140 enterprises had used over 1 trillion tokens on Volcano by early 2026, and 200 by Jun-2026, up from 100 in Dec-2025 E 1227.

What the token numbers do and don't mean. IDC counts external API calls only. Company-reported "daily tokens" include the parents' own consumer apps, and they are far larger. Doubao went from about 0.12 trillion tokens a day at launch (May-2024) to 180 trillion a day in Jun-2026, more than 1,500× R 22. China as a whole passed 140 trillion a day in March 2026, per the National Data Administration R via E 2466.

Other signals: TRAE, ByteDance's coding agent, used 5.6 trillion tokens a day in Jun-2026 (50× y/y) E 27, and DeepSeek's V4-Flash processed 8 trillion tokens in one day on one platform, OpenCode, 5 trillion of them free E 59.

Key insight: volume has run far ahead of revenue

China's token volume rose roughly 1,500× in about two years while prices barely moved until 2026. That is the core fact of this layer. Doubao's 180T a day is ~5.7× Google's API volume, but Volcano Engine's MaaS revenue for all of 2025 was about RMB 1.5bn (~US$0.2bn) E 26.

Alibaba Cloud: the scaled, profitable incumbent

Alibaba's cloud has re-accelerated every quarter since mid-2024: +6% → +7% → +13% → +18% → +26% → +34% → +36% → +38% → +45% R 12345. The June 2026 quarter was the first under a new segment, "AI Cloud and Compute Services", which adds the T-Head chip unit to Cloud Intelligence. Revenue was RMB 48.4bn (US$7.14bn at 6.7851), +45%, with external revenue also +45%, and adjusted EBITA was RMB 5.63bn (11.6% margin), +133% R 16.

  • AI share. AI-related product revenue was RMB 12.4bn, ~26% of the segment (~22% in Mar-26), the 12th consecutive quarter of triple-digit growth R 1. The +38% QoQ jump partly reflects the new perimeter: T-Head's Zhenwu chips are sold "via Alibaba Cloud services" to >650 external customers. Annualized, AI revenue is ~RMB 50bn (~US$7.4bn at 6.70) A.
  • Scale. FY2026 (to Mar-2026) revenue was RMB 158.1bn (+34%), ~US$22.0bn at 7.18, with adjusted EBITA of ~RMB 14.3bn (9.0%) R 2. The June-quarter run-rate is RMB 194bn (~US$28.9bn), about AWS's size in 2017 and one-sixth of AWS today (US$169bn) A 73.
  • MaaS. Alibaba runs a "MaaS strategy"; Model Studio (Bailian) customers grew 8× by Mar-2026 R 2. MaaS token revenue grew 15× in Jan–May 2026, and MaaS-and-application-services ARR was targeted at >RMB 10bn by Q2 and >RMB 30bn by end-2026 E 78.
  • Capex and cash. Capex was RMB 67.7bn in the June quarter (US$10.0bn at 6.7851, +75%), more than the segment's revenue. Group free cash flow was −RMB 44.7bn and group adjusted EBITA fell 30% as quick commerce and AI spending weighed R 1. The RMB 380bn plan is in C01.
  • Footprint. 92 availability zones in 29 regions (Dec-2025) 3.   • Pricing. Alibaba led the general-cloud price cuts of 2023–24 (up to ~50–55% on core products, conf: med) and the May-2024 token cut (Qwen-Long −97%) 55.   • Moat and weak points. The largest share of every revenue ranking, a full stack from chip (T-Head) to model (Qwen, >1bn Hugging Face downloads) to apps, and six years as financial-cloud leader 3. Volcano Engine (ByteDance): the share-taker

All Volcano numbers are press estimates. Revenue was about RMB 12bn in 2024, with a 2025 target of >RMB 25bn E 25 and an estimated 2025 actual of RMB 24–25bn 30 (conf: med): about 15% of Alibaba Cloud by revenue, but #1 by tokens. Takeaway

The "~30% rental margin" quoted by Chinese lessors 50 holds for fleets bought before the 2026 price spike. At today's H200 purchase prices (roughly double US levels because of the licence regime, the 25% fee and gray-market mark-ups), rent would need ~RMB 111k a month for a 10% IRR, about 40% above current contracts. The token price war, and its reversal

May 2024 started the war. DeepSeek V2 launched at ~RMB 1 per million tokens; ByteDance priced Doubao at RMB 0.0008 per 1k tokens (RMB 0.8/M); Alibaba cut Qwen-Long 97% to RMB 0.5/M; Baidu and Tencent made their lite models free R 55. Prices stayed near the cost of the GPU through 2025.

2026 reversed it. Zhipu raised prices three times and Tencent twice. Alibaba Cloud raised T-Head Zhenwu 810E and related compute-card services by 5–34% (CPFS +30%) from 18 Apr 2026, and Baidu raised AI compute by 5–30% E 5556. The industry-average API price for eight major models reached RMB 4.9/M input and RMB 21.9/M output in Q2-2026, +48% and +80% versus Q1-2025 E 56 (the compiler is not named; conf: low-med). DeepSeek raised V4 Pro output from a flat RMB 6/M to RMB 27/M at peak (+350%) and RMB 13.5/M off-peak, announced in Aug-2026 (effective date reported as 17 Aug or 10 Sep) R 605859. The 2025–26 campaign against "disorderly low-price competition" (anti-involution) gives political cover (conf: med).

The West moved the other way: OpenAI cut GPT-5.6 Sol API prices by more than 20% on 21 Aug 2026 (a promotion reported to run to 21 Nov) E 56. Chinese list prices remain far below US frontier pricing: Qwen3.8-Max output at RMB 36/M is ~US$5.4/M at 6.70 A.

Does a token pay for its GPU?

DeepSeek published the economics of its V3/R1 serving fleet for 24 hours on 27–28 Feb 2025 R 62: an average of 226.75 nodes × 8 H800 = 1,814 GPUs, costed at US$2 per GPU-hour (US$87,072 a day), served 608bn input tokens (56.3% cache hits) and 168bn output tokens. Its theoretical revenue was US$562k a day, a "545% cost-profit margin", though actual revenue was much lower (free app, discounts, night pricing). Volcano's realized price A: 2025 MaaS revenue of ~RMB 1.5bn 26 over 49.5% × 1,944T ≈ 962T tokens 12 gives ~RMB 1.56 per million tokens (~US$0.22). That blends cheap text with pricier Seedance video, so the text-only price is probably lower (conf: low).

  2. 2026 prices lift the multiple to ~1.6–3.2× (V4.1-Flash off-peak to peak), the first time Chinese MaaS plausibly covers training amortization. With ~20k H-class GPUs shared between training and inference 59, DeepSeek was forced by scarcity, not strategy.
  3. Less efficient serving loses money at these prices: non-MoE models, domestic chips with lower throughput, small batches. Scale buys batch efficiency and cache hits, which is why Volcano and Alibaba lead.

Why domestic accelerators rent so cheaply

A 910B card-hour at RMB ~2.4–3.4 49 is ~20–25% of an H100's price, while its nominal FP16 throughput is often cited at 50–80% of an H100 (conf: low; C05). The gap reflects software (CANN / MindSpore porting costs, weaker kernels, lower real utilization), oversupply in state-funded centres built for policy goals rather than demand 67, and reliability and cluster-scale limits outside Huawei's own CloudMatrix systems A. Huawei's answer is to sell tokens, not cards: its CloudMatrix384 Token Service hides the hardware 40, and Kunlunxin and T-Head do the same inside Baidu and Alibaba. The domestic-chip business model is vertical integration: chip + cloud + model + tokens.

10 · Profit captureThe whole layer earns what AWS earns in about a month

Our order-of-magnitude estimate for the cloud layer's 2026 operating profit is RMB 25–55bn (~US$4–8bn) A (conf: low). Alibaba earns about half of it; Volcano Engine probably loses money.

For scale: AWS alone earned US$16.6bn of operating income in Q2-2026, about US$66bn annualized R 73. The whole Chinese cloud layer's ~US$4–8bn a year is roughly 6–12% of AWS's annualized operating income, or a quarter to a half of one AWS quarter A. In the C11 synthesis, the cloud layer's AI-attributable slice is only ~5% of China's ~US$17bn positive AI operating-profit pool in 2026E; memory makers (C06) take ~33% A.

Where China's AI cloud profit actually sits

  1. Chip suppliers with scarcity pricing: domestic accelerator vendors, and Nvidia via the H200 licence, whose 25% fee flows to the US Treasury (C05).
  2. Owners of already-depreciated or cheaply bought Nvidia fleets: the lessors, and the hyperscalers' 2023–25 stockpiles.
  3. The parents' core businesses: ads, commerce and games, where AI raises monetization (C10). For ByteDance, Alibaba and Tencent, most of the return on AI cloud capex is booked outside the cloud segment.

Why margins are structurally lower than in the West

  1. No take-or-pay backlog. Chinese clouds have nothing like the West's ~US$2.46T of contracted backlog 73. Revenue is short-tenor and price-sensitive.
  2. State-funded competitors. Carriers and local-government centres compete on price, with capacity built for policy reasons.
  3. Open-weight models commoditize MaaS. DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi and GLM weights are free, so any cloud or on-premise appliance can serve them. Pricing power sits with serving efficiency, not model ownership.
  4. Customer culture. Chinese enterprises prefer on-premise and private deployments and have low willingness to pay for software (C10).
  5. The capital cost of controlled hardware. H200-class servers cost ~2× in China 50, yet rents are 35–50% lower.
  6. Scale. Alibaba's cloud is ~1/6 of AWS by revenue, so fixed R&D is spread thinner.

Offsetting advantages: cheaper power and land (C03, C04), cheaper engineering talent, and domestic silicon whose margin stays in-house (T-Head, Kunlunxin, Huawei).

12 · Competitive dynamicsVolume against value, open weights against margins

  1. Volcano vs Alibaba: volume vs value. Volcano owns half the external token market with cheap pricing and ByteDance's ad budget; Alibaba owns revenue leadership and the enterprise base. The 2026 price rises favour Alibaba (higher-value enterprise mix, own chips). Volcano is responding with premium models (Seed 2.1 Pro) and Seedance video 2657. Watch whether Volcano's revenue share converges toward its token share (49.5% of tokens vs ~15% of AI-cloud revenue in H1-2025).
  2. Open weights commoditize MaaS. Every Chinese cloud hosts DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM and Kimi; Tencent Cloud hosts GLM-5 and MiniMax 55, and Kunlunxin supports Kimi K3 and GLM 5.2 32. Differentiation shifts to serving efficiency, caching and agent tooling (coding plans, Qoder, TRAE, CodeBuddy).
  3. AI coding is the monetizable use case. Alibaba's Qoder holds "47.6% of revenue share" in AI programming (per a 36Kr-cited ranking, conf: low) 7; TRAE burns 5.6T tokens a day 27. Coding subscriptions are the first Chinese token product with Western-like willingness to pay.
  4. Vertical integration of domestic silicon. T-Head Zhenwu (>650 external customers) 1, Kunlunxin (spin-off pending) 32 and Huawei CloudMatrix 40 turn chips into cloud products. If domestic chips reach parity on inference, clouds with their own silicon get a cost edge, as AWS Trainium and Google TPU do.
  5. Carriers as the state's compute utility. Smart-computing revenue grows 95–130% 4243, and the state steers government AI projects to SOEs. They set prices at the low end.
  6. Consolidation of lessors. The top five are projected at 60%+ share 54; weaker players face "signed-vs-settled" gaps 53.
  7. Offshore capacity as a parallel cloud. Aolani (Malaysia) and Southeast Asian operators serve Chinese labs 7072; Alibaba adds regions in Malaysia, UAE and Europe 64. A US remote-access rule would disrupt this.
  8. Foreign hyperscalers are irrelevant in mainland AI. AWS and Azure operate only via local partners, and none appears in Omdia's top three.
  9. Disruptors to watch: DeepSeek as de facto price-setter (its Aug-2026 increase legitimized industry-wide hikes) 58; Kimi / Moonshot and MiniMax selling APIs directly (C09); and state "token vouchers" that can subsidize any vendor 66.

13 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

China cloud becomes the next profit engine

  • Acceleration is real and broad: Alibaba +45%, Baidu AI Infra +50% (GPU cloud +283%), Tencent cloud low-20s, Kingsoft +31%, and Omdia's market accelerating for four straight quarters to +26% 117313547.
  • Pricing power is back: list compute prices +5–34%, API prices +48–80% vs Q1-2025, DeepSeek +350% at peak. At 2026 prices, efficient MaaS covers GPU cost ~1.6–3.2× A.
  • The token base is enormous: 16× in 2025, 20× expected in 2026. At RMB 1.5/M, 40,000T tokens = RMB 60bn A.
  • Alibaba's margin is inflecting (9% → 11.6%), MaaS ARR is targeted above RMB 30bn by end-2026, and analysts read the 20 GW plan as >RMB 1 trillion of external revenue by 2032 76365.
  • Domestic silicon lowers capex per token over time, and the chip margin stays in-house (Zhenwu, Kunlunxin, Ascend).
  • Cheap power and state-built shells keep operating costs below US levels.

Bear case

A volume business without profits

  • Operating profit is tiny: ~US$4–8bn for the whole layer A. Alibaba's 11.6% is a third of AWS's; Volcano, the share leader, probably loses money.
  • Price rises reflect scarcity, not market power. When H200s, domestic 950-class chips and more capacity arrive, the price war may resume. Open weights make MaaS a commodity.
  • Capex is outrunning revenue: Alibaba's quarterly capex (RMB 67.7bn) exceeds its cloud revenue (RMB 48.4bn); group FCF is negative at Alibaba and Tencent 135. New Nvidia capacity at 2026 prices has a ~−3% IRR A.
  • Much of the "cloud" is state-driven: carriers growing 5–8%, idle local centres (<30–37% utilization), voucher-funded demand 6667.
  • Geopolitical tail risks: the US could close the remote-access loophole or revoke H200 licences; Beijing could tighten domestic-chip mandates; overseas regions face pushback.
  • Much token volume is captive or free (Doubao, Qwen app, free quotas). Realized revenue is ~RMB 1.6/M (~US$0.22) A.

What is structurally different

  1. Captive vs merchant compute. Western clouds rent to independent labs on multi-year contracts. Chinese clouds mostly serve their parents' own apps and a long tail, with no disclosed backlog.
  2. Tokens, not GPU-hours, are the battleground. China's price war was fought per million tokens, with open-weight models, financed by ads and commerce.
  3. The state is a market participant. SOE carriers run the second-largest "cloud" business by revenue, local governments build and subsidise capacity, and the NDRC decides which Nvidia chips enter.
  4. Hardware scarcity is policy-made. Export controls plus Beijing's own steering keep Nvidia supply tight: a seller's market for Nvidia capacity, a buyer's market for domestic capacity.
  5. Margins are a third of the West's. The layer is profitable only at Alibaba and, modestly, at Tencent and Baidu; much of the return appears in ads and commerce instead.
  6. Offshore compute is a feature. Chinese firms train on Nvidia Blackwell in Malaysia and Singapore, which has no Western analogue.
  7. Pricing runs opposite to the West. In 2026 Chinese token prices rose while US API prices fell, because Chinese scarcity is binding and the price war had pushed prices below sustainable levels.
