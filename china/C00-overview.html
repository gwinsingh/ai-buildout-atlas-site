China's AI build‑out, explained

China is building its own AI factory, mostly with its own chips, under US export controls: about RMB 0.9tn of AI data-centre capex in 2026, steered by the state, paid for beyond the platforms' cash flow, and earning almost no profit. This page shows the ten layers, where the yuan come from, who keeps what, and how the story ran from the ChatGPT shock to Xi Jinping's September 2026 visit to Washington.

The 60-second version

  1. China will spend ~RMB 0.9tn (~US$134bn) on AI data centres in 2026 A, up from ~RMB 380bn (~US$53bn) in 2024 and ~RMB 560bn (~US$78bn) in 2025.11 The West spends ~US$1.0T, about 7.5× as much.21 Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance and Baidu account for ~RMB 630bn (~US$94bn) A.1
  2. The platforms are spending beyond their cash. In Q2 2026 the listed three spent RMB 131.9bn (~US$19.7bn), ~167% (US Big-4: 96%) of operating cash flow, and free cash flow was −RMB 66.8bn (~−US$10bn) R.1 Alibaba sold HK$80bn (~US$10.3bn) of shares; ByteDance borrowed US$29.6bn at SOFR+68bp R.2
  3. The state builds, not only regulates. It directs ~19% (~RMB 170bn) of AI capex, funds ~15% of the money, and filters the rest: foreign accelerators are banned in state-funded data centres and every Nvidia H200 purchase needs NDRC approval A.11
  4. Chips are the constraint; power is the advantage. China added ~550 GW (US: 53 GW) of generating capacity in 2025 R.3 Domestic vendors now hold ~80% by value (Huawei ~50%; Nvidia ~8%) of the AI-chip market E,5 and HBM memory is the tightest input.6
  5. Usage is enormous; revenue is small. China processes >140tn/day (~97bn/min) tokens R, but end-customer AI money is only ~RMB 157bn (~US$23bn) in 2026E, an eighth of the West's A, and Alibaba and Tencent lose ~RMB 97bn/yr (~US$14.5bn) on consumer AI.1011
  6. Almost nobody earns the profit. China-domiciled firms earn ~US$17bn (West ~US$637bn) of positive AI operating profit, ~US$0bn once lab and consumer-AI losses are netted A. The largest slice goes to memory makers. Of each capex yuan, ~10 fen becomes supplier profit and ~21% (~RMB 185bn) is paid abroad.11
  7. The big question is monetization. A normal return on what is built needs ~RMB 475bn/yr (~US$71bn) of end-customer revenue, a gap of ~2.3× (West ~2.1×; ~1.8× ex-state capex) against today's run-rate. It is a platform cash-flow and local-government problem, not a lender problem A.11

01 · Start hereA state railway, a walled factory and a free food court

The Western overview (W00) pictures the West's AI build-out as a power grid, a factory and a retail chain. China has the same three floors, built differently.

A state railway (the bottom). The central government draws the map. "East Data, West Compute" (东数西算), launched in February 2022, sets 8 hub nodes and 10 data-centre clusters so that computing moves to cheap western power R.1 State grids and state generators supply the electrons (C03); carriers, local governments and landlords pour the concrete (C04). As on China's high-speed rail around 2012, the trunk lines are sold out while some branch lines were built ahead of traffic: national rack occupancy is 71.4% (Jun 2026) R,29 but AI centres used only 36.8% (1.4bn of 3.8bn card-hours) of their accelerator time in 2025 E.449

A walled factory (the middle). The factory makes tokens but may not import the best machines. Since October 2022 the US has banned Nvidia's leading chips, and since December 2024 the HBM memory they need. So China builds its own: Huawei's Ascend, Cambricon, Hygon and the platforms' in-house chips (C05), printed by SMIC on DUV-only "5nm-class" processes with memory from CXMT (C06). Weaker chips are tied together in bigger, more optical "supernodes" (C07). Against an Nvidia GB200 NVL72 rack, Huawei's CloudMatrix 384 is ~2.4× less power-efficient (0.52 vs 1.24 PF/kW); ~1.6× the BF16 compute A:5 more muscle, bought with far more electricity. The factory owners are the clouds (C08).

A free food court (the top). The designers are the model labs (C09), most of which publish their weights. The storefronts are the apps (C10). Here China differs most. The Western storefront is a paid software shop; China's is a food court run by the mall owners. The food (AI answers) is free, the owners even pay you to come in (the 2026 Spring Festival "red-envelope war" cost ~RMB 9bn E),1070 and they earn from rents (advertising) and a cut of what you buy (agentic commerce).

Alongside the stack sit the order book (C01 Demand & capex) and the bank (C02 Capital & financing): platform cash, state funds, policy banks, cheap loans and a retail-driven IPO boom.

Analogy

Picture a mall whose owners build a new wing, the power plant and the kitchen before the diners arrive, give the food away, pay shoppers to walk in, and are not allowed to buy the best ovens abroad, so they build their own and run more of them. That is China's AI build-out in 2026.

The twist. China's build runs ahead of customers and of the chips it needs. The state steers money into a chip industry that cannot yet make enough chips, while the platforms subsidise consumers to use AI they do not yet pay for A.11 As in the West, most of the money enters in the middle of the stack, not at the top; unlike the West, a large part of it is state money.

Explain like I'm new: what exactly is China "building out"?

Physical things. Warehouse-sized "intelligent computing centres" (智算中心) full of racks of AI accelerators, the power lines and substations that feed them, and the factories that make the chips and memory. A gigawatt of AI capacity in China costs ~RMB 160bn/GW (~US$24bn; US US$50-60bn) all-in E,4 far less than in the US, because the chips and buildings are cheaper.

"Capex" (capital expenditure) is money spent on long-lasting assets. It is written off (depreciated) over years: ~3-5 years (US Big-4: 5-6) for Chinese servers A,8 20+ years for buildings. China's AI depreciation already reaches ~RMB 220bn (~US$33bn) in 2026E A.11 That bill arrives whether or not customers pay.

Definition

End-customer AI money: what households, companies, government, advertisers and overseas developers actually pay for AI, net of what AI companies pay each other. In China it is ~RMB 157bn (~US$23bn) in 2026E: direct gen-AI spend ~RMB 82bn, enterprise AI cloud ~RMB 30bn and AI ad uplift ~RMB 45bn A.1011

Read the profit column top to bottom. 04 · The spending~RMB 0.9tn a year, paid for beyond cash flow

China's AI capex has more than doubled in two years and is on track to double again by 2030 in our base case. It is still about an eighth of the West's.

We estimate China will spend ~RMB 0.9tn (~US$134bn) on AI-related data centres in 2026 (range RMB 0.8–1.0tn; high case ~RMB 1.1tn (~US$164bn) if ByteDance spends at Bloomberg's upper bound) A.11 That compares with ~RMB 560bn (~US$78bn) in 2025 and ~RMB 380bn (~US$53bn) in 2024. About ~RMB 110bn of it is general-purpose kit bought alongside, so "pure" AI capex is ~RMB 0.75-0.8tn (~US$110-120bn) A. The four platforms spend ~RMB 630bn (~US$94bn); the three state carriers add ≥RMB 93.5bn (~US$14bn) G/A; local-government and SOE computing centres plus third-party landlords add ~RMB 95bn A.1

The strain on the buyers

In Q2 2026 Alibaba, Tencent and Baidu spent RMB 131.9bn (~US$19.7bn) of capex R, about ~167% (US Big-4: 96%) of their operating cash flow A. Their combined free cash flow was −RMB 66.8bn (~−US$10bn) R.1222426 Three days after its results, Alibaba sold HK$80bn (~US$10.3bn) of new shares R;38 on 3 September ByteDance signed a US$29.6bn at SOFR+68bp syndicated loan R.39 Tencent's negative cash flow came almost entirely from ~RMB 51bn (~US$7.6bn) of compute prepayments A.

Key insight

Chinese platforms are more stretched than American ones, not less. They spend a larger multiple of their cash flow, depreciate servers faster (~3-5 years (US Big-4: 5-6) A) and have no jumbo bond market to lean on, so they sell shares and borrow from banks. Money is cheap, though: China's 10-year yield is 1.67% (UST 5.195%, gap ~350bp) R.28

05 · The money mapWhere the yuan come from, and where they end up

Follow every yuan that flows through China's AI build in 2026: RMB 1,032bn (~US$154bn) A. The full map in the C11 synthesis has 76 flows in seven columns; the version below groups them into six columns and balances to the same total.11

Where the money comes from

Platform legacy cash flow (ads, games, e-commerce, fintech) supplies ~38% (West 49%). State and SOE money supplies ~15% direct + SOE (~18% incl. state-bank credit to LGFV/SOE), five times the West's ~3%. End-customer AI money is ~15%, bank loans and bonds ~14%, platform balance-sheet drawdown ~10%, and equity ~9% A.11

How it flows

The Sankey below traces each yuan from its source, through the spenders, into capex and power, and on to where it finally lands: supplier profit, payments abroad, or wages, materials and construction. Hover a band to trace it. State money is crimson; money paid abroad is purple.

What the capex buys

China's capex buys a different mix from the West's. Chips take ~25% (onshore plus offshore) against ~42% in the West, because domestic cards cost ~US$7–12k against ~US$30–60k for Nvidia's. And because the chips are weaker, China has to buy more racks, optics and power per FLOP: servers and CPUs take 14% of the budget vs 5% in the West A.11

Five things the map shows

  1. ~85% of the money is not end-customer spend (West ~82%). The build runs on platform profits, the state and borrowing A.11
  2. Under a tenth becomes operating profit (West ~48%). Most of each yuan pays for wages, materials and construction A.
  3. Nearly a fifth leaks abroad: ~21% (~RMB 185bn) goes to offshore hosts, foreign memory, the HBM stockpile, foreign CPUs and switch silicon. Export controls changed whom China pays, not whether it pays foreigners A.
  4. Platforms pay consumers ~RMB 60bn, six times what consumers spend on AI A.
  5. Circularity is smaller and different. Three patterns: equity-for-compute (Alibaba ↔ Moonshot, "nearly half" in cloud credits), platform-owns-supplier (Tencent ↔ Enflame, 83.79% of Enflame's 1H26 revenue R) and state-on-both-sides (National AI Fund ↔ DeepSeek ↔ Ascend). The circular share of lab equity is ~12-16% strict; ~30% broad (West ~45%/60%), and visible commitments are ~RMB 0.2-0.3tn (~US$30-45bn); ~1-2% of the West's US$2.46T A.11 The risk is political correlation, not financial contagion.

06 · Follow one yuanFollow one yuan down the stack

The clearest way to see where value lands is to follow a single yuan. Start with ¥1 of AI capex and ask who ends up with it A.11

  • ¥1 of capex ends as ~10 fen (+~8 fen of foreign margin abroad; West ~51¢) of supplier profit (memory ~3.3 fen, chip designers ~1.9, Nvidia onshore ~0.9), ~21 fen paid abroad and ~70 fen of wages, materials and construction A.11
  • ¥1 paid to a lab's coding plan on rented domestic compute: the lab keeps ~¥0.25, the cloud ~¥0.05, chip designers ~2 fen. Power takes ~10 fen, ~3× the West's share, because domestic systems draw ~2.4× the power per FLOP A.
  • ¥1 of end-customer money supports ~¥5.7 of capex, of which ~¥0.11 becomes Chinese chip-designer profit. In the West, $1 supports ~$5 of capex and ~$1.75 of Nvidia revenue A.

07 · Who keeps the profitMemory mints the money; the platforms' AI apps burn it

China's AI build-out has a thin, roughly zero-sum profit pool. There is no 75%-gross-margin monopolist at the top.

China-domiciled companies earn ~US$17bn (West ~US$637bn) of positive AI operating profit in 2026E, and ~US$0bn (net of lab and consumer-AI losses) once the losses are netted A.11 The largest slice is memory, ~33% (largest slice); domestic chip designers get only ~9% (West W05: 44%) A.

The money minters and the money burners

Minters (2026E): CXMT and domestic memory (AI slice ~US$4.5bn), Tencent and ByteDance ad models (~US$2.6bn), DC equipment and builders (~US$1.5bn), networking and optics (~US$1.3bn), Huawei Ascend (~US$1.2bn conf: very low), Hygon (~US$1.2bn) and Cambricon (~US$0.9bn). Burners: Alibaba's AI labs and applications (~−US$7.5bn), Tencent's new AI products (~−US$6.3bn), ByteDance's Doubao (undisclosed), independent labs (~−US$3.0bn), GPU start-ups (~−US$1.0bn), idle local centres and coal generators A.11 The exception: DeepSeek, with an 82.9% API gross margin on owned compute and a ~US$1bn run-rate E.9

Why the profit sits where it sits

Five reasons A:11

  1. No profitable domestic chokepoint. HBM, 7/5nm wafers and EUV are national projects run at a loss, so the scarcity rent goes abroad.
  2. Few, big, state-backed buyers who can squeeze suppliers.
  3. Open weights commoditize models, so labs cannot charge a premium.
  4. Platforms monetize indirectly (ads, commerce) and run consumer AI as a loss leader.
  5. The state takes strategic, not financial, returns.

Key insight

Counted causally, CXMT (~RMB 140-165bn (~US$21-25bn) of 2026 net profit) is China's biggest AI profit-taker, and the least durable: memory margins mean-revert within 4–8 quarters of a peak A.611 Most of CXMT's windfall (~US$17-20bn) is paid not by China's AI build but by phone, PC and car makers worldwide squeezed by the AI-driven memory shortage.

08 · The stateThe state in one page: seven jobs at once

Western governments shape the build mainly as regulators of sellers. China's state is planner, buyer, lead investor, lender, price-setter, gatekeeper and ultimate risk-holder at the same time A.11

How big is the state's share? ~19% (~RMB 170bn) of capex is state-directed (~21% including SOE platforms and state-bank-financed AIDC); ~15% direct + SOE (~18% incl. state-bank credit to LGFV/SOE) of funding is state money; and ~89% of capex is "state-shaped", because every yuan of accelerator spending passes a state filter A.11 The West's sovereign and enterprise own-build share is ~3%.

What the state gets

A domestic chip industry and 3× the compute

  • A domestic AI-chip market share that went from ~5% to ~80% in four years E.
  • National compute up ~3× in 18 months (725 → 2,185 EFLOPS) R/E.11
  • Labs and chip designers aligned with national priorities, and a home market walled off for Huawei and Cambricon.

What the state risks

Idle capacity on long bonds

  • Idle capacity on unwanted chips: AI centres used 36.8% of card-hours in 2025 E.
  • 20-year bonds against 4–6-year assets; retail losses when chip and lab IPOs de-rate; squeezed generators.
  • The state is the ultimate holder of the bag, so China's systemic risk sits in local-government finance, not in the platforms or banks directly.11

If controls tighten

10 · The big questionUsage is enormous; revenue is small

China processes more AI tokens than anyone, yet end-customer money is an eighth of the West's. Whether that gap closes decides who wins.

China processed >140tn/day (~97bn/min) tokens in March 2026 R,31 up from ~0.1tn a day in early 2024. ByteDance's Doubao alone reached 180tn/day (~125bn/min; ~5.7× Google's 22bn/min) R.132 Definitions differ: Doubao counts internal and free traffic, so the series are not like-for-like.

The money is far smaller. End-customer AI money is ~RMB 157bn (~US$23bn) in 2026E, about an eighth of the West's ~US$200bn A.11 Platforms pay users to try AI, and Alibaba and Tencent lose ~RMB 97bn/yr (~US$14.5bn) on consumer AI A.10 Only ~25-35% of AI end-revenue is fresh money rather than a shift from existing budgets.

Capex is ~5.7× (West ~5×) end-customer AI money A. A normal return on what has been built needs ~RMB 475bn/yr (~US$71bn) of end-customer revenue, against an exit-2026 run-rate of ~RMB 210bn (~US$31bn): a gap of ~2.3× (West ~2.1×; ~1.8× ex-state capex) A.1173

Why it matters

If the revenue does not arrive, who loses? Almost nobody has lent against China's AI build, so it is a platform cash-flow and local-government problem, not a lender problem A.11 Losses would land on platform shareholders, retail investors in chip and lab IPOs, and local governments holding idle computing centres.

20 · Glossary40 essential China terms

AI capex (China track)
AI-related data-centre spending by platforms, carriers, local/SOE centres and labs, including ~RMB 110bn of general-purpose kit.
Big-4 platforms
Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance, Baidu. The "listed three" excludes private ByteDance.
East Data, West Compute (东数西算, Dōng shù xī suàn)
The 2022 plan of 8 hub nodes and 10 clusters that moves compute to cheap western power.
Intelligent computing centre (智算中心, zhìsuàn zhōngxīn)
An AI-accelerator data centre, often funded by a local government or carrier.
AIDC
"AI data centre": carriers' and landlords' term for AI-grade halls.
Rack occupancy (上架率, shàngjià lǜ)
Installed ÷ built racks (71.4%, June 2026). Not the same as accelerator utilization.
Card-hour utilization
Accelerator hours used ÷ hours available (36.8% in 2025).
EFLOPS (FP16)
10¹⁸ operations per second at 16-bit precision; MIIT's unit for national compute.
Ten-thousand-card cluster (万卡集群, wàn kǎ jíqún)
A cluster of 10,000 or more accelerators.
Supernode / SuperPoD (超节点, chāo jiédiǎn)
Hundreds to thousands of accelerators linked as one machine (CloudMatrix 384; Atlas 950/960E).
UnifiedBus (灵衢, Língqú)
Huawei's interconnect for supernodes and clusters.
Ascend (昇腾, Shēngténg)
Huawei's AI accelerators (910B, 910C, 950, 960).
In-house ASIC
A platform's own chip: T-Head Zhenwu (真武), Kunlunxin (昆仑芯), SeedChip.
H100e / B300-equivalent
Compute measured in units of one Nvidia H100 or B300.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory stacked next to the accelerator; banned for export to China since Dec 2024; the tightest input.
N+2 / N+3
SMIC's 7nm-class and "5nm-class" DUV-only processes.
Xinchuang (信创, xìnchuàng)
Mandated replacement of foreign IT in government and SOEs; a captive market for Hygon and Kunpeng.
Domestic substitution (国产替代, guóchǎn tìdài)
Replacing foreign components with Chinese ones.
NDRC (国家发改委)
The planner that approves projects, H200 purchases and US investment in AI firms.
MIIT (工信部)
The ministry that sets compute targets and reports national EFLOPS.
CAC filing (网信办备案)
The security assessment and registration every public gen-AI service needs (988 by 30 Jun 2026).
National Data Administration (国家数据局)
Reports national token volumes.
Token (词元, cíyuán)
A chunk of text; the unit of AI output, priced in RMB per million.
MaaS (model-as-a-service)
Hosted model access sold by the token.
Compute leasing (算力租赁, suànlì zūlìn)
Renting GPU servers by the month or year.
Compute voucher (算力券, suànlì quàn) / token voucher (词元券, cíyuán quàn)
City subsidies paying 30–50% of small firms' compute or token bills.
LGFV / city-investment company (城投, chéngtóu)
Local-government financing vehicle; owns many computing centres.
Special bond (专项债, zhuānxiàng zhài)
A local project bond; compute equipment counts as project capital since Dec 2024.
Government guidance fund (政府引导基金, zhèngfǔ yǐndǎo jījīn)
A state fund-of-funds co-investing with private GPs.
Big Fund (大基金, dà jījīn)
The national chip fund; Phase III has RMB 344bn.
National AI Industry Investment Fund (国家人工智能产业投资基金)
RMB 60.06bn; led Moonshot's round; DeepSeek's only voting outside investor.
New policy financial instruments (新型政策性金融工具)
Policy-bank project capital (RMB 500bn in 2025, 800bn in 2026) levered with bank loans.
Patient capital (耐心资本, nàixīn zīběn)
The slogan for long-duration state equity in hard tech.
STAR Market (科创板) / HKEX Chapter 18C
The listing routes of the 2025–26 chip and lab IPO wave.
Energy-consumption quota (能耗指标)
The provincial allowance that gates new large loads in the east.
Compute–power coordination (算电协同, suàn-diàn xiétóng)
Siting AI load where renewables would otherwise be curtailed; paired with green-power direct supply (绿电直连).
Hundred-model war (百模大战, bǎi mó dàzhàn)
The 2023 rush of Chinese model launches after ChatGPT.
Red-envelope war (红包大战)
Platforms paying users to adopt AI apps (~RMB 9bn, Spring Festival 2026).
Super-entry (超级入口, chāojí rùkǒu)
The default AI gateway to commerce that Doubao, Qwen and WeChat are fighting to become.
Paid abroad
The share of China's AI capex that ends with foreign suppliers or offshore hosts (~21%, 2026E).
