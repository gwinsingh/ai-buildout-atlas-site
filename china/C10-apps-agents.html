The 60-second version

  1. China has the users but not yet the paying customers. 602m people used generative AI by Dec 2025 R46, and AI-native apps reached 499m monthly users in May 2026, +85.4% y/y E2. Almost all of it is free. The first mass-market paid tier, Doubao Pro at RMB 68/200/500 a month (~US$10/30/75), launched only on 24 Jun 2026 R14.
  2. Money flows the "wrong" way: platforms pay consumers. The 2026 Spring Festival giveaway cost ~RMB 9bn (~US$1.34bn) E11. Alibaba's AI apps lost RMB 13.9bn in one quarter R17 and Tencent's new AI products cost RMB 10.5bn R24: ~RMB 97bn a year between them, more than China's entire direct gen-AI revenue A.
  3. The real AI money is advertising, and much of it is re-divided, not new. Tencent's marketing revenue rose 22% on a bigger ad-ranking model while Baidu's fell 19% and Alibaba's merchant revenue fell 7% R242718.
  4. China's distinctive bet is agentic commerce. Qwen runs 400+ tasks across Taobao, Alipay, Fliggy and Amap, and 250m people have made a first AI-driven purchase through it R2117. No take-rate has been disclosed yet.
  5. Fresh money arrives mainly from AI video, coding plans and abroad. Kling earned >RMB 850m in Q2 2026 R29; Zhipu's run-rate hit ~US$1.6bn R41, Moonshot's >US$1bn E40. MiniMax earned 60.8% of H1 revenue outside China E34.
  6. Verdict on "agents bring fresh money": largely not yet. Direct end-customer spend is ~RMB 82bn (~US$12bn) in 2026E, about a tenth of the West's ~US$130bn A, and only ~25–35% of it is new money. A disclosed take-rate on agent-steered orders at Qwen, WeChat or Doubao would flip that verdict.

01 · Explain like I'm newA free food court run by the mall owners

This layer is where Chinese households, merchants, companies and government agencies meet AI, and where money from outside the AI industry is supposed to enter it.

Picture the everyday cases. A student asks Doubao to explain a physics problem. A shopper tells Qwen "find me soft trail-running shoes with Gore-Tex" and pays through Alipay without opening Taobao. An advertiser on WeChat has its ads placed by a larger recommendation model. A county government buys an "AI civil-service assistant" through a public tender. A creator pays Jimeng RMB 69 a month for AI video credits. A developer on a RMB 49 Zhipu coding plan leaves an agent running all night.

Analogy

In the West the AI storefront looks like a paid software shop: ChatGPT Plus, Copilot seats, Cursor licences. China's looks like a free food court run by the owners of a shopping mall. The mall (Alibaba, Tencent, ByteDance) gives away the food (AI answers) and even pays you to come in (red envelopes). It earns its money from rents (advertising) and a cut of what you buy in the shops (commission). Whichever food court becomes the default way into the mall, the super-entry (超级入口), wins the next decade of commerce. That is why the fight is for daily users, not subscriptions.

Why would a company give away something that costs money to run?

Because the assistant is not the product; the shopping trip is. Taobao and Douyin already earn commission and ad revenue per user that dwarfs any plausible AI subscription. If the assistant becomes the first thing a person opens when they want to buy, book or ask, the platform that owns it keeps those transactions. Google Search worked the same way; the difference is that in China one company can own the assistant, the shop, the wallet, the map and the delivery rider.

Serving a chat user is also cheap in China: our estimate is a few yuan a year per Doubao user (§7). Buying that user cost RMB 69–144 during Spring Festival 2026 E11. The expensive part is acquisition, not inference.

The question this page answers is who, if anyone, pays that back, and whether they do it with new money or old.

02 · How it worksFrom chatbot to "AI that does things" inside the super-app

The path from chatbot to copilot to agent is the same as in the West, with one twist: China's most important agents act inside their owner's own ecosystem of services, not on the open web or in enterprise software.

Alibaba's marketing shift from "AI that chats" to "AI that gets things done" (AI办事) captures it R2122. An agent that can pick a product from Taobao's 4bn items, pay through Alipay, book a Fliggy flight and send an Amap route never has to ask a third party for access. One company owns the super-app, the merchants, the payments, the maps and the logistics.

Why the super-app structure changes the economics

  1. Distribution is owned, not bought. Doubao is promoted inside Douyin (~700m+ domestic daily users, an older figure not re-verified). Yuanbao sits inside WeChat. Qwen is embedded across Taobao and Alipay. Western app start-ups must buy distribution; in China three platforms control it and can block rivals. WeChat even restricted links to Tencent's own Yuanbao for "inducing sharing" on 4 Feb 2026 R26.
  2. Monetization is indirect. The operator earns from ads and commission on the transactions the assistant steers, so it can rationally give the assistant away at a loss: the Google Search logic, compressed into one company that also owns the shop and the wallet.
  3. Payment control is the choke point. Every Chinese agent that buys something must pass through WeChat Pay or Alipay. The gen-2 Doubao phone explicitly hands the payment step back to the user R53, after gen 1's automated use of payment and banking apps got accounts logged out in Dec 2025 E53.
  4. Tokens are very cheap. China's MaaS price wars (C08) put Volcano's Doubao-Seed-2.1-Pro at RMB 6 input / RMB 30 output per million tokens (~US$0.90 / $4.48) E16 and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash at US$0.15 / $0.60 off-peak E40. Serving free users is cheaper than in the West, though not free at 140m daily users (§7).

Agents set off a token explosion

The Western pattern, agents using 4–15× the tokens of a chat (W10), shows up in China's official statistics. The National Data Administration counts daily token (词元) calls rising from ~0.1 trillion in early 2024 to over 1 trillion at the start of 2025, 100 trillion at end-2025 and over 140 trillion in March 2026, "more than 1,000× in two years", with ~21,100 trillion for full-year 2025 R4748. State media tie the 2026 surge to agents: one developer consumed 6bn tokens in a year, and one company's revenue in 20 days of early 2026 beat its whole 2025 revenue 47. Zhipu reports token calls up more than 40× year-to-date and Coding Plan calls up more than 23× (H1 2026) R42. MiniMax says it serves "1M+ enterprises and developers" in 100+ countries R33 (press reports of >2m, ~10× end-2025, are conf: low 34).

Key insight

China is ~9–10% of the West on direct spending (~US$12bn vs ~US$130bn) with more than half as many AI app users as ChatGPT has weekly users. Revenue per AI user is roughly US$20 a year in China [A: US$12bn ÷ ~600m users] against ~US$130 in the West [A: ~US$130bn ÷ ~1bn ChatGPT weekly users, a rough proxy]. And consumer-AI losses exceed revenue: Alibaba's ~RMB 13.9bn and Tencent's ~RMB 10.5bn a quarter annualize to ~RMB 97bn, above the ~RMB 82bn total. For now the Chinese application layer is a net consumer of capital, funded by ads, e-commerce and games.

How ¥1 flows down the stack

Five illustrative paths, each computed from reported numbers A. The first two show how much of the platforms' core revenue is being ploughed into AI; the last three show that most of a yuan of AI revenue goes straight to compute.

The key structural point

In the West, $1 of AI-native app revenue sends ~$0.75 to a separate model lab (W10). In China most application revenue is earned inside vertically integrated platforms that own the model, the cloud and the app, so the flow stays in-house. The main external payment is for chips (C05, C06), increasingly domestic ones.

06 · Business modelsNine ways Chinese companies make money here, or plan to

The dominant model gives the AI away and earns elsewhere. The paid models that work are the ones with a clear unit of value: a video, a line of code, a device.

1 · Dominant

Free, cross-subsidized by the core franchise

The assistant is free and paid for by ads (Tencent, ByteDance, Baidu), commerce (Alibaba, Douyin) or payments (Ant): Google's search bundle, inside one company.

Evidence: Tencent core profit ex-AI +19% while new AI cost RMB 10.5bn a quarter 24; Alibaba commerce EBITA RMB 39.7bn (−1%) vs AI Labs −RMB 13.9bn 1819

2 · Burning for the entry point

Paid user acquisition

The 2014 taxi-app and 2015 WeChat red-envelope playbook, rerun for AI. Rational only if agent use becomes a habit that commerce can later monetize.

Evidence: RMB 9bn in one holiday; one-week retention 19% (Yuanbao), 59% (Qwen), 71% (Doubao): money buys trials, not habits 11

3 · Freemium

Productivity subscriptions

Doubao Pro (RMB 68–500), Kimi (RMB 49–699), and Baidu's aborted ~RMB 200 Wenxin professional tier (end-2024). Aimed at compute-heavy tasks, not chat.

Evidence: insiders put conversion at 3–5% for small-ticket scenarios in 100m+ MAU apps; users call even US$20 "expensive" 15

4 · Works

Credits for creative output

Kling, Jimeng, Hailuo and PixVerse sell credits and memberships to creators, e-commerce sellers, ad agencies and AI short-drama studios. A usable video has a clear market price.

Evidence: Kling >RMB 850m in Q2, +200% 29: China's most successful paid consumer and prosumer category

5 · B2B

Project-based enterprise and government sales

Tenders (median RMB 860k) and private deployments dominate. Thin margins and long payment cycles, but real budgets.

Evidence: Zhipu shows the shift away: private deployment fell from 73.7% to 13.5% of revenue as API grew 41

6 · Land-grab

Cheap or free coding tools to win token share

Trae is free; coding plans cost RMB 7.9–49.9 at the hyperscalers. The goal is developer lock-in to a model and a cloud (the MaaS land-grab, C08).

Evidence: Zhipu's +101% API price rise 42 suggests the land-grab is ending for the leaders

7 · Device

Hardware as the monetized unit

Phones (Doubao/nubia), glasses (Xiaomi, Rokid, Quark, Huawei) and toys (Fuzozo on Doubao) 59. The consumer pays for the device; the AI is bundled.

Evidence: the state subsidizes phones and glasses at 15%, up to RMB 500 56

8 · Export

Go overseas for willingness to pay

Western consumers and developers pay US$10–200 a month where Chinese consumers pay RMB 0–68.

Evidence: MiniMax 60.8% international; Kimi overseas > domestic; Kling in 224 countries; Genspark and Manus HQ abroad 34663139

9 · State

State-subsidized demand

Token, model and compute vouchers pay 30–50% of SMEs' AI bills; public procurement buys agents for government services. The state is a co-payer of the layer.

Evidence: Chengdu up to 30% (50% for micro firms) 61; Shanghai RMB 600m a year 60

Why willingness to pay is low (Atlas synthesis)

  1. Two decades of free, ad-funded internet have set expectations.
  2. Free top-tier substitutes. DeepSeek's app and open weights set the price anchor at zero; Qwen and Yuanbao stay free 14.
  3. Bundling. Every platform builds AI into apps people already use.
  4. Lower disposable income. US$20 (~RMB 134) is roughly 3% of median urban monthly disposable income, against well under 1% in the US A conf: low.
  5. Platforms prefer the transaction to the subscription, because Taobao's and Douyin's commission and ad revenue per user dwarf any plausible AI subscription.

A Chinese chat user costs a few yuan a year to serve and RMB 69–144 to acquire. The margin that exists is in coding and video, and it is thin.

08 · The state's roleBuyer, co-payer, target-setter and gatekeeper

The state is the largest identifiable enterprise buyer of AI applications in China, and it pays part of everyone else's bill too.

Target-setter: the "AI+" action

The State Council's "AI+" opinion (人工智能+, adopted 21 Aug, published 26 Aug 2025) sets penetration targets for "intelligent terminals and agents" of more than 70% by 2027 and more than 90% by 2030 across six domains R45. No official penetration statistic has been published yet. Targets like these turn agents into a policy deliverable for ministries, provinces and state-owned enterprises, which then buy them.

Buyer: public tenders

Disclosed LLM-related public tenders reached 7,539 projects and ~RMB 29.5bn (~US$4.1bn at 7.18) in 2025, +396% by count and +356% by value; 5,779 of them disclosed a value. Forty-four contracts exceeded RMB 100m, and half of those were government. The median tender was RMB 860k, and about 53% of disclosed value went to compute and infrastructure E50. In H1 2026, 2,770 application tenders were awarded, about a quarter of them (673) for agents, and ~30 were "phase two" follow-ons E51. iFlytek ranked first by count and value in both periods.

Co-payer: token, model and compute vouchers

Cities hand out vouchers that pay part of firms' AI bills. Chengdu's draft token vouchers (词元券), published for comment on 11 Sep 2026, cover up to 30% of token spend (50% for micro firms), capped at RMB 2m per firm and RMB 100m a year in total R61. Shenzhen is moving from "model vouchers" to "token vouchers" 61. Shanghai runs RMB 600m a year of compute vouchers E60.

Consumer subsidy, grants, gatekeeping and exit control

  • Devices. Smart glasses joined the national trade-in subsidy (以旧换新) for 2026 at 15% of the price, capped at RMB 500, alongside phones, tablets and watches. The first tranche of special bonds for the whole program was RMB 62.5bn R56.
  • Grants. During the OpenClaw craze Shenzhen Longgang offered up to RMB 10m and Wuxi up to RMB 5m to agent start-ups E35. State funds (Yizhuang State Investment, Su Venture Capital) co-invested in PixVerse's US$300m round 65.
  • Gatekeeping. Every public-facing gen-AI service must file with the Cyberspace Administration (CAC), follow content rules and, from Sep 2025, label AI-generated content (older items, not re-verified). a16z notes that China requires AI providers to "register, host data onshore, and comply with censorship rules" 62. That favours large incumbents with compliance teams.
  • Exit control. The NDRC ordered the Meta–Manus deal unwound on 27 Apr 2026, without published reasoning 37. Relocating abroad does not free a China-built team from Chinese review.

Key insight

The state's demand-side money is modest in yuan, a few hundred million a year per city, but it shapes what gets bought: agents for government services, domestic models on domestic compute, and devices with AI built in. Its biggest effect may be the targets: a 70% agent-penetration goal for 2027 creates a buyer of last resort for enterprise agents that Western app companies do not have.

Atlas view

Tencent has the most defensible position: WeChat is the operating system of Chinese daily life, and Xiaowei needs no new app. Alibaba has the richest transactional back end. ByteDance has the users and arguably the best models but must go through others' payment and service rails. Expect the three to refuse each other's agents, as they refused each other's links and payments in the 2010s, unless regulators intervene.

Independents vs platforms

  • The consumer battle is lost for independents (Kimi 8.3m MAU 6). Their route is developers, coding and overseas, where they are winning (Zhipu, Moonshot and MiniMax run-rates 344042).
  • DeepSeek is a price disruptor. Its free, unmonetized app keeps the consumer price anchor at zero, and its open weights compress everyone's API margins.
  • Open weights let Western apps run on Chinese models (Cursor Composer on Kimi; W10), so Chinese labs earn API revenue abroad. Chinese models held ≥49.8% of OpenRouter's weekly tokens in the week of 14 Sep 2026 A (Atlas canonical, C09).

Incumbents disrupted

  • Baidu search is the clearest victim (online marketing −19%) 27.
  • No "SaaSpocalypse". Traditional SaaS never reached Western scale in China, and collaboration suites (DingTalk, Feishu, WeCom) bundle AI free. The disruption lands on IT services, outsourcing and business-process outsourcing (call centres, content moderation, e-commerce operations), which are harder to observe.
  • Live-action short-drama and ad-creative studios: AI cuts production cost 7–15× 74.

Disruptors to watch

  1. WeChat Xiaowei full launch, with agent-to-agent transactions inside mini-programs.
  2. Doubao ↔ Douyin Mall agentic purchasing at scale (the gen-2 phone already routes there 53).
  3. Phone makers' OS-level agents (Honor, Huawei, Xiaomi, OPPO, vivo; 755m monthly users of pre-installed assistants 3). They own the screen-reading permission layer that ByteDance had to rent from nubia.
  4. OpenClaw-style personal agents run by small firms on cheap domestic tokens, subsidized by token vouchers 61.
  5. Overseas-first Chinese agent start-ups (Manus, Genspark, PixVerse), which now face regulatory friction on exit 37.

Bull case

China monetizes agents through transactions, a bigger prize than subscriptions

  • Transactions beat subscriptions. Taobao/Tmall and Douyin handle trillions of RMB of merchandise value (older, not re-verified). If agents steer even a fraction and platforms keep their take-rates plus a premium for AI-originated orders, the revenue dwarfs Western subscription models. 250m first-time AI buyers on Qwen in nine months 17 is the fastest adoption of any commerce interface since mobile payments.
  • Inference is cheap and getting cheaper. A chat user costs a few yuan a year (§7), so the free model is sustainable once acquisition spending stops; Alibaba already cut Qwen marketing quarter-on-quarter 20.
  • Ads already pay. Tencent's +22% on AI ad models 24; ByteDance revenue ~+30% (press) 67.
  • The state is a guaranteed buyer. "AI+" targets (70% agent penetration by 2027) 45, tenders +356% by value 50 and token vouchers 61 set a policy-backed demand floor.
  • China exports AI at low prices. MiniMax 61% international, Kimi overseas > domestic, Kling in 224 countries: fresh money growing faster than any domestic line (Moonshot ~3× in two months 40).
  • Hardware plus subsidies can build an agent-native device base (Doubao phone gen 2 >RMB 100m in one second 53; glasses in the national subsidy 56).

Bear case

No one pays, and the money that moves is circular or subsidized

  • No one pays. After three years China has no disclosed large consumer AI subscription business; Doubao Pro met overwhelming user opposition 13; the price anchor is zero.
  • The money is circular or subsidized. Platforms pay consumers (RMB 9bn), the state pays enterprises, and Tencent's ad gains are partly Baidu's losses. Net fresh money is only ~25–35% of AI end-revenue A.
  • Cross-subsidies strain the core. Alibaba's GAAP net income fell 75% and free cash flow was −RMB 44.7bn in the June quarter 1719; Tencent's was negative in Q2 25. If e-commerce and games slow, the subsidy shrinks.
  • Walled gardens cap agent value. Cross-platform agents get blocked 53; each agent can only transact inside its own ecosystem.
  • Agentic commerce may cannibalize, not add. Qwen orders may be Taobao orders by another route, with extra inference cost and contra-revenue incentives (CMR −7% 18).
  • Compute and export controls raise serving costs and cap frontier quality (C05); the Seedance queues show it. Political and exit risk (Manus 37) deters foreign capital.

Net

We lean cautiously bullish on usage and on AI in advertising, bearish on near-term direct monetization. The pivotal variable through 2027 is agent-originated transaction revenue: a disclosed take-rate or GMV share from Qwen, Xiaowei or Doubao would flip the verdict.

What is structurally different

  1. Integrated platforms vs a specialized stack. In China the model, cloud, app, payments and merchants sit in the same company. In the West money passes through separate firms (app → lab → cloud → Nvidia). China's application layer barely pays a separate "lab tax".
  2. Ads and commerce, not subscriptions. China's main monetization is AI-improved advertising and, prospectively, agent-steered commerce. Subscriptions are an afterthought.
  3. Subsidy-first growth. Platforms pay users (red envelopes) and the state pays enterprises and consumers (vouchers, device subsidies). Western consumer AI has no equivalent cash-back war.
  4. Walled-garden agents. Cross-app agents face platform blocking. Western agents work on a comparatively open web and on enterprise software with published APIs.
  5. Export of cheap intelligence. China's fresh money increasingly comes from abroad, from developers and creators buying Chinese APIs and video tools. The West imports Chinese open weights as a cost input.
  6. The state as customer and target-setter. "AI+" targets, tenders and vouchers make government a primary demand driver; in the US the government is at most a regulator and a marginal buyer.
  7. An enterprise software gap. China lacks a large per-seat SaaS base, so there is no AI "seat" business like Copilot or Agentforce. Enterprise AI is sold as projects or private deployments.

Cross-track accounting

China's ~US$12bn of direct spend adds ~9% to the West's ~US$130bn. A "global" end-customer figure should be ~US$140–145bn, taking care not to double-count Chinese labs' overseas API revenue that Western developers already pay (W10 counts only Western vendors). That overseas revenue is money leaving the Western app layer for Chinese labs.

17 · GlossaryTerms on this page

AI办事 ("AI that does things")
Alibaba's framing for agents that complete tasks (ordering, booking, government services) rather than just chat.
AI-native app (AI原生App)
QuestMobile's category for standalone AI apps such as Doubao, Qwen and DeepSeek, as opposed to AI features inside other apps.
"AI+" action (人工智能+)
State Council policy (Aug 2025) to integrate AI into six domains, with penetration targets for 2027, 2030 and 2035.
All-platform MAU (全端月活)
A company's own user count across app, web, PC and embedded surfaces. Always above QuestMobile's app-only count.
ARR / run-rate
Annualized revenue run-rate: the latest month (or quarter) of revenue multiplied to a full year.
CAC (国家网信办)
Cyberspace Administration of China: the internet regulator that runs algorithm and gen-AI service filings and content rules.
CMR
Customer management revenue: Alibaba's advertising and commission revenue from Taobao and Tmall merchants.
Coding Plan
A flat monthly package of coding-agent model calls sold by Chinese clouds and labs (Volcano, Bailian, Zhipu).
Contra revenue
Incentives paid to customers and booked as a reduction of revenue, as with Alibaba's "business development program".
DAU / MAU
Daily / monthly active users: people who opened the app at least once in the day or month.
DAU cost (单个日活成本)
Subsidy spending divided by incremental daily active users; RMB 69–144 in Spring Festival 2026.
Adjusted EBITA
Earnings before interest, taxes and amortization, adjusted for items such as share-based pay; Alibaba's segment profit measure.
Entry point / super-entry (入口 / 超级入口)
The default interface through which users reach services; the prize in the assistant war.
GUI agent
An agent that operates other apps by reading the screen and tapping, as the Doubao phone assistant and AutoGLM do.
MaaS (模型即服务)
Model-as-a-service: selling model access by the token through a cloud platform (Volcano Ark, Alibaba Bailian, Baidu Qianfan).
Mini-program (小程序)
Lightweight apps inside WeChat or Alipay; the substrate for WeChat's "agent-to-agent transaction loop".
NDA (国家数据局)
National Data Administration: publishes the national daily token-call statistics.
NDRC (国家发改委)
National Development and Reform Commission: the planning agency that ordered the Meta–Manus deal unwound.
OpenClaw / "raising a lobster" (养龙虾)
A viral open-source personal agent (its logo is a red lobster) and the Chinese craze around it in 2026.
Private deployment (私有化部署)
Installing a model on the customer's own servers. Dominant in Chinese B2B; low margin.
QuestMobile
China's leading mobile-internet panel tracker; the source of most app user figures.
Red-envelope war (红包大战)
Holiday cash-giveaway campaigns used to acquire users.
Token (词元)
The unit of text a model reads or writes, roughly a word fragment; the official Chinese term is 词元 and the NDA publishes "词元调用量" (token calls).
Token / model / compute voucher (词元券 / 模型券 / 算力券)
Local-government subsidies covering part of firms' AI usage costs.
Trade-in subsidy (以旧换新 / 国补)
National consumer subsidy program; in 2026 it covers phones, tablets, watches and smart glasses at 15%, up to RMB 500.
Xiaowei (小微)
The WeChat AI agent Tencent is testing in 2026.
