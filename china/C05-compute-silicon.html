The 60-second version

  1. China's AI-chip market flipped from Nvidia to domestic suppliers in about four years. Nvidia held ~95% in 2022 E. In 2025 it still shipped ~55% of the ~4.0m accelerator cards sold in China, but domestic brands reached 41% E 43. For 2026 the trackers put domestic suppliers at ~80% of value and Nvidia at ~8% E 4445.
  2. Huawei is the national standard, and it sells systems, not chips. It shipped ~0.8m Ascend in 2025 and should earn ~US$12bn (~RMB 80bn) from AI chips in 2026, about half the market E 845. It compensates for weaker chips by wiring thousands of them into one "SuperPoD" 15.
  3. The market is small in dollars and thin in profit. About RMB 165bn (~US$25bn) of chips in 2026E, one-fourteenth of the West's ~US$365bn A. Cambricon is the one real profit engine (RMB 2.31bn net in 1H26) R 17. The whole layer nets ~US$1.5–2.5bn a year, roughly what Nvidia earns in three days A.
  4. Memory sets the ceiling, not design talent. The US cut off HBM in Dec-2024. A pre-ban stockpile of ~13m stacks carried 2025, but China's own HBM output in 2026 (~2m stacks) feeds only ~250–300k Ascend-class packages E 8.
  5. Beijing is now the buyer's regulator. It barred foreign chips from state-funded data centres, told platforms to stop buying Nvidia's China parts, blocked the H200 at customs and then allowed ~10k each for ByteDance and Tencent E 535558. Nvidia's licensed China data-centre revenue is close to zero R 4849.
  6. Per chip China is 1.5–2 generations behind, and the gap is widening. Ascend 950 does 2 PF at FP4 against Rubin's 50 PF R 5. Measured in Nvidia B300-equivalents, China's 2026 domestic output is ~1–2% of US output (IFP), or ~5–10% counting every source E/A 57.

01 · Explain like I'm newWhat China's AI chips are, and why they look different

Every AI model runs on chips that multiply huge grids of numbers. In China, the chips that do that work come from four very different places, and the government decides which of them a buyer may use.

This layer covers the accelerators inside China's data centres. They come in four families:

  • Huawei's Ascend (昇腾) NPUs, sold mostly as whole systems: Atlas servers, CloudMatrix and Atlas SuperPoDs.
  • Merchant chips from listed Chinese companies: Cambricon (寒武纪, "MLU" or 思元 chips), Hygon (海光, "DCU" or 深算 chips) and the GPU start-ups Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Enflame and Iluvatar.
  • In-house ASICs built by the internet platforms for themselves: Alibaba's T-Head PPU ("Zhenwu", 真武), Baidu's Kunlunxin (昆仑芯), ByteDance's SeedChip and Tencent's Zixiao.
  • Foreign parts that still get in through licences, the gray market or overseas rental: Nvidia's H20, H200 and RTX Pro 6000D, and AMD's MI308.

Analogy: road trains instead of big trucks

Picture a country that imported every truck from one foreign maker. Then the exporter capped how powerful a truck could be, and the home government told fleets to stop buying even the capped model. The home truck makers can design a good engine (chip design), but the steel mill (SMIC) runs older equipment and the tyre supplier (HBM memory) is small. So they build road trains: many smaller trucks coupled together (SuperPoDs). A road train moves a lot of cargo but burns more fuel and needs more road. The fleets (ByteDance, Alibaba, Tencent) grumble about fuel economy, but they buy, because the state decides which roads they may use.

Why can't China just buy Nvidia chips, or make its own at TSMC?

Both doors were closed from the US side, and then Beijing closed a third one itself. Since 2022 the US has capped how powerful a chip sold to China may be. In late 2024 it banned exports of HBM memory and required foundries such as TSMC to check that they are not making AI chips for Chinese designers through front companies.

From 2025 Beijing went further than Washington asked. It told its platforms to stop buying the watered-down Nvidia parts that were still legal, barred foreign AI chips from state-funded data centres, and now approves each Nvidia H200 purchase one by one. The goal is to guarantee demand for Huawei, Cambricon and the others, even at a cost in performance.

Definition

Accelerator: any chip built mainly for AI math. In China this means Huawei's NPUs, Cambricon's and Hygon's accelerators, GPU-style chips from the start-ups (GPGPUs), the platforms' in-house ASICs and imported Nvidia or AMD parts. "Market size" on this page counts chips and cards, not whole servers; servers, switches and optics are in C07, and wafers, HBM and tools in C06.

Why it matters

Compute silicon is the scarce input for China's whole AI stack. Chinese labs (C09) trained frontier-class open-weight models largely on stockpiled or smuggled Nvidia hardware, and they increasingly run inference on domestic chips. The layer is also the cleanest test of US export controls. So far the controls have capped China's compute per chip and its total FLOPs. They have not stopped the rise of a domestic chip industry; they accelerated it, by handing that industry a captive home market that Beijing then walled off.

02 · How it worksExport-control physics, the memory chokepoint and brute-force scale

Five mechanisms explain almost everything about this layer: a US performance cap that shaped every Nvidia China chip, an HBM ban that caps volumes, a domestic foundry stuck on older tools, Huawei's answer of linking thousands of chips, and a software gap that model labs are closing from above.

The export-control "physics": why each Nvidia China chip looked the way it did

US rules (Oct-2022, Oct-2023, Dec-2024, Jan-2025) cap a chip's Total Processing Performance (TPP = TOPS × bit-width) and its performance density (TPP per mm² of die). Chips above TPP 4,800 need a licence, as do chips above 1,600 with high density (prior knowledge; conf: high).

  • H100/H200 (TPP ~15,800) were banned. A800/H800, with slower interconnect, were banned in Oct-2023.
  • H20 cut compute to ~15% of an H100 but kept 96 GB of HBM3 at 4.0 TB/s. Its TPP of ~2,370 passed the test, so it became a memory-rich inference part, exactly what reasoning models need. That is why Washington restricted it in April 2025 (prior knowledge; conf: med-high).
  • B30A, a proposed single-die Blackwell, would have had TPP ~30,000 and 4 TB/s: roughly half a B300 at half the price, more than 12× an H20 and ~2.2× an Ascend 910C E 57. It was never approved.
  • The Jan-2026 H200 regime reviews exports case by case with thresholds of TPP <21,000 and DRAM bandwidth <6,500 GB/s, a 25% payment to the US government and a China cap of 50% of US H200 sales E 53. Press reports also cite 75k per customer E 54.

Why HBM, not logic, is China's chokepoint

A modern accelerator is roughly half HBM by cost (W05). The US banned HBM exports to China in Dec-2024. Before enforcement, Samsung shipped ~11.4m stacks to China, ~7m of them in a single month, and all vendors together shipped ~13m. SemiAnalysis reckons that was enough for ~1.6m 910C packages E 8.

Domestic supply is small. CXMT (长鑫存储) plans to mass-produce HBM3 in 2026 E 15; SemiAnalysis estimates ~2m stacks in 2026, enough for only ~250–300k 910C-class chips E 8. Huawei's answer is self-designed HBM: HiBL 1.0, a low-cost variant on the 950PR, and HiZQ 2.0 (144 GB, 4 TB/s) on the 950DT R 5. Where those DRAM dies and the stacking come from is covered in C06 (conf: med).

Key insight

Scarcity reshaped the product line. Huawei split the Ascend 950 into a prefill-and-recommendation part (950PR) with cheaper memory and a decode-and-training part (950DT) with more bandwidth. This mirrors Nvidia's Rubin CPX + Rubin split, but here it is born of memory rationing, not product strategy 5.

Logic: SMIC's 7/5nm-class nodes and the TSMC "die bank"

Ascend 910B/910C and Cambricon's current parts are made on SMIC's 7nm-class N+2 process, using older DUV lithography with multi-patterning. Newer parts are believed to use N+3 ("5nm-class") (conf: med; C06). SemiAnalysis projected SMIC's advanced capacity at ~45k wafers a month by end-2025, ~60k in 2026 and ~80k in 2027; Nikkei puts current 7/5nm output at under 20k E 8. Only ~20k wafers a month would be needed for Ascend at full HBM supply, so HBM, not wafers, sets the ceiling.

Before the loophole closed, 2.9m+ Ascend dies were made at TSMC through proxies such as Sophgo. That die bank carried Huawei's 2024–25 output E 8. Bloomberg reported Huawei aiming for 600k 910C and 1.6m Ascend dies in 2026 E 9; because a 910C is a two-die package, 1.6m dies ≈ 0.8m packages A.

Scale-up by brute force: SuperPoDs and optics

Nvidia puts 72 Blackwell or Rubin packages in one copper-wired NVLink domain (NVL72). Huawei cannot match per-chip FLOPs, so it links far more chips with optical scale-up over its own protocol, UnifiedBus (UB 2.0; Huawei claims 2.1 µs latency and >200 m reach) R 5. The newest system, the Atlas 960E announced on 17 Sep 2026, uses near-packaged optics: 5,500 Hi-ONE optical engines at 7.2 Tb/s replace 48,000 pluggable 800G modules, saving more than 550 kW, fully liquid-cooled, with 99.8% fault-free time claimed R 12. At the same event the OIF approved a 12.8 Tb/s NPO module standards project, initiated by Huawei, with more than 40 participating companies E 3.

Why brute force works in China

China's cheap and abundant grid power (C03) makes "more chips, more watts" a viable strategy. A CloudMatrix 384 draws ~3.9× the power of a GB200 NVL72 for ~1.6× the compute E 1011. In the West, where power is the binding constraint (W03), that trade would be ruinous; in China it costs an extra ~RMB 1–1.8m of electricity a year per system (see Unit economics).

Software: CANN, MUSA, DTK and the CUDA gravity well

CUDA is Nvidia's programming platform: about 20 years of libraries (cuDNN, NCCL, TensorRT) and the default target of every AI framework. Every Chinese vendor needs an answer to it.

  • Huawei CANN (Compute Architecture for Neural Networks), with the Ascend C kernel language, the MindSpore framework and PyTorch support through torch_npu. Huawei pledged in 2025 to open-source CANN and open its compiler and virtual instruction set by 31-Dec-2025 R 5. At HC2026 it added SIMD + SIMT programming to Ascend C for the 950 generation, which makes GPU-style kernels easier to port, and published a PTO instruction set with 120+ virtual instructions R 4.
  • Moore Threads MUSA with the Musify CUDA-porting tool E 65; Hygon DTK, which is ROCm/HIP-like so CUDA code ports with HIPify-style tools (prior knowledge); Cambricon Neuware/BANG with vLLM-MLU E 63; MetaX MXMACA and Biren BIRENSUPA (prior knowledge).
  • Model-side pull. DeepSeek V3.1 (Aug-2025) adopted the UE8M0 FP8 scaling format "for next-generation domestic chips" E 64. V3.2-Exp (29-Sep-2025) shipped with day-0 support for Ascend/CANN, Cambricon and Hygon E 63. V4 (Apr-2026) launched with Huawei saying its 950-based SuperNodes "fully support" it E 12.

Key insight

Model labs, not chip vendors, are breaking the CUDA lock-in. Once the top open-weight models run on day one on Ascend, most enterprise demand can follow. The remaining gap is frontier-scale training (numerics, collective libraries, fault tolerance), where stockpiled, smuggled or offshore Nvidia hardware is still preferred (conf: med).

03 · Market sizeMore chips, cheaper chips: how big the market is and who ships it

China bought about 4.0m AI accelerator cards in 2025 and will spend roughly RMB 165bn (~US$25bn) on chips in 2026. Units nearly doubled in two years while dollars barely moved, because the market swapped expensive Nvidia parts for cheaper domestic ones.

Why the trackers disagree

There is no single "China accelerator market" number. The trackers differ in four ways:

  1. Units vs dollars. IDC counts cards, and a two-die Huawei 910C counts as one. Bernstein counts dollars, and one Nvidia H20 or H200 is worth 2–4 domestic cards, so Nvidia's dollar share runs above its unit share.
  2. Whether in-house ASICs count as "domestic". TrendForce reports a separate ASIC bucket (T-Head, Kunlunxin, ByteDance). IDC folds T-Head and Kunlunxin into "Chinese vendors".
  3. The gray market. No tracker sees smuggled Nvidia parts, which Epoch AI estimates at ~480k H100-equivalents in 2025 alone E 61. IDC's ~2.2m Nvidia units in 2025 probably include H20s bought in 1H25 and some gray-market Hopper, but not all of it.
  4. Where the chip sits. Chinese firms rent or deploy Nvidia capacity offshore (Malaysia, Singapore, the Middle East and now Hong Kong for H200). That compute serves Chinese models but is not a "China shipment" (C04).

Key insight: the "US$50bn question"

Jensen Huang has repeatedly sized China's AI-chip opportunity at ~US$50bn a year (prior knowledge; conf: med). The Atlas 2026E market is about half that. The gap is the lower selling price of domestic chips, plus the fact that Chinese buyers were priced and regulated out of Nvidia's high end. China is ~7% of global accelerator dollars but ~20%+ of global units A.

All three trackers agree on direction and size: domestic chips went from about one-third to about four-fifths of the market in two years. The remaining disagreement (79% vs ~90%) comes down to whether gray-market and offshore Nvidia are counted.

So what

Cambricon's sequential growth slowed to +8% in Q2-26 while inventory kept rising, to RMB 8.25bn. Huawei Ascend: the national champion sells systems, not chips

HiSilicon designs the Ascend NPUs. SMIC makes them (historically TSMC too, through proxies), with HBM from the stockpile, CXMT and Huawei's own HiBL/HiZQ programmes. Huawei sells them mainly as Atlas servers, CloudMatrix and Atlas SuperPoDs: directly to platforms, telcos and state compute centres, through partners (xFusion, Digital China and other "Kunpeng-Ascend" OEMs), and as Huawei Cloud rental (C08).

Huawei has moved to a one-chip-a-year cadence, like Nvidia. It is candid that "the Chinese mainland will lag behind in semiconductor manufacturing process nodes for a relatively long time", so it competes on architecture, interconnect and memory using "process nodes that are practically available" R 5. At HUAWEI CONNECT 2026 it pulled the Ascend 960DT forward three quarters to Q1-2027 and the 960PR one quarter to Q3-2027, with the 970 in 2028 and a 980 in 2029 R/E 23.

05 · Money in / money outWho pays China's chipmakers, and where the money goes next

About RMB 165bn (~US$25bn) flows into accelerators in 2026. ByteDance is the biggest single payer; Huawei is the biggest single payee. Of each yuan a domestic vendor collects, roughly 30 fen go to memory and 40 fen stay as gross profit.

06 · Business modelsSix ways Chinese chip companies make money

  1. Huawei: sell the system, rent the cloud, subsidize the ecosystem. Huawei rarely sells bare chips at scale. It sells Atlas servers and SuperPoDs (earning hardware margin on NPUs, optics, switches, liquid cooling and Kunpeng CPUs) and Huawei Cloud Ascend instances (C08). It gives away developer compute (the 100 NPU-hour programme) and funds the ecosystem (RMB 5bn over three years) 4. Consumer, carrier and digital-energy profits cross-subsidize Ascend's R&D (group R&D RMB 121bn in 1H26) 16. Pricing power is rising: +20% on the 950PR and +60% on the 950DT reported 213.
  2. Cambricon: a classic fabless merchant with platform concentration. It sells boards and modules at ~RMB 55k each [RMB 6.50bn ÷ 117.4k cards] and ~55% gross margin to two or three internet platforms and server OEMs, and carries heavy inventory as a supply hedge. Its margin is capped by buyers' bargaining power and by Huawei's price umbrella.
  3. Hygon: cross-subsidy from a protected CPU franchise. Its x86 CPUs sell into the government/SOE xinchuang replacement market, a policy-mandated, sticky demand base that funds a DCU sold into the same state and telco accounts. It is the layer's most "policy-annuity" model.
  4. Platform ASICs (T-Head, Kunlunxin, SeedChip): cost substitution first, external sales second. The value to Alibaba, Baidu and ByteDance is avoided spending on Huawei/Nvidia and assured supply, monetized through cloud rental and model inference (C08, C09), not chip margin. Kunlunxin and T-Head are now turning captive chips into merchant businesses to justify IPO valuations; Kunlunxin's cornerstone investors reportedly must buy 3–7× their subscription in chips 37.
  5. GPU start-ups: capital-market-funded growth, with rental and bundle revenue. Revenue includes cluster rental (Biren) 31 and Tencent "AVAP" bundles (Enflame) 34. Gross margins range from ~57% (Moore Threads) to 17% (Iluvatar, buying share). Profits are frequently fair-value gains on cross-holdings 2933. The IPO boom is their main funding source; each burns RMB 1–3bn of operating cash per half-year.
  6. Nvidia and AMD: the "tolled" import. Every licensed chip pays 15–25% to the US Treasury, needs NDRC approval in China and is steered offshore (Hong Kong, South-East Asia). Economically it now resembles a luxury import under two tariffs, one American and one Chinese.

11 · BottlenecksWhat limits how many chips China can make

  1. HBM supply (binding). The ~13m-stack pre-ban stockpile is being drawn down. CXMT's ~2m stacks in 2026 support only ~250–300k 910C-class packages E 8; CXMT HBM3 volume starts in 2026–27 15. Huawei's HiBL 1.0 and HiZQ 2.0 are the swing supply (C06). Signal: the 950DT price is reportedly up 60%, and by Huawei's own framing it cannot meet demand until ~2030 2.
  2. Advanced logic wafers and yields. SMIC N+2/N+3 capacity and DUV multi-patterning yields. SemiAnalysis sees enough wafers (45k → 60k → 80k a month) if HBM were available 8. Every vendor competes for the same SMIC advanced capacity, and Huawei gets priority (conf: med).
  3. The TSMC loophole has closed. The 2.9m-die bank carried 2024–25 8. Since the Nov-2024/Jan-2025 foundry due-diligence rules, new TSMC-made Chinese AI dies should be near zero. ByteDance's SeedChip is going to Samsung, a new jurisdictional question 40.
  4. Advanced packaging (2.5D, CoWoS-class) for dual-die 910C/950 and HBM integration (C06).
  5. Software maturity. CANN, MUSA and DTK lag on new model architectures, but porting costs are falling thanks to DeepSeek/Qwen day-0 support, vLLM/SGLang backends and SIMT in Ascend C 463.
  6. Power and space: not a bottleneck in China. 2–4× the power per FLOP is acceptable given China's generation surplus and subsidies (C03). Floor space and optics are being addressed by NPO in the 960E 1.

Who this layer depends on: C06 (SMIC, CXMT, JCET, Tongfu, SJ Semi, Naura, AMEC, SiCarrier); C07 (Huawei's in-house NPO; InnoLight and Eoptolink, which supply both sides; xFusion, Inspur, Sugon and H3C servers); C01/C02 (platform capex and IPO capital); C09 (labs' willingness to optimize for domestic chips: DeepSeek, Qwen, GLM).

12 · Competitive dynamicsA Huawei-centric market with too many challengers

Six structural shifts, 2025 to 2027

  1. From "Nvidia or Nvidia-lite" to a Huawei-centric market with a merchant long tail. Huawei ~50% by value; Cambricon, T-Head, Kunlunxin and Hygon at 4–9% each; five start-ups sharing the remaining ~8–9% E/A 45.
  2. The platforms become chip vendors. T-Head (external sales, possible IPO), Kunlunxin (IPO) and ByteDance (SeedChip) shrink Cambricon's addressable market. Cambricon's customers are its future competitors.
  3. Systems beat chips. Huawei's advantage is UnifiedBus + NPO + SuperPoD + CANN, full-stack like Nvidia's. Start-ups selling PCIe cards at 8–64-card scale will struggle in training; their niche is inference, government and enterprise.
  4. Prefill/decode split and inference-first chips (950PR vs 950DT; Kunlunxin M100; Iluvatar's Zhikai inference line +652%) follow the global disaggregation trend (W05).
  5. Consolidation risk after the IPO boom. Seven listed or listing accelerator companies plus Huawei, T-Head and Kunlunxin is too many for a ~US$25bn market. Expect mergers or failures by 2028 (conf: med).
  6. Oversupply risk. Bernstein projects China will produce more domestic AI chips than it needs by 2028, with domestic sales growing at a ~74% compound annual rate E 45.

13 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

Captive demand, a credible roadmap and real profits

  • Policy-guaranteed demand. State-funded DCs must buy domestic, Beijing blocks Nvidia, and platforms have ~US$25bn a year of chip budgets that can only go domestic. Trackers put domestic share at ~80% of 2026 value 4445.
  • Huawei's roadmap is credible and accelerating: the 950 is shipping, the 960 was pulled forward three quarters, NPO SuperPoDs are real, and a 1m-NPU fabric is planned 12. The 2025 output figures (~805–812k) were independently confirmed 843.
  • Profitability arrived faster than expected at Cambricon (38.5% net margin) and Hygon, proving domestic AI silicon can be a real business.
  • Software lock-in is breaking: CANN is open-source, DeepSeek and Qwen run day-0 on domestic chips, and SIMT support lowers porting costs.
  • Power abundance makes China's watts-for-FLOPs trade cheap (C03), and bandwidth-heavy inference suits wide SuperPoDs.
  • HBM indigenization (CXMT HBM3 plus Huawei HiBL/HiZQ) could remove the binding constraint by 2027–28.

Bear case

Memory-capped volumes, thin profits and an IPO loop

  • Compute, not chips, is what matters, and China's compute is ~5–10% of the West's additions A, with the per-chip FP4 gap widening (Ascend 960 at 4 PF vs Rubin at 50 PF). Frontier training still relies on stockpiled, smuggled or offshore Nvidia.
  • HBM caps volumes. Once the Samsung stockpile is exhausted, output could fall unless CXMT and Huawei HBM ramp on time 8. The 750k 950PR plan may be HBM-limited.
  • Valuations are extreme: Cambricon at ~60× annualized sales; start-ups whose profits are cross-holding gains; Kunlunxin IPO investors required to buy 3–7× their subscription in chips 37. A reflexive IPO loop could unwind (C02).
  • Customer concentration and inventory: Cambricon's top five = 89%, inventory = 45% of assets, and Q2 growth slowed to +8% q/q. A pause by one platform hits hard.
  • Platforms insourcing (T-Head, Kunlunxin, SeedChip) squeezes the merchants.
  • Policy reversal: a US–China deal letting Nvidia back in (B30A/H200 at scale) would compress domestic share and pricing. Oversupply by 2028 (Bernstein) and too many vendors mean price wars 45.

What is structurally different

  1. The customer cannot choose freely. In China, Beijing decides whether Nvidia is purchasable. In the West, customers choose and Washington limits only exports.
  2. Scarcity is of memory and wafers, not money. Chinese buyers have budgets they cannot spend (Tencent's 2H25 capex fell on chip availability 42). Western buyers have supply they can buy, at a price.

17 · GlossaryTerms on this page

Accelerator
Any chip built mainly for AI math: GPUs, NPUs, GPGPUs and ASICs.
Ascend (昇腾)
Huawei's AI NPU family: 910B, 910C, 950PR/DT, 960, 970, 980.
ASIC
Application-specific integrated circuit: a chip built for one job or one customer, such as Alibaba's PPU or Baidu's Kunlunxin.
AVAP
A sales model Enflame uses with Tencent that bundles chips with services (per its prospectus; conf: med).
B30A / B300-equivalent
B30A: a proposed single-die Blackwell for China, never approved. B300-equivalent: a way to count compute in units of Nvidia's B300, used by IFP.
BF16 / FP8 / FP4 (MXFP4, HiF8, HiF4)
Number formats with 16, 8 or 4 bits. Fewer bits = more operations per second but less precision. MXFP4 is the open standard 4-bit format; HiF8/HiF4 are Huawei's own formats.
CANN
Compute Architecture for Neural Networks: Huawei's answer to CUDA, now open-sourced, with the Ascend C kernel language.
CAC (国家互联网信息办公室)
Cyberspace Administration of China. Summoned Nvidia over H20 security and told platforms to stop buying its China parts.
CloudMatrix / Atlas SuperPoD (超节点)
Huawei's rack-scale systems that make hundreds to tens of thousands of NPUs act as one computer over UnifiedBus.
CUDA
Nvidia's programming platform and libraries (since 2006), the core of its software moat.
DCU (深算)
Hygon's "deep computing unit", a GPGPU descended from AMD technology, programmed with the ROCm-like DTK.
Die bank
A stockpile of chip dies made before a control took effect: 2.9m+ Ascend dies from TSMC via proxies.
DUV / EUV
Deep- and extreme-ultraviolet lithography. China lacks EUV, so SMIC makes 7/5nm-class chips with DUV and multiple exposures per layer, which lowers yields.
East Data, West Compute (东数西算)
The national programme that steers data centres to power-rich western hubs.
EF / ZF (EFLOPS, ZFLOPS)
ExaFLOPS (1018 operations a second) and zettaFLOPS (1021).
Fair-value gain
An accounting profit from marking up the value of a shareholding. MetaX and Iluvatar reported profits in 1H26 mainly from such gains.
GPU "four dragons" (GPU四小龙)
Market nickname for the listed GPU start-ups Moore Threads (摩尔线程), MetaX (沐曦), Biren (壁仞) and Enflame (燧原); Iluvatar (天数智芯) is often grouped with them.
GAC (海关总署)
General Administration of Customs; ran the 2025 port crackdown and ruled the H200 "not permitted" in Jan-2026.
GPGPU
General-purpose GPU: a GPU used for computation rather than graphics, the design most Chinese start-ups follow.
H20 / H200 / RTX Pro 6000D
Nvidia's China-specific or licensed parts. H20 = cut-down Hopper (weak compute, strong memory); H200 = full Hopper with 141 GB; RTX Pro 6000D = a workstation-class card with GDDR memory.
H100e
H100-equivalents: compute expressed in units of one Nvidia H100, used to add up different chips.
HBM
High-bandwidth memory: DRAM chips stacked vertically next to the processor to feed it data fast. Banned for export to China since Dec-2024.
HiBL 1.0 / HiZQ 2.0
Huawei's in-house HBM-class memory: a low-cost variant (HiBL, on the 950PR) and a high-bandwidth variant (HiZQ, 144 GB at 4 TB/s, on the 950DT).
N+2 / N+3
SMIC's 7nm-class and 5nm-class processes, made with DUV lithography.
NDRC (国家发展和改革委员会)
National Development and Reform Commission, China's planning agency; now approves each Nvidia H200 purchase.
NPO
Near-packaged optics: optical engines placed next to the chip package, between pluggable modules and co-packaged optics. Used in Huawei's Atlas 960E.
NPU
Neural processing unit: a chip designed specifically for neural-network math. Huawei's term for Ascend.
OCF
Operating cash flow: cash actually generated by the business, before investment. Deeply negative at most start-ups.
PPU (真武)
Alibaba T-Head's "parallel processing unit", branded Zhenwu.
PR / DT; prefill / decode
Huawei's suffixes for Prefill-and-Recommendation and Decode-and-Training variants. Prefill processes the prompt (compute-heavy); decode generates tokens one at a time (memory-heavy).
SAMR (市场监管总局)
State Administration for Market Regulation; issued a preliminary antitrust finding against Nvidia in Sep-2025.
STAR Market (科创板)
Shanghai's Science and Technology Innovation Board, where Cambricon, Hygon, Moore Threads, MetaX and Enflame list.
TPP
Total Processing Performance (TOPS × bit-width), the US export-control yardstick. Above 4,800 needs a licence.
UnifiedBus (UB, 灵衢)
Huawei's scale-up and scale-out interconnect protocol (UB 2.0), the glue of its SuperPoDs.
UE8M0 FP8
DeepSeek's FP8 scaling format (8-bit exponent, no mantissa in the scale factors), chosen to suit domestic chips.
Xinchuang (信创)
The state IT-localization programme that mandates domestic CPUs, operating systems and software in government and SOEs.
Intelligent computing centre (智算中心)
A government-backed AI data centre; since Nov-2025 state-funded ones must use domestic chips.
