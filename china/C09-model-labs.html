01 · Explain like I'm newWhat a Chinese model lab does, and what makes it different

A model lab does three jobs: it trains a large model, teaches it to be useful, and sells access to it. Chinese labs do the same jobs as their US twins, in a very different environment.

First the lab pre-trains a model on trillions of words and code. Then it post-trains it with reinforcement learning (RL), tool use and safety tuning; in China that tuning also includes censorship alignment. Finally it sells access: per-token through an API (MaaS), as a monthly coding plan, through a chat app, or as an on-premise install for a state-owned company. The Western version of this layer is W09. Four things are different in China:

  1. Compute is rationed. US export controls have cut off Nvidia's best chips since Oct 2022. Labs use older Nvidia stock (A100, H800, H20), a trickle of H200s, Huawei Ascend and other domestic chips, and GPUs rented offshore. By our reading of Epoch's model data, China's flagship training runs used roughly 20–25% of the compute of the largest US runs (~1.5e25 vs ~6.6e25 FLOP) A 67 conf: low.
  2. Open weights are the default. Most Chinese labs publish their models' parameters (open weights) under permissive licences such as MIT or Apache 2.0, so anyone can download, run and fine-tune them. In the US, open weights are the exception.
  3. The state is inside the room. Every public-facing model must pass a security assessment and be filed with the CAC before launch R 77. The state also invests directly, buys through state-owned enterprises (SOEs) and decides who may buy Nvidia chips.
  4. Money is scarce and cheap at the same time. Private AI investment in 2025 was US$12.4bn in China against US$285.9bn in the US, a 23× gap E 68. Labs run lean: DeepSeek had ~160 staff in 2025 1 and Moonshot ~300 in 2026 36. OpenAI has thousands.

Analogy: street-food chefs on rationed gas

US frontier labs are Michelin-starred restaurants with unlimited budgets, charging top prices for the best dish in town. Chinese labs are brilliant street-food chefs on a rationed gas supply. They publish their recipes (open weights) so anyone can cook the dish at home, and sell nearly the same meal for a fraction of the price. Now they are so busy that they have started raising prices. Some have been accused of sneaking into the Michelin kitchens to copy techniques (distillation). And the city government owns part of several stalls and decides who gets gas.

Why would anyone give away a model that cost tens of millions of dollars to build?

Because the model is not the only thing being sold. A Chinese lab cannot easily hire a sales force in the US, but free weights spread through Hugging Face and cloud marketplaces on their own. The lab that built the model also knows best how to serve it cheaply, so its own API stays the cheapest place to run it. Once developers build on a model family, tools, chips and talent line up behind it.

The catch is that most of the money made from running an open model goes to whoever hosts it, not to the lab. Section 04 shows how large that leak is.

Why it matters

Chinese labs are the cleanest test of two big questions in the build-out. Can export controls stop a rival from reaching the frontier? (So far: they slow scale, not capability.) 02 · How it worksEfficiency under constraint: the Chinese technical playbook

Chinese labs could not scale compute the way US labs did, so they competed on how much capability they could get per unit of compute.

Five recurring techniques

  • Sparse mixture-of-experts (MoE). Only a small slice of the model runs for each token. DeepSeek V3 has 671B parameters in total but 37B active R 1; V4-Pro 1.6T / 49B, about 3% active R 2. Kimi K2 is 1T / 32B 34, Qwen3.8 2.4T / ~95B 39, Step 3.5 Flash 196B / 11B 42 and Tencent's Hy3 295B / 21B 51. Very sparse models are cheap to serve per token but need a lot of memory to hold all the weights.
  • Attention tricks for long context. DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA, V3.2-Exp, Sep 2025) plus V4's "token-wise compression" made a 1M-token context the default on DeepSeek's services R 2. Moonshot's Kimi Linear claims 5–6× faster decoding at very long context E 36.
  • Low-precision training and serving: FP8, and Int4/FP8 on Cambricon chips for GLM-4.6 E 15.
  • Cheap reinforcement learning. DeepSeek's R1 paper (Nature, Sep 2025) put the RL stage at ~US$294k 87 conf: med. Xiaomi says MiMo-V2.6-Pro's RL run cost US$2.62m (30 RL steps, under six days, ~750k trajectories) E 54.
  • Distillation, legitimate and contested. Labs train smaller models on the outputs of bigger ones. Doing it through a rival's API breaks that rival's terms of service; that is the core of the US accusations (section 13).

Open weights as a strategy

Giving away a model that cost tens of millions of dollars looks irrational. It makes sense for five reasons, and it has three costs.

  1. Distribution without a sales force. Chinese labs cannot easily sell to US enterprises, but open weights spread through Hugging Face, OpenRouter, Bedrock and other hosts. China reached ~70% of new derivatives by Feb 2026 E 61.
  2. The first-party API is still the cheapest place to run it. The lab knows its own architecture best. DeepSeek reported a theoretical 545% cost-profit margin on its own serving stack (Feb 2025) R 5, and its API gross margin was 82.9% in Jan–Jul 2026 E 12.
  3. Ecosystem and standard-setting. Once Qwen is the default base for fine-tunes (942m downloads; 69% of derivatives 61), tooling, hardware ports (Ascend, Cambricon) and talent line up behind Chinese architectures. The US-China Economic and Security Review Commission (USCC) calls this the "digital loop" E 63.
  4. Upsell to closed tiers and on-prem: open the good model, keep the best one. Qwen3-Max and Qwen3.7/3.8-Max are proprietary 3941; Zhipu sells GLM "Prime".
  5. Policy alignment. Beijing's "AI+" plan (Aug 2025) and the draft 15th Five-Year Plan favour diffusion and open ecosystems 63. Open weights also blunt the effect of US export controls on end users.

The costs of openness

Third-party hosts capture most inference revenue on open models: DeepSeek had the largest share of OpenRouter tokens 55, but much of that is served by third parties at US$0.03–0.40/M input R 57. The weights help rivals, including Western labs: Thinking Machines' Inkling was built on DeepSeek-V3 84. And open weights draw US regulatory fire. By 2026 some labs had started to close their flagships: Alibaba stopped open-weighting its largest Qwen models 41, while ByteDance Seed and Baidu ERNIE 5.x were always closed. The pure-plays have stayed open so far. Kimi K3's weights were released on 27 Jul 2026 3459, although Artificial Analysis still lists K3 as proprietary 65 conf: low on that label.

What "filing" means: the regulatory stack

  • Interim Measures for Generative AI Services (seven agencies led by the CAC; issued 10 Jul 2023, in force 15 Aug 2023). Services with "public-opinion attributes or social-mobilization capability" need a security assessment and algorithm filing before launch. Content must uphold "core socialist values", and training data must come from "legitimate sources" R 77.
  • A two-tier registry. Models and services are filed (备案) with the national CAC. Apps and functions that call an already-filed model are registered (登记) with provincial CAC offices. By 30 Jun 2026: 988 filed and 598 registered, up from 748 and 435 at end-2025 (796 filed by end-Feb, +72 in Mar–Apr, +120 in May–Jun) R 73747576 (chart in section 09).
  • AI-content labelling measures (CAC, MIIT, MPS, NRTA; issued 7 Mar 2025, in force 1 Sep 2025). Visible labels and hidden metadata are required on all AI-generated text, audio, images and video; app stores must check compliance R 78.
  • What it costs the labs A: launch delays conf: low, political red-teaming, and a handicap in consumer chat abroad where censorship shows. DeepSeek reportedly used Claude to write "censorship-safe alternatives" 69.

11 · Profit captureNo independent lab makes money; profit lands upstream and sideways

  • No independent Chinese lab makes a profit. Zhipu (adjusted net loss 206% of revenue), MiniMax (307%) and DeepSeek (151%) all lose more than they earn 122889. In China, as in the West, profit in this layer is captured upstream by compute suppliers (Huawei, Cambricon, domestic clouds; C05, C08), sideways by big-tech parents through cloud and ads, and in 2026 by equity sellers who priced placements at the top of the HK bubble.
  • API gross margins show the potential. DeepSeek's 82.9% API gross margin (2026) and 545% theoretical cost-profit margin (2025) show a lean lab with owned compute can earn software-like margins on inference 512. The problem is scale and training: US$1bn of revenue at 80% gross margin is US$0.8bn of gross profit, less than one frontier training cycle at US scale, but plenty at Chinese efficiency (V3's final run cost US$5.6m).
  • China's independents earn ~2% of global lab revenue while serving far more of global usage. Our rough 2026 split: ~US$110bn in total (Anthropic ~60, OpenAI ~36, others ~10, China independents ~2), against ≥50% of OpenRouter tokens and ~70% of new fine-tunes A 5561. Open weights shift value to users and inference hosts. Firms building on Qwen, DeepSeek and Kimi (Thinking Machines' Inkling 84; Airbnb's reported Qwen use 87, conf: med) capture surplus the Chinese lab never bills.
  • Big-tech labs are the best-hedged profit capturers, mirroring W09. Alibaba (Qwen → Cloud), ByteDance (Seed → Volcano Engine's 49.5% MaaS share, Doubao, Douyin), Tencent (Hy → WeChat, Yuanbao, Cloud) and Xiaomi (MiMo → devices) earn their return indirectly. The pure-plays must earn it directly, at prices the big techs can subsidise down.
  • The state captures strategic rather than financial value: domestic-compute validation (GLM-5 and V4 on Ascend), global standard-setting through open weights and influence in the Global South. The USCC's "Two Loops" thesis argues that a "physical loop" of deployment data in factories and robotics compounds this 63.

Why it matters for the whole China stack

Lab losses are one reason the Atlas estimates China's 2026 AI profit pool at ~US$17bn gross but ~US$0bn net of lab and consumer-AI losses (C11). In the West the same netting still leaves ~US$589bn. The lab layer is where China's build-out spends money it has not yet earned.

14 · Bull vs bearThe strongest case on each side

Bull case

Parity at a fraction of the cost, and monetisation has started

  • Parity at a fraction of the cost. A 1.4% Arena gap and a ~7-month lag 6667, achieved with ~2–5% of US R&D budgets and 1/23 of the VC money 68. Efficiency compounds.
  • Distribution is won. ~70% of new fine-tunes, 1.15bn downloads and ≥50% of OpenRouter tokens 5561. When agents route tokens by price, Chinese models become the default "worker" tier.
  • Monetisation inflected in 2026. Zhipu revenue +400%, MiniMax +283%, Moonshot ARR ~5× in four months, DeepSeek API GM 82.9%, and price rises that stuck.
  • Domestic compute works. GLM-5 was trained entirely on Ascend 33; V4 is optimised for and runs on Ascend 68. Every Ascend 950 ramp lowers serving cost, and Beijing's H200 gate protects the domestic ecosystem 79.
  • The state is a patient anchor investor and customer, and HK/STAR markets now fund loss-making labs.

Bear case

A rounding error of revenue, and the gap widens where the money is

  • Revenue is a rounding error. Filed 1H26 revenue of US$259m is ~0.9% of OpenAI + Anthropic. Valuations (Zhipu ~160× booked revenue) assume a US-style revenue curve that has not appeared.
  • The gap widens where the money is. AA shows a 12-point gap on agentic and reasoning composites, and US labs' revenue comes from long-horizon agents 65. Distillation is being shut off 6971.
  • Compute-starved growth. Price rises are rationing, not pricing power. Offshore rentals may be closed, and H200s depend on Beijing.
  • The open-weight value leak. Third parties capture the inference revenue, and big-tech rivals give comparable models away. The tigers' only moat is speed.
  • The Western market can be switched off by procurement bans, Entity-List additions or app-store actions, hitting MiniMax (61% overseas) hardest 71. And the equity bubble has already burst once: Zhipu fell ~68% in a month, and a lock-up and placement wall continues 2021.

Our judgement A

Chinese labs are a world-class R&D engine attached to a weak revenue engine. The 2026 inflection (API and coding plans, price rises) is real, but it rests on a base about 1/40th the size of US labs'. We expect:

  1. the capability gap to stay in a 3–9 month band (the saw-tooth) as long as compute ratios hold;
  2. consolidation, with 3–4 independent labs surviving (DeepSeek, Zhipu, Moonshot, MiniMax; StepFun if its IPO lands) and the rest absorbed by big tech or pivoting;
  3. prices to keep rising until Ascend 950-class capacity arrives in volume (2027);
  4. Chinese independent-lab revenue of ~US$2.4bn in 2026 (range US$2.0–2.7bn; revised up by the fact-check) and ~US$5–8bn in 2027 conf: low, still under 5% of US labs'.

The largest swing factor is US policy on remote compute and on the use of Chinese models, not Chinese technical ability.

What is structurally different

  1. Open by default. China monetises weights indirectly and the West directly. China wins usage share; the West wins revenue share.
  2. Compute is rationed by two governments, so Chinese capacity, not demand, sets prices. Prices rose in 2026 while Western frontier prices fell at fixed capability.
  3. Big tech dominates spending. Alibaba, ByteDance and Tencent fund most Chinese model R&D as cost centres. In the West, independent labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) lead.
  4. Listed earlier, with filed accounts. Zhipu and MiniMax are the world's first listed pure-play LLM labs, which gives China better lab-level disclosure than the US.
  5. Revenue mix. Western labs earn from enterprise seats and agents. Chinese labs earn from cheap API tokens, on-prem SOE projects and overseas consumer apps.
  6. The state is on the cap table: the National AI Fund in DeepSeek with voting rights; Shanghai and Pudong funds; China Mobile.

18 · GlossaryTerms on this page

AA index
Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index: a composite of reasoning, coding and agentic benchmarks. Versions change, so compare scores only within one version.
ARR / run-rate
Annual recurring revenue, or a recent month's (or quarter's) revenue multiplied up to a year. Chinese labs' figures are mostly peak-month annualisations.
Ascend (昇腾)
Huawei's AI chip family (910B, 910C, 950), programmed with the CANN software stack. See C05.
Blended price
On this page: (3 × input price + output price) ÷ 4, per million tokens, with no cache discount.
CAC (国家互联网信息办公室)
Cyberspace Administration of China: files generative-AI services before launch and sets content and labelling rules.
备案 / 登记 (filing / registration)
The CAC's national filing of a generative-AI model or service, and the provincial registration of apps that call an already-filed model.
CANN
Huawei's Compute Architecture for Neural Networks: its answer to Nvidia's CUDA.
Chapter 18C / "-P" marker
The HKEX listing regime for specialist-tech issuers. "P" marks a pre-commercial company; MiniMax lost it on 18 Mar 2026.
Coding plan
A monthly subscription that bundles a token quota for coding agents (GLM Coding Plan, Kimi Code).
Convertible bond (CB)
Debt that can be swapped for shares at a set price. Zhipu's RMB 20.14bn zero-coupon CB converts at HK$892.5.
Cost per task
Artificial Analysis's measure of what it costs to run its benchmark tasks on a model. Not a per-token price.
Distillation
Training a model on another model's outputs. Contested when done through a rival's API in breach of its terms.
ECI
Epoch Capabilities Index: Epoch AI's single scale for model capability, used to estimate how many months China trails.
Entity List
A US Commerce Department list of foreign firms that need a licence to receive US technology. Zhipu was added in Jan 2025.
FLOP
Floating-point operations: the count of arithmetic steps used to train a model. 1e25 = 10 million billion billion.
LMArena
A leaderboard built from blind human votes between two models' answers, scored like chess ratings (Elo).
Lock-up
A period after an IPO during which early shareholders may not sell. Zhipu's and MiniMax's expired in Jul 2026.
MaaS
Model-as-a-service: per-token API sales, usually through a cloud (Volcano Ark, Alibaba Bailian, the Z.ai open platform).
MoE (mixture-of-experts)
A model design in which only a few "expert" sub-networks run for each token. "1.6T / 49B" means 1.6T total and 49B active parameters.
NDRC (国家发展和改革委员会)
National Development and Reform Commission: China's planning agency, the gatekeeper for H200 purchases in 2026.
On-prem / localised deployment (本地化部署)
A model installed in the customer's own data centre: the typical purchase by an SOE, bank or government body.
Open weights
Downloadable model parameters under a licence such as MIT or Apache 2.0. Not the same as open training data or code.
OpenRouter
A marketplace that routes developers' API calls to many models and hosts; its public rankings show token shares.
Peak / off-peak pricing
DeepSeek's time-of-day tariff from 16 Aug 2026; off-peak is 50% of peak.
Placement
A sale of new shares by a listed company to selected investors. Zhipu placed HK$31.4bn in Jul and HK$15.7bn in Sep 2026.
RL (reinforcement learning)
Post-training that rewards a model for good answers or successful actions, the main source of reasoning and agentic skill.
Six tigers (六小虎)
Zhipu, MiniMax, Moonshot, StepFun, Baichuan and 01.AI.
SOE
State-owned enterprise: a company controlled by central or local government.
USCC
US-China Economic and Security Review Commission: a congressional advisory body. Its "Two Loops" paper describes China's open-model strategy.
