The 60-second version

  1. China's best logic is "5nm-class" and made without EUV, in small volume. SMIC's N+3 process, found in Huawei's Kirin 9030, is "significantly less scaled" than TSMC or Samsung 5nm R 8. Estimates of China's 7/5nm-class capacity run from under 20k to ~45k wafers a month, heading to ~60k in 2026 E 911. TSMC's N2 alone is heading to ~90k.
  2. SMIC is booming on AI's side-demand, not on AI chips. Record 2Q26 revenue of US$3.006bn (~RMB 20.1bn), +36% YoY, came mostly from power-management, optical and board-level chips for AI servers R 782. It is now the world's #3 foundry at 5.4% share E 1, but earns a 25% gross margin against TSMC's 67.7%.
  3. The money is in memory. CXMT, a DRAM maker that had negative gross margins in 2023, earned RMB 33.0bn (~US$4.9bn) of net profit in 1Q26 alone R 14 and listed on the STAR Market in July, raising RMB 57.9bn 77. We expect it to book ~75–80% of the listed chain's 2026 profit A.
  4. HBM is the hard stop for China's AI chips. The US banned HBM exports in Dec 2024. Huawei is living off a stockpile of ~13m Samsung-era stacks; CXMT's own 2026 output of ~2m stacks feeds only ~250–300k Ascend 910C-class packages E 11. Logic wafers are not the constraint; memory is.
  5. The state writes the rules of demand, and both sides hold chokepoints. New fabs must informally spend ≥50% on domestic tools 44; localization is ~35% by count but ~20–25% by value, and under 1% in lithography E/A 26. Washington's Affiliates Rule and Beijing's rare-earth and gallium controls are both suspended until 9–10 Nov 2026 2833.

01 · Explain like I'm newWho makes China's AI chips, physically

A Huawei Ascend or Cambricon accelerator is designed in China (C05). Increasingly it must also be made in China, which puts the whole physical chain on Chinese soil.

Since 2020 Huawei cannot legally use TSMC, and since Oct 2022 no Chinese designer can get leading-edge AI chips made abroad without a US licence. So the chain looks like this. SMIC (中芯国际) prints the logic die on a 7nm-class process, using mostly Dutch and US machines bought before the bans plus a growing share of Chinese ones. CXMT (长鑫) supplies the stacked memory (HBM), or will; until now that came from a stockpile of Samsung stacks. A domestic OSAT or Huawei's own lines package the die. Chinese EDA software covers only parts of the design flow, so designers still rely on Synopsys and Cadence licences.

This layer also covers the toolmakers (Naura, AMEC, Piotech, SiCarrier, SMEE), materials (photoresist, gases, wafers), YMTC for NAND flash, and the controls imposed by the US, the Netherlands, Japan and Taiwan, plus China's counter-levers: rare earths, gallium, germanium, antimony and the Nexperia episode. Chip designs belong to C05; servers and optics to C07.

Analogy: skyscrapers without cranes

Picture a country told it can no longer buy the world's best cranes (EUV lithography) or elevators (HBM). It can still pour concrete, and it has more concrete trucks than anyone (mature-node fabs). So it builds a lot of 20-storey buildings very cheaply and exports some of them. It also learns to stack 20-storey buildings on top of each other (DUV multi-patterning) to reach skyscraper height, at double the cost and with a lot of cracked floors (low yields). Meanwhile it is building its own cranes in a secret workshop in Shenzhen (the EUV prototype).

Why can't China just buy the machines, or have TSMC make its chips?

Because the US and its allies closed both doors. No EUV scanner has ever been shipped to China. Since Oct 2022 and Oct 2023, US-origin tools for logic at 16nm and below need licences, and the Netherlands and Japan added their own rules. Since Dec 2024 HBM memory itself is banned. TSMC stopped making dies for Chinese AI designers after a TSMC-made die turned up inside a Huawei Ascend in late 2024.

So China uses what it already owns (a large installed base of older Dutch and US machines), builds what it can at home, and fills the gaps with stockpiles. That works well for mature chips and ordinary memory. It works badly for the most advanced logic and for HBM.

Definition

Localization rate (国产化率): the domestic share of equipment or materials. It can be counted by number of tools, by value, by tender or by installed base, so published figures differ a lot. On this page "headline" means the widely quoted third-party figure and "by value" means our own dollar-weighted estimate.

Why it matters

  • It is the binding constraint on China's AI compute. Demand (C01) and capital (C02) are abundant. What limits how many domestic accelerators ship is 7nm-class wafer capacity, yield and, above all, HBM.
  • It is where the state is most directly an investor. Big Fund I/II/III, provincial funds, municipal SOEs (SiCarrier, SwaySure, CXMT's Hefei backers) and SASAC-controlled companies (Naura, SMEE, Empyrean via CEC) own large stakes in almost every company here.
  • It is the front line of the tech war. Most US, Dutch, Japanese and Taiwanese export controls on China target this layer, and so do China's counter-controls on rare earths, gallium, germanium and Nexperia.

02 · How it worksMaking 7nm and 5nm without EUV, and why HBM is harder

China reaches advanced nodes by exposing the same layer several times with older machines. That works, at a price. HBM has no such trick.

Multi-patterning: stacking exposures to fake a finer brush

An ASML NXT:1980i/2000i immersion scanner (DUVi) prints lines down to a ~38–40nm half-pitch in one exposure. 7nm-class metal layers need a pitch of ~40nm, so fabs expose the same layer two to four times (LELE, SADP, SAQP). SiCarrier holds a late-2023 patent for SAQP "to reach 5nm thresholds" R 36.

The cost shows up in SMIC's margins. Its gross margin fell to 19.2% in 4Q25 when "low yields at 7nm-equivalent nodes" weighed on profitability E 6. The ceiling is real: TechInsights' teardown of the Kirin 9030 found that N+3 is "a scaled evolution of its 7nm-class technology" with "meaningful density improvements" over N+2, but it "remains significantly less scaled" than TSMC N5 or Samsung 5nm R 8. Most analysts see DUV multi-patterning topping out around the 5nm class.

HBM: China's hardest problem

HBM stacks 8–16 DRAM dies, joined by TSVs (through-silicon vias), on a base die. To make it, China needs four things at once:

  1. Leading-edge DRAM dies. CXMT is at a ~16nm-class node; SK hynix is at 1b/1c-nm.
  2. TSV etch, deposition and fill tools. Naura is developing HBM-optimized etch, deposition and clean E 37.
  3. Thermo-compression or hybrid bonders, from Maxwell and U-Precision E 37.
  4. A lot of wafer capacity. HBM dies are 3–4× larger than DDR5 dies E 37.

The Dec-2024 US rule banned HBM2E and above, plus related tools, for China (pre-2026 knowledge). Huawei's roadmap now depends on in-house HBM: the Ascend 950PR (1Q26) and 950DT (4Q26) carry "self-developed HBM" R 50, reported as HiBL 1.0 (128 GB, 1.6 TB/s) and HiZQ 2.0 (144 GB, 4 TB/s) (pre-2026 knowledge; conf: med). The stacks are presumably built from CXMT dies or those of Huawei-linked SwaySure (conf: low). The arithmetic of why this binds is in Unit economics.

03 · EquipmentThe world's biggest tool buyer, with a shrinking share

China bought more chipmaking equipment than any other region in 2025, but its share of the world market fell, because AI capex outside China grew much faster.

SEMI puts China's 2025 equipment billings at US$49.3bn (~RMB 354bn), −0.5% YoY, or 36.5% of a US$135.1bn world market, down from 42.3% in 2024. Taiwan nearly doubled to US$31.5bn R 23. SEMI expects China to stay #1 through 2028, with growth "moderating in 2026"; its July forecast puts the 2026 world market at US$165.9bn, up from US$145bn in its Dec-2025 forecast E 6125. Global 2Q26 billings were US$40.5bn (+23% YoY); SEMI's free release did not split out China 24.

What drove it. 2023–24 was a pull-forward: Chinese fabs bought tools before each new control (Oct 2023, Dec 2024) and stockpiled DUV immersion scanners. SMIC's co-CEO said the company "procured some key equipment in advance, while the supporting equipment may not be purchased yet" R 10. 2025 held flat on mature-node and memory builds (CXMT, YMTC Phase 3, Hua Hong Fab 9, Nexchip IV). The share is falling because the AI capex cycle outside China (TSMC capex US$60–64bn, the memory Big 3) is growing much faster (W06).

Cross-check against the toolmakers' own numbers

China was 28% of Applied Materials' FQ3-26 revenue (35% a year earlier) 71, 26% of Lam's June-2026 quarter 72, and 33% of ASML's 2025 revenue (36% in 4Q25), with ASML guiding ~20% for 2026 R/G 47. Direct US tool exports to China were only US$2bn in 2025 (−34%), while shipments routed via Singapore (US$5.7bn) and Malaysia (US$3.4bn) were larger E 51. Much "US" equipment reaches China from non-US plants.

How "localized" is China's tool base, really?

The headline localization rate went from 14% (2019) to 25% (2024) to ~35% (2025) E 26. The Information Network agrees: ~65% of tools were imported in 2025 52. But the same source that gives 35% "overall" gives 23% for new-equipment purchases, and the average hides a huge spread by segment.

Our value-based cross-check A (conf: med-low)

Add up the 2025 semiconductor-equipment revenue of the domestic vendors:

  • Naura: RMB 39.35bn total 53, of which "electronic process equipment" is ~95% 18. That line also includes PV, LED and battery tools, so we take ~RMB 30–34bn as semiconductor.
  • AMEC: RMB 12.38bn 22, including some LED MOCVD.
  • Everyone else (Piotech, ACM Shanghai, Hwatsing, Kingsemi, SMEE, SiCarrier, Skyverse, RSIC and others): ~RMB 25–35bn. Piotech's 2025 revenue was "~13× 2020" 51; SiCarrier's reported 2025 target was ~RMB 4.5bn 70 (conf: low).
  • Total ≈ RMB 70–80bn ≈ US$9.7–11.1bn at 7.18, against the US$49.3bn market: ~20–23% by value.

That matches the "23% of new-equipment purchases" figure 26 and The Information Network's estimate that Chinese vendors had 6.5% of global WFE in 2025 (1.2% in 2021) E 52. Bottom line: by count or tender China is ~35% localized; by dollars it is ~20–25%, because the expensive tools (lithography, metrology, advanced deposition) are still imported.

04 · FoundrySMIC at #3, Hua Hong at full tilt, and the advanced-node mystery

China's foundries are growing fast on mature and specialty chips. How much 7/5nm-class capacity exists is the most disputed number in this layer.

China's top three foundries together took ~US$4.72bn, ~8.8% of top-10 revenue in 2Q26 A (4.72 ÷ 53.49). One secondary report puts mainland China's top-12 foundries at >15% of global foundry revenue in 2025, probably on a broader basis including captive fabs (conf: low) 37.

On price, SMIC says it is "never the first to raise prices, nor raises them the most aggressively" 3.

How much 7/5nm-class capacity does China have? Estimates differ by 3×

SMIC does not disclose it. Nikkei put China's combined 7/5nm output at "fewer than 20k" wafers a month in early 2026, with Beijing wanting 100k within 1–2 years and 500k by 2030 E 910. SemiAnalysis estimates SMIC's N+2/N+3 at ~45k wpm at end-2025 → ~60k in 2026 → ~80k in 2027 11. UBS sees 50–60k wpm of additions in 2026 and 150–160k advanced-node wpm by end-2027 for China as a whole 10.

Our reconciliation (conf: low-med)

Installed 7nm-class capacity is likely ~40–50k wpm, but effective good-die output is much lower because of 20–40% yields. The Nikkei figure probably reflects actual advanced wafer starts, not installed tool sets. Either way, as Unit economics shows, logic capacity is not what limits Ascend output.

Hua Hong: the specialty foundry in the AI-power boom

Hua Hong (华虹) makes embedded memory, power and analog chips on 28–55nm. Its 2Q26 revenue was a record US$717.5m (~RMB 4.8bn), +26.8% YoY, gross margin 16.5% (+5.6pp) and net profit attributable US$38.6m (+385.9%). Utilization was 102.8% and wafer shipments 1,538k (+17.9%) R 39. China was 78.6% of revenue (US$563.7m, +20%), but North America grew +77% (US$93.8m) and Europe +90.1% (US$28.0m).

  • Capacity. Fab 9A (Wuxi) is full in 3Q26. Fab 9B tools are being installed, with small volume from 1Q27. Fab 9C: ~US$6bn over several years for 55k wpm. The group will spend ~US$1.5bn a year of capex for 2–3 years; the Huali (HLMC, Fab 5) acquisition has final approval and consolidates from 3Q26 R 38.
  • Pricing power. Orders run at 1.5–2× capacity, and 60% of the 3Q guide (US$770–780m, gross margin 16–18%) comes from price R/G 38.
  • Advanced node. Nikkei and TrendForce name Hua Hong as the second driver of China's push for 5× advanced-node capacity 9. We have no confirmed Hua Hong 7nm output (conf: low).

Mature nodes: where China is gaining share

57 of 97 new fabs built worldwide in 2023–25 were in China E 46. Mainland 12-inch capacity is ~1.2m wpm, heading to ~1.5m (2026) and ~1.8m (2027) 37 (conf: low). 28nm wafer prices went from US$3,200–3,500 (3Q23) to US$2,900–3,200 (1Q24) and back to US$3,000–3,200 (1H26) 37 (conf: low-med). The US answer is a Section 301 finding (Dec 2025) with an initial tariff of 0% until June 2027 while a deal is sought; the rate after that is not set R 45.

05 · MemoryCXMT and YMTC: from subsidized import substitutes to global exporters

The global memory shortage turned China's two memory champions into its most profitable chipmakers, and made them exporters at world prices.

CXMT: from loss-maker to China's most profitable chip company

CXMT is China's #1 and the world's #4 DRAM maker 14, with fabs in Hefei and Beijing 13. It makes DDR4/DDR5 and LPDDR4X/LPDDR5(X) at a ~16nm-class node and launched DDR5 in early 2025 1315. Its gross margin was negative in 2022 and 2023 R 15. Then the supercycle arrived: 1Q26 revenue of RMB 50.8bn (~US$7.6bn), +719% YoY, and net profit of RMB 33.0bn (~US$4.9bn). It guided 1H26 revenue to RMB 110–120bn (~US$16.4–17.9bn) and net profit to RMB 66–75bn R/G 14.

  • Losses erased in a quarter. CXMT had accumulated losses of RMB 40.9bn (~US$5.7bn) at June 2025 15; it will likely erase them in a single quarter of 2026 A. By our rough read, its guided 1H26 net profit is about what SMIC has earned in total since its founding in 2000 (conf: low).
  • Global-grade pricing. SCMP, citing SemiAnalysis, reports ASPs "within 5 to 10 per cent of" Samsung, SK hynix and Micron E 12. Tencent and ByteDance placed DDR5 server orders after validation 12.
  • Heavy assets. D&A was RMB 14.9bn in 2024 and fixed assets RMB 171.6bn (~US$24bn) at June 2025 15. Capacity is ~320k wpm now, heading to 420k in 2027 E 65.
  • The IPO. CSRC approval came on 5 Jun 2026 and the STAR Market listing on 27 Jul 2026 at RMB 8.66. It raised RMB 57.9bn (~US$8.6bn), the largest IPO in STAR's history, at an IPO valuation of RMB 579bn (~US$86bn); the prospectus had targeted RMB 29.5bn R 7714. Shares opened at RMB 49.50 (+~470%), a day-one market cap of ~RMB 3.28tn (~US$490bn) 13. Proceeds fund line upgrades (RMB 7.5bn), DRAM technology (RMB 13bn) and R&D (RMB 9bn), including a "G5" process and 12-high HBM3 1365.
  • HBM. HBM3 sampling began in 2H25 with Huawei. Mass production is targeted by end-2026 and HBM3E in 2027 E 3738. Counterpoint sees ~US$2bn of HBM revenue by 2028 65. Reported HBM3 yield is ~25% (conf: low) 38.
  • Risks. Its tools are mostly imported, and CXMT itself says further restrictions "may" disrupt its supply chain 15. It is on the US DoD 1260H list (date inconsistent across sources; conf: med) 13. Its top five customers took ~60% of 1H25 revenue 15. And it is fully exposed to the memory cycle.

CXMT's revenue share runs below its bit share because its mix leans toward consumer DDR4/LPDDR4X rather than HBM. Our implied 2Q26 revenue share of ~5.5–6.5% A compares CXMT's implied 2Q revenue of US$8.8–10.3bn (at 6.70) with TrendForce's US$154.7bn industry total, which excludes CXMT 59.

YMTC: the NAND challenger

YMTC (长江存储) had 14% of global NAND bit shipments in 2Q26, #3, ahead of Micron and Kioxia and behind Samsung (25%) and SK hynix (22%), but only #5 by revenue E 16. Its Xtacking architecture has iterated to 3.0/4.0. Phase 3 in Wuhan (registered capital RMB 20.72bn, ~US$2.9bn; Hubei-SOE co-investor) broke ground in Sep 2025 and mass production was pulled forward to 2H26 R 17. It aims for ~50% enterprise-SSD mix by end-2026, holds 11,000+ patents, and its IPO is at the tutoring-acceptance (辅导验收) stage 1617. Hurun valued it at RMB 160bn (~US$22bn) in 2025 E 16. On the Entity List since Dec 2022, it relies heavily on domestic tools; 2025 reports described a "first fully domestic production line" trial (conf: low).

For context, the top five listed NAND brands took US$68.87bn in 2Q26 (+77% QoQ) 60. We estimate the whole NAND market at ~US$79bn that quarter (Samsung's US$23.06bn at a 29.3% share) A, so YMTC's implied 2Q26 revenue is roughly US$8–10bn (conf: low). China is expected to make ~19% of the world's NAND by 2027 16.

09 · Business modelsFive ways Chinese companies make money in this layer

China's chain runs on business models that differ sharply from their Western twins. The common thread: the state supplies capital and demand, and the companies supply capacity.

(a) Foundries

Utilization plus state capital, not a technology premium
  • Near cost-plus pricing for a captive home market: SMIC's ASP was ~US$991 per 8-inch equivalent in 2Q26, ~US$2,230 per 12-inch wafer A (×2.25), against ~US$30k for a TSMC N2 wafer. They win on availability and "local-for-local", not on node.
  • 2026 pricing turned: price drove 60% of Hua Hong's guided growth; SMIC's ASP rose 5.7% QoQ.
  • JV capital structure: big fabs are joint ventures with local governments and the Big Fund; SMIC later buys partners out (SMIC North, ~US$5.7bn) 4674.
  • Thin profit, fat cash flow: D&A takes ~40% of revenue, yet EBITDA is ~70%. The model generates cash to reinvest, not earnings.
  • Advanced node as a national service: 7/5nm-class output at 20–40% yields is uneconomic; SMIC is effectively paid in policy support to run it (conf: med).

(b) Memory

"Hefei model" state venture capital that pays off in a supercycle
  • The Hefei government, the Big Fund and Qinghui Jidian funded CXMT through six years of losses (RMB 40.9bn accumulated by Jun 2025). The exit is the STAR IPO (valued at RMB 579bn; +~470% on debut) 1577.
  • Commodity pricing: DRAM at near-global prices (within 5–10%), so CXMT captures the supercycle almost fully: 1Q26 net margin ~65% A.
  • Strategic cross-subsidy: turning wafers into low-yield HBM3 for Huawei displaces DDR5 at supercycle prices. Doing it anyway looks like state direction or a long-term bet (conf: med).

(c) Equipment

Mandated demand, bought share, heavy R&D
  • The 50% rule and the Entity List give domestic toolmakers a protected home market, but each tool still has to win qualification. Naura's gross margin fell from 42.9% to 40.1% on "new product verification costs and commercial discounts".
  • Extreme R&D: AMEC ~30% of revenue vs ~12% at Applied Materials.
  • Working capital as financing: customer prepayments (RMB 4–6bn at Naura) and big inventories (RMB 28–31bn, ~1.5× half-year revenue) show multi-year tool programmes.
  • Other lines: Naura also sells PV, LED and battery tools; the weak 2025 PV market dragged its profit.

(d) Captive and loss-tolerant

Huawei's network and domestic EDA
  • SiCarrier, SwaySure, PXW are owned by Shenzhen municipal vehicles, with Huawei as anchor customer and technology source (2012 Lab patents transferred 36). Revenue targets such as SiCarrier's RMB 7.5bn are unaudited (conf: low). These are strategic cost centres, funded by Huawei's consumer and carrier profits and by city budgets.
  • EDA: Empyrean runs at a loss with R&D at ~73% of revenue, kept alive by CEC ownership, customer loyalty mandates and M&A. It makes money on analog, flat-panel and point tools.

(e) OSATs

Low-margin volume with one foreign anchor
  • JCET (net margin ~4%) and Tongfu (~11% in 1H26) earn thin margins 4167.
  • Tongfu's economics depend on AMD. Western chipmakers remain Chinese OSATs' best customers even as domestic AI GPU packaging grows.

15 · Competitive dynamicsA two-speed chain, a memory windfall and a national IDM

  1. The "two-speed" chain. China is world-class and gaining share in mature nodes (34% → 38%+ of capacity 45) and commodity memory (CXMT ~8–9% of DRAM bits, YMTC 14% of NAND bits 1665). It is structurally stuck ~2–3 generations behind at the AI-critical frontier: EUV logic, HBM3E/4, CoWoS-L. US controls were designed to create exactly this split, and they have.
  2. The memory shortage changed China's position. In 2023 CXMT and YMTC were subsidy-dependent import substitutes. In 2026 they are exporters earning global prices: IC exports doubled 57, Western OEMs are qualifying CXMT DRAM (W06), and Tencent and ByteDance buy CXMT DDR5 for servers 12.
  3. Domestic toolmakers take share inside China. Chinese vendors' global WFE share rose from 1.2% (2021) to 6.5% (2025), and their mean revenue growth was +30.5% against +9.3% for foreign vendors in 2025 E 52. The cost is margin compression (Naura gross margin −2.8pp in 2025 53).
  4. Western vendors' China share is normalizing, not collapsing. AMAT went from 35% to 28% 71, ASML from 33% to a guided ~20% 47, and China's share of global equipment from 42% to 36.5% 23. The fall is driven more by the AI boom elsewhere than by lost China orders; China's absolute spending (~US$49bn) has not fallen.
  5. Huawei is becoming a vertically integrated "national IDM". It designs chips and funds fabs (PXW), DRAM/HBM (SwaySure), tools (SiCarrier), EUV light sources and EDA, all through municipal-SOE vehicles: the closest thing China has to a combined TSMC + SK hynix + ASML, though far smaller and far less efficient.

16 · Bull vs bearCatching up fast enough to matter, or capped with a boom masking it

Bull case

China's chain catches up fast enough to matter

  • Scale is solved at mature nodes and in commodity memory: >38% of mature capacity 45; CXMT ~9% of DRAM bits heading to 11% in 2028; YMTC 14% of NAND bits. They are self-funding: CXMT's ~US$20bn+ of 2026 profit pays for HBM and new fabs without Big Fund money.
  • Advanced capacity is scaling 3–5×: 45k → 80k wpm (SemiAnalysis) or 20k → 100k (the Nikkei target). Logic is not the Ascend bottleneck.
  • The tool base localizes every year: 14% → 25% → 35%. Etch and clean are already majority-domestic; Naura and AMEC grow 25–35% a year.
  • HBM is coming: CXMT HBM3 mass production by end-2026, HBM3E in 2027, ~US$2bn of HBM revenue by 2028, and Huawei's own HBM in the 950 series.
  • Export controls leak: the US$252m AMAT penalty and the 2.9m-die TSMC bank show how much got through. The Busan truce paused escalation, and the MATCH Act is not law.

Bear case

China stays capped, and the boom masks it

  • The ceiling is real. N+3 is "significantly less scaled" than 5nm. Without EUV, China's best node in 2027–28 is still ~5nm-class at poor yields while TSMC ships N2/A16: the gap is widening in absolute terms.
  • HBM is the hard stop. A 25%-yield HBM3 in 2026 compares with HBM4 in the West. Once the stockpile is used up, Ascend output is capped at CXMT's ~2m stacks, each costing ~US$1.75k of forgone DDR5 revenue.
  • Servicing is a time bomb. A MATCH Act or a Dutch or Japanese servicing ban would degrade a ~US$150bn+ installed base of Western tools (our rough 2020–25 China equipment spend) with no domestic substitute in litho or metrology.
  • Profits are cyclical and concentrated. ~75–80% of the chain's profit is CXMT at peak memory prices; SMIC loses 42% of revenue to D&A. When memory turns (2027–28), the chain's profits could fall by more than half A.
  • Mature-node overcapacity brings tariffs. The 34% → 38% share invites the Section 301 step-up (June 2027) and EU measures; 2026's price firming may not last once Fab 9C, Nexchip IV and CanSemi add ~300k 12-inch wpm by 2027 37.

Our balance (conf: med)

China's chain will produce enough 7nm-class logic for its domestic AI needs by 2027. It will stay HBM-bound. It will become financially strong only through commodity memory. The single variable that would most change this picture is a servicing ban (bearish) or domestic EUV plus HBM3E at yield (bullish), and neither is likely before 2028.

What is structurally different

  1. No profit at the AI frontier. In the West, TSMC (67.7% gross margin) and the HBM makers (80%+) capture AI-specific rents. In China the AI-specific parts (7nm-class wafers, HBM) are loss-leading national projects, and profit comes from commodity memory sold globally.
  2. Capacity without the leading edge. China outspends everyone on tools (US$49bn in 2025) but mostly on mature nodes and memory. The West spends on EUV logic, CoWoS and HBM.
  3. The state sets demand, not only finance. The 50% tool rule, procurement preferences and guidance against Nvidia create a captive market. The West relies on subsidies plus private demand.
  4. A vertical "national IDM" instead of a specialized ecosystem. Huawei's affiliates (fab, DRAM, tools, EUV) substitute for what the West gets from separate, profitable specialists (TSMC, SK hynix, ASML).
  5. Both sides hold chokepoints. The West holds EUV, HBM, EDA and metrology. China holds gallium, germanium and rare earths, mature-node volume and, as Nexperia showed, packaging and assembly for Europe's car industry.

20 · GlossaryTerms on this page

N+1 / N+2 / N+3
SMIC's internal names for its 14nm-derived 10nm-class, 7nm-class and "5nm-class" DUV processes.
DUVi (ArF immersion)
193nm lithography with water between lens and wafer (ASML NXT:1980i/2000i). China's most advanced installed lithography.
SAQP
Self-aligned quadruple patterning: a spacer-based technique that quarters the pitch of a single DUV exposure, at a cost in steps and yield.
EUV / LPP / LDP
Extreme-ultraviolet (13.5nm) lithography. The light comes from a laser-produced plasma (ASML's LPP) or a laser-induced discharge plasma (LDP, Huawei's reported approach).
HBM / TSV / hybrid bonding
High-bandwidth memory: DRAM dies stacked and joined by through-silicon vias (TSVs). Hybrid bonding joins dies copper-to-copper without solder bumps.
8-inch equivalent
SMIC's capacity and shipment unit. One 12-inch wafer ≈ 2.25 8-inch wafers.
wpm / wspm
Wafers (starts) per month.
Localization rate (国产化率)
Domestic share of equipment or materials. It can be measured by count, value, tender or installed base, so figures vary.
Entity List
BIS list requiring licences (usually denied) for US-origin items.
Affiliates Rule
BIS 2025 rule extending Entity List restrictions to ≥50%-owned subsidiaries. Suspended to 9 Nov 2026.
NS-CMIC / 1260H lists
US Treasury and Defense Department lists of "Chinese military companies".
VEU (Validated End-User)
US status that let Samsung, SK hynix and TSMC fabs in China receive tools without individual licences. Revoked effective end-2025.
MATCH Act
Proposed US bill (Apr 2026) to ban DUV-immersion exports to China and restrict servicing of installed tools. Not law.
Big Fund I/II/III (大基金)
China's National IC Industry Investment Fund. Phase III is RMB 344bn (May 2024).
Hefei model (合肥模式)
A city government acts as a venture investor in strategic manufacturing and exits via IPO.
Xtacking
YMTC's architecture that bonds a separately made CMOS logic wafer to the memory-array wafer.
